The Dutch Reformed Church (in Afrikaans, the Nederduitse Gereformeerde Kerk or NG Kerk) came to the Cape with the [[history/institutions/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]] in 1652 [IC]. It was the church of the company and of the colony's Dutch-speaking farmers [IC]. In this valley, its ministers opposed Georg Schmidt's baptisms in 1742, and its congregation in Greyton, [[ng-gemeente-greyton|NG Gemeente Greyton]], was founded in 1918 [IC]. This page tells its history in the valley.

## The church of the colony

Most of the company's people at the Cape belonged to the Dutch Reformed Church, and its first ordained minister arrived in 1665 [IC]. For most of the company's rule it was the colony's official church [TBV]. Its first synod met in 1824 [IC].

## The church and the first mission

When [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]] began to baptise Khoekhoe people at Baviaanskloof in 1742, the Cape's Dutch Reformed ministers objected that he had no right to baptise [IC]. On 4 September 1742 the company's Council of Policy banned him from baptising any more [TBV]. He left the Cape in 1744 [IC]. The Moravians could not return until 1792 [IC].

## Separate churches

In 1857 the church's synod decided to allow separate services for coloured members, citing "the weakness of some" [IC]. In 1881 this led to a separate Dutch Reformed Mission Church for coloured members [IC]. The church supported apartheid, and in 1982 the World Alliance of Reformed Churches suspended it for this reason [IC]. In 1986 the Mission Church adopted the Belhar Confession, on unity, reconciliation, and justice, and in the same year the Dutch Reformed Church's synod opened its doors to people of all races [IC]. In 1994 the Mission Church and the Dutch Reformed Church in Africa united as the Uniting Reformed Church in Southern Africa [IC].

## In Greyton

Before 1918, Dutch Reformed members in Greyton received communion once a month from visiting ministers [MS]. The NG Gemeente Greyton was founded on 7 May 1918, in the Caledon circuit of the church's Western Cape synod [MS]. Its first church building in Greyton later became the Moravian church [IC]. Its present church, on the corner of High and West Streets, was built in 1964 [MS].
