For most of its history the valley had no single local government. Greyton had its own municipality from 1910, while Genadendal and the other mission villages were run first by the Moravian missionaries and then by boards set up under laws for mission land [IC]. The two were joined only in December 2000, in Theewaterskloof Municipality, of which Ward 2 is a part [IC]. This page tells that history. It describes municipal positions only as the municipality's own official documents state them.

## Two ways of being governed

**Greyton** was founded in the 1850s as a freehold village, with owners holding title deeds [IC]. It became a municipality in 1910 [IC]. After coloured families were moved to Heuwelkroon under the Group Areas Act, Heuwelkroon had its own management committee under Greyton's white municipal council [IC].

**Genadendal** and the mission villages were run by the [[history/institutions/moravian-church|Moravian Church]]. From 1909 the Mission Stations and Communal Reserves Act placed mission land under management boards, and residents could live on the land but not own it [IC]. In 1924 a secular board of management replaced the missionaries [IC]. From 1987 the Rural Areas Act 9 of 1987, a law of the coloured House of Representatives, governed the former mission areas, and Genadendal was added to it in 1989 [IC].

## After 1994

In the mid-1990s, transitional local councils replaced the old structures [IC]. Greyton's white council and the Heuwelkroon Management Committee were dissolved, and Greyton and Genadendal each had a transitional local council [IC].

At the local elections of December 2000 these councils were dissolved, and Theewaterskloof Local Municipality was created [IC]. It brought together the councils of Grabouw, Villiersdorp, Botrivier, Riviersonderend, Caledon, Greyton, Genadendal, and Myddleton, with the farming areas around them [IC]. Its seat is Caledon [IC]. Ward 2 covers Genadendal, Greyton, Heuwelkroon, Madiba Park, Bereaville, Voorstekraal, and Boschmanskloof [MS].

## The municipality and Farm 39

When TRANCRAA came into force in 1998, the municipality was given the first duty to propose how the trust land of Farm 39 would be transferred [IC]. It ran the process from 2002, through consultants it appointed [IC]. The full story, with each party's position in its own words, is on [[trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]]. The municipality does not provide full basic services at [[villages/madiba-park|Madiba Park]], which stands on Farm 39 land [MS]. See [[madiba-park-land-and-services|Madiba Park: Land Status and Basic Services]].

## Financial recovery

On 11 November 2024 the Western Cape Government announced an intervention in Theewaterskloof Municipality under section 139 of the Constitution [IC]. The province said the municipality had shown "prolonged financial instability, including mounting debt, severe cash flow issues, and challenges in revenue collection" [IC]. Revenue collection had fallen from over 95% in 2022/23 to 64% in August 2024 [IC]. The Western Cape Government approved a Financial Recovery Plan on 7 October 2025, with three phases: rescue, stabilisation, and sustainability [IC]. The plan reports that Genadendal accounts for about a fifth of the municipality's outstanding debt, and that many residents there hold that the land does not belong to the municipality [IC].

## The municipality and the Forum

Theewaterskloof Municipality's Economic Development office co-convened the first meeting of the [[civic/governance/ward-2-development-forum|Ward 2 Development Forum]] on 16 September 2026, and gives the Forum administrative support [MS]. The municipality does not moderate this site.
