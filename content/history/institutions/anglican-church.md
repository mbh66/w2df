The Anglican Church came to the valley with the English-speaking settlers who bought plots in Greyton after 1854 [TBV]. Its congregation today worships at [[st-andrews-anglican-church-greyton|St Andrew's Anglican Church]] in Greyton, which is listed in the Register. This page gathers what is known of its history in the valley, which is still little.

## In Greyton

Greyton was founded by [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]], an Englishman, and its early plot owners included people of British descent, among them the family of [[history/people/elizabeth-vigne|Elizabeth Belshaw]] [MS]. Within fifty years of its founding, Greyton had two churches [TBV].

The first Anglican services in Greyton were held in an old flat-roofed building, which later became the Moravian school and is now the town library [TBV]. St Andrew's church was built in 1904 [MS]. Theewaterskloof Municipality's planning documents note that the church and the streets around it form an attractive historic streetscape [IC].

The gravestones of Herbert and Elizabeth Vigne were later moved to the grounds of St Andrew's [MS].

## The wider church

The Anglican Church at the Cape grew under British rule after 1806 [TBV]. The Diocese of Cape Town was divided into three in 2005, and the Overberg became part of the new Diocese of False Bay [IC].
