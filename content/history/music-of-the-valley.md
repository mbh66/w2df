Music has been part of life at Genadendal from the first year of the mission. Hymns were taught under the pear tree in 1737, brass instruments were part of the teachers' college from 1838, and a brass band has played in the village since at least 1856 [MS]. The Mission Museum holds what is described as South Africa's oldest playable pipe organ, and a large collection of music written and copied in the valley [IC]. This page brings that story together.

## Hymns and singing

Music came to the valley with the first mission. [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]] taught sacred songs alongside reading and writing from 1737, with Africo interpreting [MS]. Schmidt also wrote hymns of his own [MS]. When [[history/people/missionaries-of-1792|the missionaries of 1792]] reopened the mission, they began teaching hymn singing again [MS].

People at the mission copied out by hand the music the missionaries gave them, and this became a lasting tradition [MS]. In 1880 the Moravian Church's Dutch hymnal, first printed in 1773, was revised at Genadendal, with about 800 hymns [TBV].

## The organ

A pipe organ arrived at Genadendal in 1832, and at first the missionaries played it [TBV]. The Mission Museum holds what the museum and music researchers describe as the oldest playable pipe organ in South Africa [IC]. A 2023 article in the *Journal of the Musical Arts in Africa* studies its pipework and history [IC]. Whether the organ in the museum is the one that arrived in 1832 has not yet been confirmed for this page [TBV].

## The teachers' college and music

The teachers' training college founded by [[history/people/hans-peter-hallbeck|Hans Peter Hallbeck]] in 1838 taught music as part of its course [IC]. Students learned violin, piano, organ, singing, and choir, and brass instruments were taught with energy [MS]. The college's first director, Francke, was a gifted musician who set up church choirs [MS]. A music instruction book from the college, dated 1853, survives [MS]. Moravian sources credit Hallbeck and especially [[history/people/benno-marx|Benno Marx]] with fostering the musical talents of the students [MS].

Some sources give 1840 for the start of the college's music teaching [TBV].

## The brass band

Brass playing came to South Africa with the Moravian Church, and Genadendal was where it started, with the training school of 1838 [IC]. The Genadendal brass band gave its first recorded performance at Easter 1856 [MS]. It has played ever since [MS].

In Moravian villages, brass bands are part of church life [IC]. The bands played at the consecration of churches, on the church square during festivals, and on New Year's Day and other festive days [MS]. At Easter, Moravian sources say, the procession to the graveyard at dawn without the brass band "was unthinkable" [MS]. The Genadendal band still plays at services, funerals, and weddings, and gives an end-of-year Christmas concert in the Moravian church [MS]. Several of its members have played for more than 50 years [MS]. It played for President Mandela during his visit in 1995, and has worked with musician Abdullah Ibrahim's M5 Jazz Academy [MS].

Genadendal was one of the first bands in a movement that spread to Moravian congregations across the Western and Eastern Cape [IC]. In 1951 the bands formed the Moravian Brass Band Union of South Africa, which celebrated its 70th anniversary in 2021 [IC]. The union's history names Genadendal among the historic bands of the Overberg, with Elim, Groenland, and Karwyderskraal [MS]. Brass bands meet each year at a *Basuinfees* (trombone festival), named after Psalm 150's call to praise "with the sound of the trumpet" [MS].

## Composers

The mission's musical life produced composers of its own [MS]. Music researchers name Sacks Williams, Dan Apolles, and Daniel Ulster among them [MS]. Daniel Ulster (1926 to 1986), born at the Moravian station of Clarkson in the Eastern Cape, was an organist and choir leader who wrote new tunes for the church's hymns [MS]. More research is needed on the composers who lived and worked in this valley.

## The Genadendal Music Archive

The Mission Museum keeps printed and handwritten scores of sacred and secular music, portraits of composers, photographs, newspaper articles, and instruments, including small pipe organs, harmoniums, brass and string instruments, and pianos [MS]. The collection includes works by Mozart and Handel as well as chorales, hymns, and music by Moravian composers such as C.I. Latrobe [MS].

In 2020 the Genadendal Music Archive began cataloguing and digitising this collection, with funding from the German Federal Foreign Office and Stellenbosch University [IC]. It was first hosted by the Africa Open Institute at Stellenbosch University, and now works with the Moravian Knowledge Network at the Technische Universität Dresden [MS]. Digitised items are published on Stellenbosch University's SunDigital platform [IC]. The archive's aim is to support research into the role of music in the Moravian mission [MS].

## Why it matters

The valley's music is one of the clearest examples of what began here and lasted. It was carried by households and congregations through every hard period, from the 48 years without a missionary to the closing of the college in 1926 [MS]. It is still played and sung today. See [[history/what-began-here|What Began Here]].

## What is still open

- Whether the museum's organ is the one that arrived in 1832, and who built it.
- The history of brass bands and choirs in Bereaville, Voorstekraal, and Greyton.
- The lives of the valley's own composers, and where their music is kept.
- The history of choirs in the valley's churches and schools.
- The year the college's music teaching began: 1838 or 1840.
