Brian Rycroft was a South African botanist who led the National Botanic Gardens at Kirstenbosch for thirty years, until 1983 [IC]. In 1977, while still Director of Kirstenbosch, he established the Greyton Nature Reserve on the mountain commonage above [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] [IC]. The reserve covers about 2,200 hectares, and its trails, fynbos, and water sources are among the valley's most valued places today [IC].

## His life

Hedley Brian Rycroft was born in Pietermaritzburg on 26 July 1910, the only son of English parents who had come to South Africa from Bedford [TBV]. He became a botanist, and succeeded Professor Robert Harold Compton as Director of the National Botanic Gardens at Kirstenbosch [IC]. Sources give the year he took up the post as 1953 or 1954 [IC]. SANBI describes him as the first South African-born director of Kirstenbosch [MS].

During his thirty years as director, the number of national botanical gardens in South Africa grew a great deal, and SANBI credits him with a leading part in that growth [MS]. He retired in July 1983 [IC]. He died on 1 December 1990 in East London [TBV].

## The Greyton Nature Reserve

In 1977 Rycroft established the Greyton Nature Reserve [IC]. It was proclaimed on 13 May 1977 [IC]. It lies on the steep southern slopes of the Riviersonderend mountains, on the commonage that Greyton's histories say [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]] left to the village's plot owners [IC]. It covers about 2,200 hectares, up to the peaks of Uitkyk, Wa en Osse, and Perdekop [IC].

The [[greyton-conservation-society|Greyton Conservation Society]], active since 1979, helps Theewaterskloof Municipality manage the reserve [MS]. The reserve protects Greyton Shale Fynbos, a plant community named after the village, which was listed as Critically Endangered in 2022 [IC].

Whether Rycroft lived in Greyton, or how he came to take up the reserve, is not recorded in the sources found so far [TBV].
