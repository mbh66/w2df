Leefregt Ari and Sebastian Hendrik were two older men of Genadendal who, in 1855, were the last people there who could still speak the Hessequa language [MS]. When the Governor of the Cape, Sir George Grey, visited on 3 January 1855, they were called to speak it in front of him [MS]. This page covers the two together, because the records name them together and say little about either.

## The language

When [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]] came in 1737, a Khoekhoe man named [[history/people/africo|Africo]], who understood Dutch, acted as his interpreter [MS]. Schmidt tried to learn the Khoekhoe language, but found one of its clicks too hard, and taught in Dutch instead [MS]. Over the next century the Khoekhoe language at Genadendal gave way to Dutch, and only older residents could still speak it [MS]. [[history/people/hans-peter-hallbeck|Hans Peter Hallbeck]], who led the mission from 1817 to 1840, compiled a list of words in this dialect of Hessequa, the *Hottentotsche Wörterbuch*, now in the archives at Herrnhut in Germany [MS].

## The visit of 1855

Sir George Grey became Governor of the Cape in 1854, and visited Genadendal on 3 January 1855 [MS]. [[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says that because of his interest in the cultures of indigenous peoples, he asked about the language spoken there, and that Leefregt Ari and Sebastian Hendrik, the last two who could speak Hessequa, were called to meet him [MS]. They spoke together in the language in front of him [MS]. Balie's history says it was the first time Grey had heard it [MS].

## Leefregt Ari as a herdsman

The English artist George French Angas visited Genadendal in 1846 and painted Leefregt Ari as a herdsman [MS]. In a verse with the painting, Angas described him tending another man's flock on land his father had once owned [MS]. Balie's history uses the picture to tell how the Khoekhoe, once cattle owners, became herdsmen for the settlers [MS]. A photograph of both men is credited to the photographer F.A. York, who visited Genadendal in the 1840s [MS].
