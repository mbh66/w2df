Herbert Vigne was an English settler who bought the farm Weltevreden in 1846 and laid out a village on it, which he named Greyton [IC]. Greyton's histories call him the father of Greyton [MS]. He came to the valley a century after [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]], and his village grew up beside the mission settlements. Its story is part of the history of the whole valley.

## His life

Vigne was the son of a London merchant whose family descended from Huguenots, French Protestants who had fled to England [TBV]. He came to the Cape in 1842 with his older brother, Henry Vigne (1805 to 1881), who farmed Tygerhoek near present-day Riviersonderend [TBV]. The family held Tygerhoek for about 120 years [TBV]. Greyton histories say the brothers fell out, and that Herbert had to leave Tygerhoek [TBV].

In 1846 he bought Weltevreden [IC]. Some sources give 1845 [IC]. A Greyton history says he first settled on a farm at Boschmanskloof [TBV]. Weltevreden had been granted to [[history/people/marthinus-theunissen|Marthinus Theunissen]] in 1793, on land that Greyton's histories say had been held by the Khoekhoe leader [[history/people/stoffel-koekson|Stoffel Koekson]] [IC]. It later passed to [[history/people/hendrik-cloete|Hendrik Cloete]] of Groot Constantia in 1795, and in 1839 to [[history/people/john-malcolm-stewart|John Malcolm Stewart]], its first British owner [IC].

In the 1860s he married [[history/people/elizabeth-vigne|Elizabeth Belshaw]], who was 27 years younger than him [TBV]. They had three sons and a daughter [TBV]. He died in 1895 [IC]. After his death his heirs divided his farm in the village, De Bos, and Greyton went through hard years [TBV]. The gravestones of Herbert and Elizabeth Vigne were later moved to the grounds of St Andrew's Anglican Church in Greyton [MS].

## Founding Greyton

Vigne laid out a village on Weltevreden. Sources date its founding to 1852, with the first plots sold in 1854 [IC]. He named it after Sir George Grey, the Governor of the Cape [IC]. The surveyor [[history/people/j-g-rietz|J.G. Rietz]] drew the town plan [IC].

South African History Online says the plots came with title deeds, water rights, and grazing rights, and were sold to buyers of any race, nationality, or religion [TBV]. It calls Greyton the only town in the Cape where such land was for sale to anyone [TBV]. This claim matters for the valley's later history, because a century later the Group Areas Act forced coloured families out of Greyton to [[villages/heuwelkroon|Heuwelkroon]] [IC]. It has not been checked against the deeds.

Greyton's histories say Vigne kept two small portions for himself and left the rest of the farm to the owners of the plots as a commonage, shared land for grazing [TBV]. The same wording appears in many sources, which suggests they share one origin [MS]. His will or deed of gift has not been found [TBV]. Part of the commonage is now the Greyton Nature Reserve, proclaimed on 13 May 1977 [IC].

## Links to other figures

- [[history/people/stoffel-koekson|Stoffel Koekson]]'s people are said to have held the land before it became Weltevreden, and to have settled at Boschmanskloof, where Vigne first lived [TBV].
- [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]] started the mission at Genadendal 116 years before Greyton's first plots were sold.

## What is still open

- Whether his will or a deed of gift created the commonage, and on what terms. A search of the Deeds Office and the Cape Archives would be needed.
- Whether the first plots were open to buyers of every race, as Greyton sources say. The first title deeds would show this.
- His exact dates of birth and death.
- His links with Genadendal, which Greyton histories mention without detail.
