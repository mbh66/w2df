Hans Peter Hallbeck was a Moravian missionary from Sweden who led the Moravian missions in South Africa from Genadendal between 1817 and 1840 [MS]. In 1838 he founded South Africa's first teachers' training college at Genadendal [IC]. He became the first bishop to serve in South Africa [MS]. The years he led the mission are often called Genadendal's golden age [MS].

## His life

Hallbeck was born in Sweden in 1784 and studied theology at Lund University [MS]. After his studies he joined the Moravian Church [MS]. [[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] gives his birth as 18 March 1784 in Malmö, the son of a cooper, and says he served the church at Gracehill in Ireland from 1813 and at Fairfield in England from 1815 [MS]. In 1817 he was appointed superintendent of the Moravian missions in South Africa and settled at Genadendal [MS]. Balie's history says he and his wife, Johanna Beck, arrived on 17 December 1817 [MS].

He led the missions through a time of fast change. Ordinance 50 of 1828 gave free people of colour legal equality [IC]. Slavery ended in law in 1834 and completely on 1 December 1838, and many freed people came to the mission stations [IC]. Genadendal's population grew from 1,446 in 1837 to 2,187 in 1840 [MS]. Hallbeck also set up several new Moravian mission stations and wrote rules for how the missions were run, suited to conditions at the Cape [MS]. Balie's history names them as Enon and Hemel-en-Aarde (1818), Elim (1824), and Clarkson (1839) [MS].

At Genadendal, Balie's history credits him with a lending library in 1825, a tannery and new clothing and shoe trades from 1828, and South Africa's first kindergarten, opened on 12 September 1831 [MS]. He began training teachers privately in 1828, and infant schools were opened on all the mission stations [MS]. To win over the crying infants at the kindergarten, the book says, he dressed up as one of their mothers [MS].

He wrote regular reports that were published in Moravian mission magazines in Europe and North America [MS]. They are among the main sources for the valley's history in these years. In a letter dated 27 September 1838 he reported that 92 of 97 girls examined in the mission schools could read as well as children of their age in European schools [MS].

He was the first bishop to serve in South Africa [MS]. He died in 1840 [MS]. Balie's history quotes the missionaries' account of his last illness, an abscess and erysipelas, which began on 16 December 1840 and ended with his death a few days later [MS].

## The teachers' training college

In 1838 Hallbeck founded the first teachers' training college in South Africa at Genadendal [IC]. Mission sources give its opening date as 12 September 1838 [MS]. Balie's history says 11 students enrolled that day, with Hallbeck as principal and C.F. Francke as lecturer [MS]. It says the college was a response to the end of slavery: the mission expected many freed people to settle at its stations, and decided to train teachers from its own congregations to serve them [MS]. It trained teachers, and later also pastors, for the mission schools [MS]. It is known in Afrikaans as the Kweekskool [MS].

The government closed the college in 1926, arguing that coloured people did not need higher education because they were suited to farm work [IC]. One source says teacher training ended in 1937, and that Emil Weder Secondary School opened in 1938 in its place [TBV]. Balie's history gives 1926 and 1927 for the closure, and says 236 teachers qualified there in 90 years [MS]. See [[emil-weder-secondary|Emil Weder Secondary School]].

## His legacy in the valley

- Hallbeck House, in the mission complex on Church Square, is a Provincial Heritage Site [IC].
- The college he founded is the first link in the valley's long tradition of training teachers. The [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Valley of Grace Learning Campus]] idea proposes bringing study after school back to the valley for the first time since the college closed.

## What is still open

- The exact dates and places of his birth and death.
- Which mission stations he founded. Balie's history names four, listed above, and they have not been checked against other sources.
- When teacher training at Genadendal ended: 1926, 1927, or 1937.
- How the teachers trained at the college, and the families of the valley, saw his leadership.
