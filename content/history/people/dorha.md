Dorha, whom the Dutch called Klaas, was the head of a Chainoqua clan in the late 1600s [IC]. The Chainoqua were the Khoekhoe people whose land took in this valley [IC]. Dorha traded cattle with the Dutch East India Company (VOC) and, from 1679 to 1693, acted as its go-between with the Chainoqua and Hessequa [IC]. His life shows how hard it was for Khoekhoe leaders to hold their ground as the colony grew. He worked with the VOC, fought alongside it, and was later imprisoned by it.

## His life

Dorha first appears in the Dutch records around 1672, as a trader of cattle with the VOC [TBV]. His kraal stood at Knoflokskloof, near the present Lebanon forestry station outside Grabouw [IC].

In the war of 1673 to 1677 between the VOC and the Cochoqua leader Gonnema, Dorha fought against Gonnema [TBV]. From 1679 to 1693 he served as the go-between for the VOC with the Chainoqua and the Hessequa [IC]. By the end of the century the company had several cattle posts in the Overberg, where it kept the animals it bartered from the Khoekhoe [IC].

In 1686, after San hunters known to the Dutch as Obiqua had killed colonists, Dorha attacked them, and the VOC's Council rewarded him with copper beads, tobacco, arrack, and rice [TBV].

His standing with the company did not last. In 1693 Governor Simon van der Stel sent him to Robben Island, after he was accused of taking cattle from Koopman, a rival Khoekhoe leader [TBV]. After his release, his wife was killed by Koopman's people [TBV]. Dorha was killed in the winter of 1701, while searching for a lost ox near Koopman's kraal [TBV].

## How to read his story

Historians describe Dorha in different ways. Some sources call him an ally of the Dutch [TBV]. Others count him, with Soeswa, among the Chainoqua leaders who tried to hold back the colony's spread into their land [IC]. Both can be true. Leaders like Dorha had to deal with a power that wanted their cattle and, more and more, their land. They traded and made alliances, and fought when they had to. They were pushed aside all the same.
