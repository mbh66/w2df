Johann Daniel Beinbrecht was a Moravian missionary and coppersmith who came to Genadendal in 1815 [MS]. In 1819 and 1820 he led the residents of Genadendal in building a bridge over the Sonderend River [IC]. It was built as the residents' own undertaking, and it is now a Provincial Heritage Site [IC]. He also made much of the copperware now in the mission museum [MS].

## His life

Beinbrecht was a trained coppersmith [MS]. [[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says he came to South Africa in 1815, and elsewhere that he settled at Genadendal in 1816, the first fully trained coppersmith there [MS]. By then Kühnel had died, and the cutlers, blacksmiths, tinsmiths, and coppersmiths shared one workshop [MS]. Many of the copper vessels in the museum came from his workshop [MS].

## The bridge

Genadendal was one of the largest settlements in the colony, but in winter the Sonderend River often rose and cut the road [MS]. In 1819 the community decided to build a bridge, with Beinbrecht in charge [MS]. The superintendent, [[history/people/hans-peter-hallbeck|Hans Peter Hallbeck]], reported that a bridge built as a private undertaking by the Genadendal residents was "such an unheard-of thing in this Colony" that it was talked of "with astonishment from Cape Town to Grahams Town", and hoped it would silence those who accused the Genadendal people of sloth [MS]. In 1830 the Dutch traveller Teenstra wrote that it was the only bridge in the colony apart from a few small culverts near Cape Town [MS].

Balie's history calls it the first solid bridge in South Africa, and says its six original pillars can still be seen [MS]. Residents called it *die Steg* [MS]. The bridge over the Sonderend River, of 1819 and 1820, is a Provincial Heritage Site [IC].

## His death

In April 1824 a missionary wrote that Beinbrecht was so ill with dropsy that he might not be alive when the letter arrived [MS]. He died on 2 August 1824, in the 52nd year of his age [MS]. The mission's report says the welfare of the mission lay close to his heart [MS].

## Links to other figures

- [[history/people/hans-peter-hallbeck|Hans Peter Hallbeck]] was superintendent when the bridge was built, and reported on it.

## His legacy in the valley

- The bridge's stone pillars still stand over the Sonderend River [MS].
- His copperware is in the [[cultural/sport-heritage-arts-culture/genadendal-mission-museum|Genadendal Mission Museum]] [MS].

## What is still open

- Whether he came in 1815 or 1816. Balie's history gives both.
- The spelling of his name. Balie's list of missionaries gives Beinbrech, and this wiki earlier used Beinbach.
- Who among the residents built the bridge, and how the work was organised.
