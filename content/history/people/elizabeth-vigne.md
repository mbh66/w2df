Elizabeth Vigne, born Elizabeth Belshaw, was the wife of [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]], who founded [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] [IC]. She came from a Greyton family of British descent [MS]. She married Vigne in the 1860s, when she was 27 years younger than him [IC]. They lived on their town farm, De Bos, and had three sons and a daughter [TBV]. Greyton's histories tell the village's founding as Herbert's story. Elizabeth lived in Greyton through its first decades, and her family's house still stands there [MS].

## Her life

Elizabeth was born into the Belshaw family, who lived in Greyton [MS]. Their family home is now a guesthouse in the village, the Lord Pickleby [MS]. Greyton's histories describe her as "a young girl of British stock" [MS]. Robinson's study names her father as James Belshaw, one of the buyers of Herbert Vigne's plots, and says the Belshaw family held large pieces of land in the village [TBV].

She married Herbert Vigne in the 1860s [IC]. He had founded the village a few years before, and was 27 years older than her [IC]. They settled on De Bos, their farm in the village [TBV]. They had three sons and a daughter [TBV]. Robinson says they had five children [TBV].

A genealogy site gives her dates as 1847 to 1895, which would mean she died in the same year as her husband [TBV]. Robinson's study, which drew on the family's papers, agrees: it says she died without a will, aged 47, four months after her husband [TBV]. A death notice or church record would confirm the exact dates.

After Herbert's death in 1895, their heirs divided De Bos, and Greyton went through hard years [TBV]. The gravestones of Herbert and Elizabeth Vigne were later moved to the grounds of [[st-andrews-anglican-church-greyton|St Andrew's Anglican Church]] in Greyton [MS].
