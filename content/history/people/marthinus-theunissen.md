Marthinus Theunissen was the first colonist known to have farmed the land where Greyton now stands [IC]. In 1793 he was granted the farm Weltevreden, on land that Greyton's histories say had belonged to the Khoekhoe leader [[history/people/stoffel-koekson|Stoffel Koekson]] [IC]. He built a homestead there, but did not stay long [TBV]. Sixty years later, [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]] laid out the village of Greyton on the same farm [IC].

## Weltevreden

Forum research notes record Theunissen holding Weltevreden as a loan farm from about 1791, and the farm was granted to him in 1793 [IC]. A loan farm was land the VOC let to a colonist for a yearly fee, without full ownership [IC]. Greyton's histories describe him as a young Dutchman [TBV]. The homestead he built on the farm no longer stands [TBV].

## Why he left

Wikipedia, drawing on Greyton's histories, says Theunissen could not settle his constant disputes with his neighbours at Boschmanskloof and Genadendal, over boundaries and straying cattle [TBV]. By 1795 the farm had passed to [[history/people/hendrik-cloete|Hendrik Cloete]] of Groot Constantia [IC].

The timing matters. The Moravian mission at Baviaanskloof reopened on 24 December 1792, and Khoekhoe families moved there in large numbers in 1793 [MS]. Overberg farmers opposed the mission in those years, because it drew Khoekhoe workers away from their farms [IC]. Theunissen's quarrels with Genadendal fall in the same years, though the sources do not say what they were about beyond boundaries and cattle.

## In the missionaries' diaries

The diaries of the Moravian missionaries at Baviaanskloof, now Genadendal, never name Weltevreden or Marthinus Theunissen. They do describe, from 1792 to 1796, a young neighbour they call "young Teunis" or "Baas Teunis's son", the son of [[history/people/baas-teunis|Baas Teunis]], who kept the company's post at Soetemelksvlei [MS]. What they say of him matches what is known of Theunissen and Weltevreden so closely that he was probably the same man, and his place was probably Weltevreden [TBV]:

- **A young man with a new farm.** In December 1792 he was unmarried and building a house about an hour to an hour and a half from the mission. The missionaries called him their nearest neighbour, and "a very pleasant man" [MS]. Weltevreden was held by Theunissen as a loan farm from 1791 and granted in 1793 [IC].
- **Land with a condition.** His father told the missionaries that the place had belonged to the Khoekhoe, and that the acting governor, Johan Isaac Rhenius, had given it to his son on condition that he did no harm to any Khoekhoe and did not drive them away. If he did, he must leave the place [MS]. Rhenius headed the government from 1791 to 1793 [MS].
- **Married at Stellenbosch.** In May 1793 he married at Stellenbosch, and he and his wife and sister visited the mission in June [MS]. In August 1794 their child died while they were staying at his parents' post [MS].
- **Vines.** In 1793 he laid out "a very large vineyard", and showed the missionaries how to plant vines [MS]. Greyton's histories say Theunissen later owned the wine farm Vergelegen [TBV].
- **Quarrels over cattle.** By November 1794 he was writing that he was not obliged to let the Khoekhoe's cattle graze on his ground, and that he would beat them and make them pay for any damage [MS]. He had Khoekhoe cattle impounded, and their owners paid a shilling a head to get them back [MS]. Greyton's histories say Theunissen left Weltevreden after disputes with his neighbours at Boschmanskloof and Genadendal over boundaries and straying cattle [TBV].
- **The place is sold.** By January 1795 his father held the place again [MS]. In February 1795 "Mr Clüde, to whom Constantia belongs", visited the mission, and by April a Mr Claude had bought the land where the son had impounded the Khoekhoe's cattle [MS]. Weltevreden passed to [[history/people/hendrik-cloete|Hendrik Cloete]] of Groot Constantia in 1795 [IC].

He stayed in the district for a time. In March 1796 he quarrelled with the Khoekhoe of the mission, who had complained about his father to the British General [MS].

The name Theunissen means "son of Teunis", which fits the diaries' "Baas Teunis's son". Only a deed or a company record naming the holder of the place could confirm the identification.

## Later life

Greyton's histories say Theunissen later became a wealthy man in Stellenbosch and owned the famous wine farm Vergelegen for a time [TBV]. The Theunissen family bought Vergelegen, near Somerset West, in 1798 and held it until 1899 [IC]. Whether Marthinus himself was the buyer has not been confirmed [TBV].

## Links to other figures

- [[history/people/stoffel-koekson|Stoffel Koekson]]'s people are said to have held the land before it was granted to Theunissen.

- [[history/people/missionaries-of-1792|The missionaries of 1792]] reopened the mission at Genadendal while he was farming next door.

## What is still open

- The dates of his birth and death.
- Whether the VOC grant of 1793 names Koekson or any Khoekhoe occupiers of the land.
- Whether he is the "young Teunis" of the missionaries' diaries, as the evidence above suggests. A deed or company record naming the holder of Weltevreden in 1791 to 1795 would settle it.
- What his disputes with Boschmanskloof were about.
- Whether he is the Theunissen who bought Vergelegen in 1798.
