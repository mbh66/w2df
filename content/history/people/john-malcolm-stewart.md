John Malcolm Stewart became the first British owner of Weltevreden in 1839 [IC]. He held the farm until 1846, when [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]] bought it and later laid out [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] on it [IC]. Apart from these two dates, the sources found so far say nothing about him. His page records what is known, so that anyone who finds more can add it.

## What is known

The British took over the Cape from the Dutch for good in 1806 [IC]. In the decades that followed, British settlers and merchants bought land across the colony [TBV]. Stewart acquired Weltevreden in 1839, the year after slavery ended completely at the Cape [IC].

He owned the farm for about seven years. In 1846 it passed to Herbert Vigne [IC].

How he came to own the farm, whether he lived on it, and what he used it for are not recorded in the sources used for this page. It is also not known who owned Weltevreden between the death of [[history/people/hendrik-cloete|Hendrik Cloete]] in 1799 and 1839.

## Links to other figures

## What is still open

- Who he was, where he came from, and when he lived and died.
- Whether he lived at Weltevreden, and what he farmed there.
- How the farm passed to him, and to Vigne. The Deeds Office and the Cape Archives hold the title records.
