Johannes Ruiter was a resident of Genadendal who, in 1849, became the first person from the village appointed as its policeman [MS]. He had served in almost all the wars on the Eastern Frontier [MS]. Farmers in the district petitioned against a Khoekhoe man holding the post, and four months later he was replaced by a European constable [MS]. His short time in office shows how the colony's race order reached into the mission village.

## His appointment

By the middle of the 1800s the missionaries and their church council dealt mainly with church matters, and lawlessness in the village's everyday affairs was growing [MS]. Strangers settled in the village without leave [MS]. The magistrate recommended that a policeman be appointed [MS]. The missionary Christian Ludwig Teutsch, who had succeeded Hallbeck as superintendent, appointed Ruiter in 1849 [MS].

He was described as "honest with superior intellect and resolution that secured him general obedience and esteem" [MS]. Farmers in the region objected. They feared that his appointment would open the way to "Khoi field-cornets acting under the orders of Khoi justices of peace and Khoi magistrates", to whom they might one day be subject [MS]. They petitioned against him, and four months later he was replaced by a European constable [MS].

[[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] also says, on another page, that Ruiter was appointed in 1854 and reported to the field-cornet [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]], the founder of Greyton [MS].
