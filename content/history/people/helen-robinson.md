> [!warning] Died in living memory
> Dr Helen Robinson died in September 2026, and her family and many people who knew her are alive. This page draws on her study of Greyton and on one published tribute. It is open to correction by her family and by those who knew her, and the moderators will review it as responses come in.

Dr Helen Robinson was a Cape Town historian, author, and former lecturer in drama and communication, who died in Wynberg on 13 September 2026, aged 96 [MS]. In the mid-1980s she studied [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] for a postgraduate degree in social anthropology at the University of Cape Town [IC]. Her thesis, *Invisible Villagers*, traces the village from its founding in 1854 to the Group Areas removals to [[villages/heuwelkroon|Heuwelkroon]] and the newcomers who bought into Greyton afterwards [MS]. It is the fullest written account of the removals, and it is often mentioned in the valley's discussions of its history.

## Her life

Robinson began as a theatre practitioner and became a lecturer in drama and communication [MS]. She moved from Port Elizabeth to Wynberg in 1973 [MS]. She was a founding member of the Maynardville Open Air Theatre in Wynberg, and stayed involved with it from its beginning [MS].

While lecturing at the University of Cape Town, she took up postgraduate studies in social anthropology and psychology [MS]. Her master's degree was a study of forced removals at Greyton [MS]. She later wrote a PhD on Wynberg in the university's Department of History, published in 1998 as *Beyond the City Limits: People and Property at Wynberg* [IC]. In the years that followed she wrote further histories of the southern suburbs, a history of the Shakespeare season at Maynardville, and a novel [IC].

## Her study of Greyton

Robinson did her fieldwork in several long stays in Greyton: June and July 1984, October 1984 to January 1985, and June and July 1985, with weekend visits between them [MS]. She first drew a sample of 80 households and later widened it to 150, including households in Heuwelkroon [MS]. She interviewed residents in English and Afrikaans, and changed their names in the thesis to protect them [MS]. Her supervisor was Professor Martin West [MS]. The Human Sciences Research Council, the University of Cape Town, and the Centre for African Studies supported the study [MS].

Her written sources included the Cape Town Deeds Office and Archives, the archive of the Genadendal mission, the records of the Divisional Council in Caledon and of the Greyton Municipality, and family papers of descendants of [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]] [MS]. She used the papers of Dr Oscar Wollheim on the Group Areas Act in Greyton, held in the University of Cape Town's archives, and tape recordings of interviews made in Greyton by Karl von Holdt in 1978 and 1979 [MS].

The thesis asks whether Greyton was ever one "community", and what was lost when the people classified coloured were removed from the village [MS]. It covers:

- the farm Weltevreden, its owners, and the dealings between local farmers and the Genadendal mission before 1854 [MS];
- Herbert Vigne's family, the conditions under which the first plots were sold in 1854, and the design of the village [MS];
- the village's schools, churches, and population up to 1910, with census figures from 1911 to 1980 [MS];
- the application of the Group Areas Act in Greyton from 1964, the removals of 1969 to 1971, and the eight families who refused to move [MS];
- the property market that followed, and the retired people and weekend residents who bought into the village [MS];
- life in Heuwelkroon in 1985 and 1986, and the debates in Greyton about preservation and growth [MS].

The tribute gives the degree as a master's of 1985 [MS]. The thesis's title page is dated 1986, and the university's library lists it under that year as a doctoral thesis, titled "Invisible villages" [IC]. The title page reads "Invisible Villagers" [MS].

## Her publications

- *Invisible Villagers: Changing Residential Patterns and Relationships in a Rural Village* (1986). Thesis, Department of Social Anthropology, University of Cape Town. A study of Greyton from its founding to 1986, centred on the Group Areas Act and its effects on residents classified coloured. ![[thesis_hum_1986_robinson_helen.pdf]]
- *Beyond the City Limits: People and Property at Wynberg, 1795 to 1927* (1998). Her PhD thesis, published in Cape Town. The history of land and its owners in Wynberg.
- *Wynberg: A Special Place* (2001). A history of Wynberg.
- *Shakespeare at Maynardville* (2005). A history of the Shakespeare season at the Maynardville Open Air Theatre, for its fiftieth year.
- *The Villages of the Liesbeeck* (2011). The early settlements along the Liesbeeck River in Cape Town.
- *The Trovato Link* (2012). Her first novel.
- *Constantia and Its Neighbours* (year not yet found). The wine farms and people of the Constantia Valley.
