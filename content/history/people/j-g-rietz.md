J.G. Rietz was the senior surveyor who designed and set out the village of [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] for [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]] in the 1850s [IC]. The layout he drew has changed little since, and it is one reason Greyton looks the way it does today [IC]. Only his initials, his title, and this work are recorded in the sources found so far.

## His plan for Greyton

Vigne founded the village in the early 1850s, and the first plots were sold in 1854 [IC]. Rietz's plan divided the farm Weltevreden into long, narrow plots [IC]. Each was large enough for a house and a strip of land for a few animals and crops [IC]. Houses stood close to the street, with vegetable gardens and orchards behind [MS]. Water from the mountain reached every plot through furrows, the leiwater system still in use [MS]. Around the village lay the commonage, where plot owners had grazing rights [MS].

The village still keeps this pattern. Its oak-lined streets, its long plots, and its furrows are part of what makes Greyton a heritage village today [IC].
