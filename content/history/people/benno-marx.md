Benno Marx was a Moravian missionary at Genadendal [IC]. In 1859 he founded *De Bode van Genadendal*, a monthly paper printed on the mission's press [IC]. *De Bode* was written in the plain Dutch spoken at Genadendal, and it came out 17 years before *Die Afrikaanse Patriot* (1876), the paper often called the first in Afrikaans [IC]. Marx is called the father of the Genadendal printing works [MS].

## The printing press

Genadendal had one of the first printing presses in the colony [IC]. Sources differ on when it arrived: 1834 or 1837 [IC]. It was a simple wooden press of the Gutenberg type, and it is now in the [[genadendal-mission-museum|Genadendal Mission Museum]] [IC]. A more advanced Albion press followed in 1861 [TBV].

Marx wanted the press to print books, pamphlets, and papers through which the people of the mission could speak to each other [MS]. Under him it became a printing works that served the mission stations for generations [MS]. It closed in 2003, after more than 170 years [MS].

## De Bode van Genadendal

*De Bode* ("The Messenger") first appeared in 1859 [IC]. It was one of the first five church papers in South Africa [MS]. It carried articles on faith, the histories of the mission stations, life stories of members, poems, and letters from readers, who often wrote under pen names [MS]. Its language was a simple Dutch, close to how people at Genadendal spoke [IC].

The last issue of *De Bode* appeared in March 1914. From April 1914 it was followed by *Die Huisvriend* [IC].

Historians use *De Bode* in the debate about where written Afrikaans came from. Its Genadendal Dutch had drawn away from formal Dutch long before the white Afrikaans language movement of the 1870s [IC]. Some historians argue that this shows how much written Afrikaans owes to coloured communities such as Genadendal [IC].

Marx also wrote *Benigna*, a story that some consider the first novel in Afrikaans [TBV].

## His legacy in the valley

- The museum keeps the press and a record of what it printed [MS].
- *De Bode* is one reason Genadendal holds a place in the history of Afrikaans.

## What is still open

- Where Marx was born, when he came to Genadendal, and when he left.
- Whether *Benigna* is the same work as *Benigna van Groenekloof*, and when it was printed.
- When the first press arrived: 1834 or 1837.
- Who wrote for *De Bode* from the valley, and whether copies survive in families.
