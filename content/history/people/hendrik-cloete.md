Hendrik Cloete was one of the best-known farmers of the Cape in the late 1700s. He owned Groot Constantia near Cape Town from 1778, and made its sweet Constantia wine famous in Europe [IC]. In 1795 he took over the farm Weltevreden, where [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] now stands, from [[history/people/marthinus-theunissen|Marthinus Theunissen]] [IC]. He is part of the valley's story as one link in the chain of owners through which Khoekhoe grazing land became a colonial farm, and later a village.

## His life

Cloete was born in 1725 and grew up on the family farm Nooitgedacht near Stellenbosch, which came to him through his mother's family [IC]. He married Hester Anna Lourens, and they had eleven children [MS].

In December 1778 he bought Groot Constantia for 90,000 guilders, a very high price at the time [IC]. Over fourteen years he rebuilt the manor house, built a new wine cellar (finished in about 1791), and planted thousands of new vines [IC]. The architect Louis Michel Thibault and the sculptor Anton Anreith are credited with the cellar and the gables of the manor house [IC]. From 1788 he argued with the Dutch East India Company (VOC) for the right to sell his wine freely, outside the company's quotas [IC].

Like other large Cape farms of the time, Groot Constantia depended on the labour of enslaved people [TBV]. How many people Cloete held in slavery, and whether any of them worked at Weltevreden, is not recorded in the sources used for this page.

After his wife died in 1794, Cloete went back to Nooitgedacht, where he died in 1799 [IC]. His youngest son, also named Hendrik, inherited Groot Constantia [IC].

## Weltevreden

In 1795 Cloete took over Weltevreden from Marthinus Theunissen, who had left after disputes with his neighbours at Boschmanskloof and Genadendal [IC]. Why a Constantia wine farmer wanted a farm in the Overberg is not recorded. Many Cape farmers of the time held farms far from home for grazing cattle [TBV].

Cloete died four years later. Who held Weltevreden between his death in 1799 and 1839, when [[history/people/john-malcolm-stewart|John Malcolm Stewart]] became its first British owner, has not yet been found.
