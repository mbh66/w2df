Edward Lees was a British doctor and a member of the Moravian Church who, from 1832, practised at Genadendal as its first qualified physician [MS]. He served both the mission's residents and the farmers of the district until his death in 1837 [MS]. He was also one of the organists of the mission church [MS].

## His life

Lees was born at Fairfield in England in 1806, and was taught as a child by [[history/people/hans-peter-hallbeck|Hans Peter Hallbeck]], who served the Moravian congregation there from 1815 [MS]. In 1832 he came to the Cape for his health, and met his old teacher in Cape Town [MS]. Hallbeck invited him to Genadendal, and he agreed to set up his practice there [MS]. [[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says this was a great benefit to the mission and to the neighbouring farmers, since until then the nearest doctor was far away [MS]. He also helped with the teaching of English, and became one of the organists when the mission's first pipe organ came into use in 1832 [MS].

He fell ill early in 1837, and was treated by Dr Zeederberg of Cape Town and nursed by his wife [MS]. A fellow missionary, Teutsch, wrote that he had seldom seen greater willingness to leave this world than Lees showed in his last illness [MS]. He died on the morning of 14 April 1837, and is buried in the old mission cemetery [MS].

## His legacy in the valley

- A Laennec stethoscope, of the kind Lees brought with him, was found in an attic about 140 years after his death, and is in the [[cultural/sport-heritage-arts-culture/genadendal-mission-museum|Genadendal Mission Museum]] [MS].
- The museum's medical collection begins with his time [MS].

## What is still open

- His wife's name, and what became of her after 1837.
- Where the mission's first apothecary fits in. Balie's history dates it to 1825, before Lees came, and also credits Lees with opening it.
- Whether Mrs Lees's knitting and embroidery classes, which Balie's history dates to 1825, belong to his wife or to someone else.
