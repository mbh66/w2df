Christian Ludwig Rose was a Moravian missionary who came to Baviaanskloof in 1800 as superintendent of the church's mission at the Cape [MS]. In his five years there he reorganised the mission's finances and work, laid out its cemetery, and settled disputes over its boundaries with the farmers and the Cape authorities [MS]. He died in 1805, the first missionary buried in the cemetery he had laid out [MS].

## His life

Rose's first calling as a missionary was to Labrador, where he spent eight years [MS]. He and his wife arrived at Baviaanskloof on 17 May 1800, and he was made superintendent of the mission at the Cape [MS].

[[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says he reorganised the mission's finances, set up a new work schedule, and brought in new industries such as growing and spinning flax [MS]. He laid out the cemetery in four equal quarters, on the pattern of the one at Herrnhut, with each grave marked by a numbered wooden cross [MS]. He settled disputes over the mission's boundaries with the neighbouring farmers and the Cape authorities [MS]. Balie's history calls his rule strict and patriarchal [MS].

The missionaries' diaries show him at work in 1803 and 1804. In 1803 he began classes for people preparing for their first communion [MS]. In December 1803 he and [[history/people/johann-christian-kuhnel|Kühnel]] rode with some Khoekhoe residents to the edge of the mission's land to welcome the Commissioner-General, De Mist [MS]. In February 1804 he signed the mission's diary with Kohrhammer, [[history/people/hendrik-marsveld|Marsveld]], Schwinn, and Kühnel [MS].

He was ill for a year before he died, on 12 October 1805 [MS]. The mission's report says that after his death the congregation came in groups into the room and sang hymns around his body, and that he was buried on 16 October, after a funeral dinner, as the custom of the country required [MS]. Johann Adolf Küster came from Germany in 1807 to succeed him [MS].

## His legacy in the valley

- Visitors can still walk through the old mission cemetery he laid out [MS]. Its entrance bears the words *Gezaaid in Zwakheid* (Sown in weakness) [MS].
- Men and women were buried in separate rows until 1923, as at Herrnhut [MS].

## What is still open
