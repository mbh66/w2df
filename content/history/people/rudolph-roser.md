Rudolph Roser was a Moravian missionary and doctor who worked at Genadendal from 1849 to 1869 [MS]. He cared for the village through two epidemics, and his study of the plants, animals, and remedies of Genadendal earned him a doctorate from the University of Tübingen in 1856 [MS]. The museum's garden is named after him [MS].

## His life

[[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says Roser and his wife arrived at Genadendal on Wednesday 13 June 1849 [MS]. Its list of missionaries gives his years there as 1848 to 1869 [MS]. He knew the local plants and animals, and used homeopathic treatments [MS]. In 1856 the University of Tübingen awarded him a doctorate for a thesis on his natural history and medical observations at Genadendal [MS].

He saw the village through two epidemics. When measles swept Genadendal in 1858, 3,000 residents were vaccinated; 29 became seriously ill, and 10 died [MS]. In the typhoid epidemic of 1865, 30 of his 250 patients died, among them the missionary Johann Friedrich Heinrich [MS]. Balie's history quotes his belief that God used illness to bring people closer to Him [MS].

After he left in 1869, the missionaries Fischer, Wedemann, and Mosel carried on his medical work [MS].

## His legacy in the valley

- The museum's Baroque garden is named the Rudolph Roser Garden [MS].
- His thesis is one of the earliest studies of the valley's plants and animals [MS].
