Hendrik Marsveld was one of the three Moravian missionaries who reopened the mission at Baviaanskloof, now Genadendal, on 24 December 1792 [IC]. He was a tailor, and the only Dutchman of the three [MS]. Because he spoke Dutch, the language of the colony, he preached in it from the first months and made most of the long journeys to Cape Town to speak for the mission to the government [MS]. His reports of those journeys, copied into the missionaries' diaries, are some of the fullest records of how the company's officials treated the mission and its Khoekhoe residents. His story is also told, with that of Daniel Schwinn and Johann Christian Kühnel, on [[history/people/missionaries-of-1792|The Missionaries of 1792]].

## Before the Cape

Marsveld was a tailor [MS]. He was Dutch, while Schwinn and Kühnel were German [MS]. In 1794 letters reached him at Baviaanskloof from his own brother in Gouda, in the Netherlands [MS]. On his first journey to Cape Town in March 1793 he recorded that when he joined the church, he had hoped only to be "the kind of brother who had nothing else to see to than just to earn his bread", and was amazed to have been called to such a task [MS].

## At Baviaanskloof

The three missionaries arrived on 24 December 1792 [IC]. They found [[history/people/magdalena|Magdalena]], who had kept Georg Schmidt's congregation together for 48 years, and Marsveld recorded that she came to support them and helped students who were struggling [MS]. Formal lessons began on 4 March 1793 [MS]. When the school for adults opened, the children's school was put off until Marsveld came back from Cape Town [MS]. The two German missionaries could not yet preach in Dutch [MS].

Diaries: [A363349, entry for February and March 1793, and its note 16](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363349/52-february-and-1-to-3-march-1793.md)

### Speaking for the mission

Marsveld went to Cape Town again and again to secure the mission's place:

- **March 1793.** He travelled with [[history/people/baas-teunis|Baas Teunis]] of the company post at Soetemelksvlei. In Cape Town he met the acting governor, Johan Isaac Rhenius, and asked whether the mission could buy its land. Rhenius answered: "That is not necessary; the place is yours". When Marsveld asked what would happen if a later governor wanted it back, Rhenius said: "Even if 10 governors came, they could not do it, because the Council of Seventeen has so ordered it" [MS].
- **January 1794.** Hearing that the mission's place was to be taken, he rode to the Landdrost at Stellenbosch and on to the new Commissioner, [[history/people/abraham-josias-sluysken|Abraham Josias Sluysken]], who told him the mission could stay, "and so it shall remain" [MS]. Marsveld again offered to buy the land, and asked for the promise in writing. Neither was granted [MS].
- **January 1795.** He went to Sluysken to ask him to withdraw his order that the Khoekhoe living at the mission send their cattle away. Sluysken refused, saying farmers complained that Khoekhoe who were taught "would get behind their secrets". Marsveld answered that the farmers had no secrets except ploughing, sowing, and reaping, "and those they have to learn from the Hottentots" [MS].
- **October 1795.** After the British took the Cape, he went to the British commanders and asked permission for the mission to go on preaching, and for their protection. Both were granted [MS]. He also thanked Sluysken, who told him he had written to the British on the mission's behalf [MS].

Diaries: [A363595, lines 2604 to 2747](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363595/05-brother-marsvelds-journey-to-the-cape-march-1793.md); [A363595, lines 7645 to 7728](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363595/17-january-1794.md); [A363595, lines 13680 to 13806](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363595/30-january-1795.md); [A363596, lines 2612 to 2652](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363596/09-october-1795.md)

### Journeys in the country

In April 1794 Marsveld and Kühnel rode east with a Khoekhoe man from the mission, Jacob, to the Landdrost at Swellendam and on to the Snake River, probably the Slangrivier near present-day Heidelberg. There they visited Khoekhoe kraals, among them the family of a man who wanted to move to Baviaanskloof [MS]. In November 1794 Marsveld visited the Dutch Reformed minister Vos at Roodezand, now Tulbagh [MS].

At home he did the daily work of the mission: fetching firewood with Khoekhoe helpers, visiting the sick, and sitting with the dying [MS].

Diaries: [A363595, lines 8337 to 8427](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363595/20-1-to-8-april-1794-and-the-journey-to-the-snake-river.md); [A363595, lines 12317 to 12366](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363595/28-november-1794.md)

### The expulsion of 1795

On 29 July 1795 an order from [[history/people/bisani|Commandant Bisani]], a leader among the rebel burghers of Swellendam, told the missionaries to leave within three days [MS]. Marsveld went with the others to Cape Town, and returned with them after Sluysken told them on 7 August that they could go home [MS].

### Later years

Marsveld was still at Baviaanskloof in February 1804, when he signed the mission's diary with Christian Ludwig Rose, Johann Philipp Kohrhammer, Schwinn, and Kühnel [MS]. By then more than 1,000 people lived at the mission [MS]. When and where he died has not yet been found.

Diaries: [A363597, lines 1506 to 1510](https://github.com/mbh66/genadendal-diaries/blob/main/translations/en/A363597/09-february-1804-and-the-signatures.md)

## How to read his story

Almost everything known about Marsveld comes from the diaries he and his fellow missionaries wrote for their church in Europe. They record his journeys and conversations in his own words. They show the Khoekhoe of the mission through the missionaries' eyes, and they do not say what the Khoekhoe thought of him.

## His legacy in the valley

The school that began on 4 March 1793 has run ever since, and is now [[lr-schmidt-primary|L.R. Schmidt Primary School]] [MS]. The mission he helped to hold in place through the company's last years grew into Genadendal. The question he raised in 1793 and 1794, of who owns the mission's land, is still open today [IC]. See [[trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39]].
