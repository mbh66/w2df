Jan Willem Janssens was the Governor of the Cape for the Batavian Republic, the Dutch state of the time, from 1803 until the British took the Cape in January 1806 [IC]. In 1806 he renamed the mission at Baviaanskloof Genadendal, "valley of grace" [IC]. His government recruited Khoekhoe soldiers at the mission, and mission sources say it was their courage that moved him to give the new name [MS].

## Governor of the Cape

The Cape was handed back from Britain to the Dutch, now the Batavian Republic, in 1803, and Janssens governed it from 1 March 1803 [IC]. On 8 January 1806 a British force defeated his troops at Blaauwberg, near Cape Town, and the colony surrendered on 18 January [IC].

## Soldiers from the mission

Janssens's government recruited Khoekhoe men at Baviaanskloof for its army. The missionaries' diaries record that 22 men enlisted in September 1803, and that in October two baptised men were made corporals [MS]. In 1804, 187 women and children of the soldiers came from the camp at Wynberg to be cared for at the mission [MS]. [[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says men from the mission served in the Corps Vrije Hottentotten, the Battaillon Ligte Infanterie, and later the Cape Regiment, and that the missionary Johann Philipp Kohrhammer was chaplain to the Corps Vrije Hottentotten from 1804 to 1806 [MS].

## The new name

Balie's history says Janssens was so moved by the courage of the soldiers from Baviaanskloof that he decided in 1806 to replace its name, "glen of baboons", with Genadendal, "valley of grace" [MS]. It also says he took the decision after a visit to the mission in 1806 [MS], and it reproduces his letter announcing the new name [MS]. The mission's guest house lists him among the governors who stayed there [MS].

## What is still open

- The exact date of the renaming, and of the letter that announced it. Janssens lost the Cape in January 1806, so a visit in 1806 would have come in the first days of that year.
- How many men from the mission served in his army, and what became of them after Blaauwberg.
- Whether the residents themselves asked for, or welcomed, the new name.
