Janneke Tikkuie was the husband of Vehettge Tikkuie, who was baptised [[history/people/magdalena|Magdalena]] in 1742 [MS]. The two were married according to Khoekhoe custom [MS]. He helped [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]]'s mission in its first years by fetching supplies and hunting to feed the community [MS]. The Moravian Church in South Africa says he was Chainoqua and his wife Hessequa [MS]. If that is right, their marriage joined the two main Khoekhoe peoples of the Overberg.

## What is known

Sources spell his first name in different ways: Janneke, Jantjie, and Jaktie [MS]. All are Dutch forms, and his Khoekhoe name is not recorded [TBV]. Tikkuie (also written Tikhuie) was the family name he shared with his wife [MS].

He and Vehettge were among the Khoekhoe who went with Schmidt when he moved to Baviaanskloof in April 1738 [MS]. A mission of this kind depended on its Khoekhoe members for food and supplies. Janneke fetched supplies and hunted for the community [MS].

When Schmidt baptised Vehettge in 1742, he did not baptise Janneke [MS]. One historian suggests Schmidt judged him less ready in his faith [MS]. Whether he was baptised later is not recorded.

After Schmidt left in 1744, Magdalena went back to the Sergeants River area and kept the congregation together for 48 years [IC]. The sources do not say whether Janneke was with her in those years, or when he died.

## What is still open

- His Khoekhoe name, and which spelling of his Dutch name is correct.
- Whether he was Chainoqua and his wife Hessequa, as Moravian sources say.
- Whether he was ever baptised.
- Whether he lived through the 48 years with Magdalena, and when he died.
- Whether the couple had children, and whether families in the valley descend from them.
