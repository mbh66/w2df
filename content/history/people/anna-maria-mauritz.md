Hanna was a Khoekhoe woman, a daughter of Kupido, whom [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]] baptised as Jonas in 1742 [MS]. After Schmidt left in 1744, she stayed at Baviaanskloof [MS]. When the missionaries returned in 1792 she cooked for them, and on 19 July 1793 she became the first person baptised at the reopened mission, with the name Anna Maria [MS]. She later married a man named Mauritz [MS]. Through the mission's registers, the old families of Genadendal trace their roots to her [MS].

## Her life

Hanna appears, with her father Kibido (Kupido), on a list of Schmidt's congregation made in 1740 [MS]. [[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says that after Schmidt left in 1744 she stayed at Baviaanskloof, because she believed that if everyone left, no new teachers would be sent [MS]. It says she asked [[history/people/magdalena|Magdalena]], who had settled at the Sergeants River, to read to the people regularly from the New Testament Schmidt had given her [MS].

When [[history/people/missionaries-of-1792|the missionaries of 1792]] arrived, Hanna became their cook [MS]. The missionaries' diaries record the first baptism at the reopened mission, on 19 July 1793: Anna Maria, one of the daughters of Kupido [MS]. Balie's history names four daughters of Jonas among the first residents of the reopened mission: Anna Mauritz, Eva Witbooi, Magdalena Fredericks, and Rachel Pitt [MS].

## Her descendants

From 1744 the missionaries kept registers of baptisms, marriages, schooling, and deaths [MS]. Using them, the Mission Museum linked the old Genadendal families to one another for the century from 1744 to 1844 [MS]. Balie's history says every early Genadendal family can trace its roots to Anna Maria Mauritz, and through her to Kupido [MS]. In 2005 the museum began a genealogy project on this, called "The Children of Jonas" [MS]. Families at Elim, Mamre, and Enon also find their ancestors in the Genadendal registers, because people from Genadendal helped to found those stations [MS].
