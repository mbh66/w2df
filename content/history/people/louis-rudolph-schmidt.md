> [!warning] Died in living memory
> Louis Rudolph Schmidt died in 1963, and people who knew him are still alive. This page draws on one published history. It is open to correction by those who remember him, and the moderators will review it as responses come in.

Louis Rudolph Schmidt was a Moravian missionary who served at Genadendal for about forty years from 1901 [MS]. He had trained as a locksmith [MS]. In the valley he is remembered as its land surveyor, doctor, and dentist [MS]. His maps of the mission villages were used by state departments long after he made them [MS]. [[cultural/education/lr-schmidt-primary|L.R. Schmidt Primary School]] is named after him [MS].

## His life

Schmidt was born at Elim, where his parents were missionaries [MS]. At nine he was sent with other German missionaries' children to school at Klein-Welka in Saxony [MS]. He trained as a locksmith and art metalworker at Niesky, and worked in Wiesbaden, Stuttgart, Zürich, and Bern [MS]. In 1895 he came back to South Africa and worked at the Phoenix Ironworks in Port Elizabeth [MS]. During a typhus epidemic in 1896 he fell critically ill, and on his sickbed committed his life to Christ and vowed to preach [MS]. He entered the mission's service in 1901 and was sent to Genadendal [MS]. The church's list of missionaries gives his years there as 1902 to 1942, and marks him as superintendent [MS].

On 13 October 1902 he married Anna Helena Schmitt, a music teacher from Germany who had taught at the girls' school in Paarl [MS]. At Genadendal she taught music to the students of the training college and needlework to the girls [MS]. Their two children, Theodore and Margarethe, died young, and Anna died on 15 December 1947 [MS]. Schmidt retired to Greyton, where he died on 28 May 1963 [MS]. The *Cape Times* reported his death under the heading "Death of Schweitzer of South Africa" [MS].

## Surveyor, doctor, and dentist

At the end of the 1800s residents of Genadendal had begun to claim rights to their land [MS]. With a compass and his own paces, Schmidt mapped Genadendal, Berea, Voorstekraal, and Boschmanskloof, with every house and garden plot [MS]. [[history/genadendal-a-long-walk|Balie's history]] says his maps were so accurate that state departments were still using them in recent times [MS]. His compass is in the [[cultural/sport-heritage-arts-culture/genadendal-mission-museum|Genadendal Mission Museum]] [MS].

He had studied medicine on his own, and with no hospital or ambulance nearby, residents sent for him at any hour [MS]. He charged nothing, walked to his patients at night with a lantern, and in old age travelled by donkey cart [MS]. He treated inflammation and aching joints with compresses of white clay from Koringlandskloof, used an electromagnetic machine for rheumatism and nervous complaints, prescribed herbal and homeopathic remedies, and pulled teeth without anaesthetic [MS].

In November 1917 he was assaulted one night on his way back to his office, and the church council offered a reward for information [MS]. Residents also tell a story of a mountain fire that threatened the village, which ended in sudden rain while he prayed [MS].
