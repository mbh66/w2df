The Chainoqua were a [[history/tribes/khoekhoe|Khoekhoe]] people of the Overberg [IC]. Their land stretched from the Hottentots Holland mountains towards Cape Agulhas and inland to the Riviersonderend mountains, some 12,000 square kilometres [IC]. It took in this valley. Some of their kraals stood near present-day Genadendal and Riviersonderend [IC]. The Breede River divided them from their eastern neighbours, the [[history/tribes/hessequa|Hessequa]] [IC].

## Their land and way of life

The Chainoqua lived in four main groups of kraals [IC]. Soeswa's own group lived in the Villiersdorp valley and along the upper Riviersonderend [IC]. Another group lived between the Riviersonderend and the mountains, with smaller kraals near present-day Genadendal and Riviersonderend [IC]. Each kraal had a headman, and camps held from 20 to more than 100 *matjieshuise* [IC]. Cattle were the measure of wealth, and land was held by the group as a whole [IC].

## The Chainoqua and the Dutch

The Dutch first met the Chainoqua on 14 November 1657 [IC]. In 1661 Soeswa visited the company's commander, Jan van Riebeeck, and agreed to trade cattle [IC]. Within twenty years the Dutch often called the Chainoqua by their chief's name [IC]. From 1662 to 1713, the Chainoqua and the Hessequa were the colony's largest suppliers of cattle and sheep [IC].

In the war of 1673 to 1677, the Chainoqua sided with the Dutch against the Cochoqua, another Khoekhoe people [IC]. From 1679 to 1693, the Chainoqua leader Dorha acted as the company's go-between with the Overberg Khoekhoe [IC]. The company kept cattle posts in the Overberg to hold the animals it bartered [IC].

Trade and alliance did not protect their land. Dutch travellers found rich grazing at the Huis River, where Greyton now stands, in 1669 [IC]. By 1727 the company was farming timber and cattle between present-day Greyton and Riviersonderend [IC]. Smallpox killed many Khoekhoe in 1713 [IC]. Over the 1700s most Chainoqua land was divided into farms for colonists [IC]. Some Chainoqua leaders, among them Soeswa and later Dorha, tried to hold back the colony's spread [IC].

## In the story of Genadendal

When Georg Schmidt arrived in 1737, the people he taught were living on what had been Chainoqua land [IC]. The Moravian Church in South Africa says that [[history/people/janneke-tikkuie|Janneke Tikkuie]], the husband of [[history/people/magdalena|Magdalena]], was Chainoqua [MS]. Whether [[history/people/africo|Africo]]'s clan was Chainoqua is not recorded [TBV]. Independent Chainoqua kraals were still recorded near Genadendal as late as 1803 [IC].
