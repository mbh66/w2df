The San are the hunter-gatherer peoples of southern Africa [IC]. Their ancestors have lived in the region for tens of thousands of years, and theirs is among the oldest continuous cultures in the world [IC]. In the south-western Cape, including this valley, the Khoekhoe called them the [[history/tribes/soaqua|Soaqua]] [IC]. Their rock art survives in the Riviersonderend mountains [IC].

## The names

The San have no single word for themselves in their own languages [IC]. "San" comes from a Khoekhoe word, and was taken up by scholars from the 1970s [IC]. "Bushmen", from the Dutch *Bosjesmans*, is older, and many South Africans now consider it offensive, though some San use it for themselves [IC]. In 2003 San representatives said they would rather be called by the names of their own nations [IC]. This wiki uses "San" as the general name, and "Soaqua" for the San of this part of the Cape, because that is the name the records use.

## Way of life

The San lived by hunting and gathering, moving through the year as water, plants, and game allowed [IC]. They did not have chiefs or clans. Decisions were made by the elders of each band [IC]. Their rock paintings, found across southern Africa, are one of the great bodies of art in the world [IC].

When Khoekhoe herders arrived in the Cape with sheep and cattle about 2,000 years ago, the San of this area moved higher into the mountains, where they lived on plants and small animals such as tortoises and dassies [IC]. See [[history/tribes/soaqua|Soaqua]].

## The colonial frontier

When the Dutch East India Company arrived at the Cape in 1652, there were perhaps 300,000 San in southern Africa [TBV]. As colonists spread inland in the 1700s, the San lost access to land, water, game, and plants [IC]. From the 1770s to the late 1790s, violence against the San became general along the colony's frontier. Armed commandos of colonists killed San people in large numbers, and in 1777 the company officially backed a policy of wiping them out [IC]. Khoekhoe men took part in some of these commandos, and sometimes led them [IC]. In 1686 the Chainoqua leader [[history/people/dorha|Dorha]] attacked San hunters after colonists had been killed [TBV].

## Today

Most people who live as San communities today are in Botswana and Namibia [IC]. In South Africa, many people of San descent live within coloured communities, including in the Western Cape [TBV]. The [[genadendal-mission-museum|Genadendal Mission Museum]] runs school programmes on San and Khoekhoe history [MS].

## What is still open

- What San families in the valley know of their ancestors, and what names they use.
- The age and meaning of the rock art in the Riviersonderend mountains. The fourteen cave sites there have not been formally surveyed [TBV].
