*Genadendal, The Valley of Grace: A Long Walk Through the History of the First Mission Station in South Africa* is a history of Genadendal by Dr Isaac Balie, a former director and curator of the [[cultural/sport-heritage-arts-culture/genadendal-mission-museum|Genadendal Mission Museum]] [MS]. He first published it himself in September 2020, and AuthorHouse published the current edition in July 2023 [MS]. The book tells the story of the mission village from 1737 to the present, through its church, schools, buildings, crafts, people, and museum collections [MS]. This page describes the book, sets out the major events it records in one table with page numbers, lists where it differs from other sources, and explains how this wiki uses it.

> [!info] What the letters in square brackets mean
> Facts on this page are followed by a tag that shows where they come from.
>
> - **[IC] Independently Corroborated.** Confirmed by at least two sources that do not depend on each other, or by an official record. On this page, a row is tagged [IC] only when the book agrees with a fact that [[history/history-of-the-valley|History of the Valley]] already supports from independent sources.
> - **[MS] Mission-Sourced.** Comes from the book alone. The book draws on the mission's archives and the museum's collections, and on the author's own interviews and memories, so it is one side's record. It has not been checked against sources that do not depend on it.
> - **[TBV] To Be Verified.** The book's date differs from other sources used on this wiki, or the book gives more than one date and no other source settles it. See *Where the book differs* below.

> [!note] Whose voice this is
> The author says the book follows no strict chronology, and that it is told in the first person and reflects his own memoirs (p. 8). He grew up in Genadendal and led its museum, and he writes as a Christian: most pages close with a Bible verse or a reflection. The book records the valley's first people as "Khoi". Where it quotes older writers, they sometimes use colonial names now considered offensive, and this page does not repeat them.

## Where the book differs

The book sometimes gives different dates for the same event on different pages, and some of its dates differ from other sources used on this wiki. Each is set out here without choosing between them.

- **The day Schmidt settled at Baviaanskloof.** The book gives 25 April 1738 (p. 9) [MS]. [[history/history-of-the-valley|History of the Valley]] gives 23 April 1738 [IC], and the valley's tercentenary is set for 23 April 2038.
- **The first baptisms.** The book gives 1739 for Magdalena's baptism (p. 132), 1740 for Schmidt's first baptisms in the river (p. 32), 12 April 1742 for Kibido's (p. 175), and 1743 for the first five candidates (p. 131) [MS]. This wiki gives 1742 [IC].
- **The first church.** The book says permission came in 1798 and dates the Middle House chapel to 1798 (pp. 36, 63), but its map of the square dates the first church to 1795 to 1796 (p. 64) [MS]. This wiki gives 1796 [IC].
- **The guest house.** The book says Jean Baptiste Nigrini and his wife opened it in 1798 (p. 24), and elsewhere that Jean Pierre Nigrini built it in 1800 (p. 179) [MS].
- **The population of 1,234.** The book gives this figure for 1806, with 228 homes (p. 22), and for 1799 (p. 38) [MS]. The missionaries' diaries count 1,093 people in 217 houses at the end of 1804. See [[history/the-valley-through-time|The Valley Through TIME]].
- **The watermill.** The book gives 1797 (p. 91), 1799 (p. 63), and about 1796 (p. 199) [MS]. This wiki gives 1797 [IC].
- **The library.** The book dates the lending library to 1825 (p. 56) [MS]. This wiki gives 1823 [IC].
- **The first policeman.** The book gives 1854 (p. 115) and 1849 (pp. 115, 145) for Johannes Ruiter's appointment [MS].
- **The land grant.** The book dates the grant to 15 February 1858 (p. 15) and to 1858 (p. 115), and once says Governor Grey granted the land in 1856 (p. 159) [MS]. This wiki gives 1858 [MS].
- **The size of the mission land.** The book gives 5,644 morgen and 289 square roods (p. 15) [MS]. At about 0.857 hectares to the morgen, that is roughly 4,830 hectares, a little above the range of 4,515 to 4,800 hectares given on [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39]]. The conversion is this page's own.
- **The end of mission rule.** The book says a Board of Management replaced the Council of Overseers in 1924 (p. 159), shows the last Council of Overseers in 1925 (p. 154), and dates government management from 1926 (p. 17) [MS]. This wiki gives 1924 [IC].
- **The college's closure.** The book gives 1926 (pp. 16, 17, 53) and 1927 (pp. 48, 49) [MS]. This wiki gives 1926 [IC].
- **The monuments on Church Square.** The book counts 20 declared monuments in one place (p. 7) and 25 in others (pp. 63, 200) [MS].
- **The printing works.** The book says the presses moved to Cape Town in 2002 and in 2003 (p. 110) [MS]. This wiki gives 2003 for the closure [MS].
- **The bridge builder's name.** The book spells it Beinbrecht (pp. 66, 139). This wiki earlier spelled it Beinbach, and now follows the book.

## How this wiki uses the book

- Facts taken from the book are tagged [MS]. A fact moves to [IC] only when a source that does not depend on the book or on the mission's records confirms it.
- Citations give the page number in the AuthorHouse edition of 2023.
- Where the book and another source disagree, this wiki gives both and says which source gives which.
- The book has a bibliography, but no footnotes, so a single fact usually cannot be traced to the source it came from.
- The author's reflections on faith, and his views on present-day matters, are his own. This wiki reports them only as his, and only where they bear on the valley's history.

## What is still open

- Which source gives 25 April 1738 for Schmidt's settlement, and whether Schmidt's own diary, which the book lists in its bibliography, settles the day.
- Whether the Supreme Court case over land at the end of the 1800s (p. 115) and the Magerman case of 1924 (p. 159) are one dispute or two.
- Records of the 500 Genadendal soldiers of 1915 to 1918, and of the 19 who died, in military archives.
- An independent record of the loft movement and of Rev Daniel Wessels's house arrest, and what families remember of them.
- The Khoi and slave ancestry the book traces through the mission registers, told by the families themselves.
