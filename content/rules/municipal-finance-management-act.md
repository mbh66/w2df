The Local Government: Municipal Finance Management Act 56 of 2003 (MFMA) is the national law that governs municipal money [IC]. It came into force on 1 July 2004 [IC]. It sets out how a municipality budgets, spends, buys, reports and manages its assets, and what happens when its finances fail [IC]. National Treasury and the provincial treasuries oversee it, and the municipal manager, as accounting officer, carries it out [IC]. For the valley it matters because it governs every rand Theewaterskloof spends in the ward, including on [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]], and because it frames the province's rescue of the municipality's finances.

## What it does

**Capital projects (section 19).** Section 19(1) says a municipality "may spend money on a capital project only if" four things hold [IC]:

- the money has been set aside in the capital budget;
- the council has approved the project, "including the total cost";
- section 33 has been followed, where it applies (contracts that run beyond three years);
- the sources of funding "are available and have not been committed for other purposes".

Before it approves a project, the council must consider "the future operational costs and revenue on the project, including municipal tax and tariff implications" (section 19(2)) [IC]. A draft amendment Bill, published for comment on 21 October 2026, would change the opening words of section 19(1) to "may commit or spend money on a capital project only if" [IC].

**Selling or giving away assets (section 14).** A municipality may not permanently dispose of a capital asset "needed to provide the minimum level of basic municipal services" (section 14(1)) [IC]. It may dispose of any other asset only after the council, in a meeting open to the public, has decided the asset is not needed for those services and has considered its fair market value and "the economic and community value to be received in exchange" (section 14(2)) [IC]. The [[rules/municipal-asset-transfer-regulations|Municipal Asset Transfer Regulations]] set out the detailed steps.

**Looking after assets (section 63).** The accounting officer is responsible for "the assets of the municipality, including the safeguarding and the maintenance of those assets" [IC].

**Payments to outside organisations (section 67).** Before a municipality transfers money to a body outside government, it must meet the conditions in section 67 [IC]. National Treasury's guidance on this section is explained on [[rules/mfma-circular-131|MFMA Circular 131]].

**Reporting.** The accounting officer must report on the budget "by no later than 10 working days after the end of each month" (section 71(1)) [IC]. The mayor must report to the council on the budget "within 30 days of the end of each quarter" (section 52(d)) [IC]. These reports carry the debt figures by town that the register uses.

**Financial crisis (Chapter 13).** Section 139 provides for the province to intervene when a municipality, "as a result of a crisis in its financial affairs", is in serious or persistent breach of its obligations [IC]. A financial recovery plan "must be aimed at securing the municipality's ability to meets its obligations" (section 142(1)) [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**The rule on investing on Farm 39.** The municipality's Spatial Development Framework says that "applicable municipal legislation" does not allow it to invest in infrastructure on land it does not own [MS]. The four conditions in section 19 say nothing about who owns the land. On the Act's own words, ownership is not one of the tests. Accounting guidance asks a municipality to have control of the land, or rights such as a servitude from the owner, before it builds there [IC]. Read together, the question for Farm 39 becomes whose consent or rights the municipality needs: the Minister's, as trustee of the land outside the townships, or its own, where [[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]] section 2(1) vested township land in the municipality [IC]. Which law the municipality has in mind has not been confirmed.

**Following the money.** Every capital project in Genadendal, Greyton or the other settlements must appear in the adopted capital budget before money is spent. A resident who wants to know whether a project for a Farm 39 settlement is real can look for it in the budget's capital tables (Table SA36) and then in the monthly and quarterly reports.

**Land the municipality holds.** Where the municipality owns land on Farm 39, such as the township land that vested in it under TRANCRAA or erven registered in its name, section 14 and the asset transfer regulations would govern any sale, transfer or long lease. A transfer of that land to another body would need a council decision in a meeting open to the public.

**The recovery plan.** The province's intervention in Theewaterskloof, and the [[rules/theewaterskloof-financial-recovery-plan|Financial Recovery Plan]], run under Chapter 13 of the Act and section 139 of the Constitution. The plan limits what the municipality can spend across every town, so it bears on capital projects in the valley whatever the land question.
