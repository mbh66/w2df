The Theewaterskloof Financial Recovery Plan is the plan that the Western Cape Government approved on 7 October 2025 to rescue the municipality's finances [IC]. It was drawn up after the province intervened in the municipality under section 139 of the [[rules/constitution|Constitution]] [IC]. While the plan is in force, the municipal council and the Municipal Manager still run the municipality, but every budget, tariff, and spending decision has to fit inside it [IC]. For the valley, the plan matters twice over. It decides how much money is available for services in Ward 2, and it names Genadendal's unpaid accounts as about a fifth of everything owed to the municipality [IC].

## How the municipality came to be under the plan

| Date | Step |
| --- | --- |
| 16 September 2024 | The MEC for Local Government gives the municipality formal notice of serious financial problems [IC] |
| 6 November 2024 | The Provincial Executive approves an intervention under section 139(5) of the Constitution [IC] |
| 11 November 2024 | The province announces the intervention [IC] |
| 17 December 2024 | The Minister of Finance approves National Treasury's recovery service drawing up the plan [IC] |
| March to June 2025 | Workshops with the municipality, its creditors, organised labour, and support agencies [IC] |
| 13 August 2025 | The draft plan is published for 14 days of public comment [IC] |
| 7 October 2025 | The Western Cape Government approves the plan [IC] |
| May 2026 | The provincial Cabinet adopts the first progress report. The municipality is still in Phase 1 [IC] |

**Why the intervention was mandatory.** The law sets thresholds at which a province must step in. One of them is unpaid bills: creditors more than 30 days overdue reached 7.3% of the municipality's expenditure budget, where the threshold is 2% [IC]. Once a threshold is crossed, the province has no choice about intervening, and only National Treasury's recovery service may draw up the plan [IC].

**No administrator.** In November 2024 the MEC for Finance said the intervention would not, at that stage, involve appointing an administrator [TBV]. One newspaper reported the opposite in the same week [TBV]. Later official documents describe oversight by Provincial Treasury and the Department of Local Government, and name no administrator [IC].

## What it does

### Three phases

- **Phase 1, Rescue.** Planned for up to eight months from approval [IC]. The municipality must adopt a funded budget, watch its cash daily, cut costs, collect more of what it is owed, keep grant money apart from its own, and pay creditors on time [IC].
- **Phase 2, Stabilisation.** Planned for months 9 to 24, or longer [IC]. Governance and staffing reform, repair of infrastructure, an updated valuation roll, and complete billing [IC].
- **Phase 3, Sustainability.** Tariffs that cover the real cost of services, and a long-term financial strategy [IC]. Before the intervention ends there must be "reasonable assurance" that the earlier measures will last [IC].

### Targets

| Target | 2025/26 | 2026/27 | 2027/28 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Share of rates and service charges collected | 85% | 88% | 90% |
| Spending on contractors, as a share of operating spending | Under 8% | Under 7% | Under 7% |
| Cash held, in months of spending | 0.5 | 0.5 | 1 |
| Payment plans with creditors kept | 100% | 100% | 100% |

For 2025/26 the plan set increases of 7% on rates, 12.7% on electricity, and 5% each on water, sanitation, and refuse [IC]. From 2026/27, increases follow inflation plus growth [IC].

### The money

- **Owed to the municipality.** R384 million in unpaid consumer accounts at 28 February 2025 [IC].
- **Owed by the municipality.** R91 million to creditors at 31 January 2025, of which just over R65 million was owed to Eskom [IC]. The municipality was taking an average of 132 days to pay its bills, where the norm is 30 [IC].
- **Eskom.** The municipality was not eligible for the national Eskom debt relief programme because it "missed the opportunity to apply" [IC]. It signed a new repayment agreement with Eskom in March 2026 [IC].
- **The outlook.** Even if every saving in the plan is made, the plan projects cash shortfalls of about R320 million in 2025/26, R302 million in 2026/27, and R178 million in 2027/28, with a surplus "by around 2030" [IC]. The MEC for Finance said the finances "could be stabilised within approximately five years" if the plan is followed with discipline [IC].

### Staff and controls

- The municipality has 636 staff, and staff costs take 38% of its operating spending [IC].
- The plan orders it to investigate and reverse illegal promotions and post upgrades, check qualifications, physically verify staff, control overtime, and fill the post of supply chain manager [IC].
- When the plan was written, the municipality had had no Bid Adjudication Committee since October 2024 and no Disciplinary Board since June 2017, and only 9 of its 14 ward committees were working [IC].

### Services

- 43% of water put into the system is lost or not billed [IC].
- 43% of roads are in poor or very poor condition, and 52% of the vehicle fleet is past its useful life [IC].
- Spending on repairs and maintenance is far below the national norm of 8% of the value of assets [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**Genadendal is at the centre of the numbers.** The plan records that Genadendal "equates to around 20 per cent of the total outstanding" debt owed to the municipality [IC]. The municipality's own quarterly reports show the share rising since the plan was written [IC]:

| Date | Genadendal (Eskom area) | Share of all debtors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 31 October 2025 | R97,783,258 | 23.0% |
| 31 January 2026 | R103,966,817 | 23.2% |
| 31 March 2026 | R104,916,378 | 23.8% |
| 31 May 2026 | R108,357,195 | 23.7% |

In May 2026 Greyton's municipal-supply area owed R7,981,241 (1.75%), and the Eskom-supplied areas of the whole municipality, where the municipality cannot block electricity meters, owed 86% of all debt [IC]. The reports do not break Genadendal's debt down by rates, water, sanitation and refuse [IC]. The plan also records that many residents of Genadendal hold that the land does not belong to the municipality [IC]. That view rests on the unfinished transfer of [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] under [[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]], and in 2019 the High Court allowed two Genadendal residents to defend the municipality's claims for debt on those grounds [IC]. The Forum reads this as the largest single question in the plan that no one has yet set out to settle. See [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39]].

**Electricity works differently on each side.** Greyton buys its electricity from the municipality, while Genadendal is supplied directly by Eskom [IC]. The municipality can cut off electricity to collect debt in Greyton. In Genadendal it has no such lever, because it does not sell the electricity there.

**The plan binds the next council.** Local government elections are due on 4 November 2026 [TBV]. Whoever forms the next council must pass budgets inside the plan's limits, so the plan sets the money available for Ward 2's needs in the [[rules/theewaterskloof-integrated-development-plan|Integrated Development Plan]] for several years.

**Residents had one formal say.** The plan's public participation was the 14-day comment window in August 2025 [IC]. It gives no standing seat to residents or civil society. It does require the municipality to adopt a communication plan, and it records that "the Theewaterskloof community expects municipal empowerment programs to include them" [IC]. The quarterly progress reports are published, so residents can follow progress [IC]. The Forum's idea for taking part is on [[concepts/citizen-led-municipal-recovery|Citizen-Led Recovery for Ward 2]].

**Heritage and tourism.** The plan links Genadendal's heritage to tourism in Greyton as an opportunity the municipality has not used [IC].
