The Western Cape Land Use Planning Act, known as LUPA, is the province's own planning law [IC]. It works alongside the national [[rules/spatial-planning-and-land-use-management-act|Spatial Planning and Land Use Management Act]] (SPLUMA): municipalities decide land use applications, and the Province keeps a say in matters of provincial interest [IC]. It replaced the Land Use Planning Ordinance of 1985 [IC]. One of its sections speaks directly to the valley: it requires land that was once governed by the Rural Areas Act, as [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] was, to be brought into a zoning scheme [IC].

## What it does

- **Municipalities decide.** Municipalities are responsible for land use planning in their areas, including zoning schemes and land use applications (section 2) [IC].
- **The Province plans and comments.** The Province adopts the Provincial Spatial Development Framework (section 3) [IC]. A municipality must ask the Province for comment before deciding certain applications, including those outside the planned edge of urban growth and rezonings of agricultural or conservation land; the Province has 60 days to reply (section 45) [IC].
- **Old schemes stay until replaced.** Town planning schemes made under the 1985 Ordinance remain in force until amended under the municipality's by-laws (section 33) [IC].
- **Land with no scheme must be brought in.** On the Act's commencement, a municipality must incorporate land that was not regulated by a zoning or town planning scheme, "including land that was regulated by the Rural Areas Act", into its zoning scheme (section 34) [IC]. Until it does, the municipality must give the land a zoning or a deemed zoning that takes into account its lawful use and any conditions in its title deed [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**On Farm 39.** Genadendal, Bereaville, Voorstekraal, and Boschmanskloof stand on land governed by the Rural Areas Act 9 of 1987 until [[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]] replaced it in 1998 [IC]. Section 34 therefore applies to them. The Forum reads it as meaning that every piece of Farm 39 land should by now carry a zoning or a deemed zoning in Theewaterskloof's [[rules/zoning-scheme-by-law|Zoning Scheme By-law]], based on how it was lawfully used. That matters for anyone who wants to build, trade, or farm there, because what is allowed depends on the zoning. Whether the municipality has done this, and with what zoning, is not yet known to this site [TBV]. A zoning does not change who owns the land: the Minister still holds the Remainder in trust.

**Farm and conservation land.** A rezoning of agricultural land anywhere in the valley, such as on the farms around Greyton or along the Riviersonderend, goes to the Province for comment before the municipality decides [IC]. So does development outside the urban edge drawn in the [[rules/theewaterskloof-spatial-development-framework|Spatial Development Framework]] [IC].

**In Greyton and Heuwelkroon.** Land here was already zoned under the older schemes, and those zonings carried over [TBV].
