The Theewaterskloof Integrated Waste Management Plan (IWMP) is the municipality's five-year plan for waste [IC]. Zutari (Pty) Ltd prepared it for the municipality, and the final report, Revision 4, was submitted on 19 October 2023 after the provincial environment department's comments were addressed [IC]. It covers all 14 wards and every town, Genadendal and Greyton included [IC]. For the valley it matters because it records the state of the two landfills that serve Ward 2, one of which stands on [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] land outside the townships.

## What it does

**Why it exists.** The Waste Act requires each municipality to produce an IWMP and submit it to the provincial MEC for approval, and the approved plan must be included in the municipality's IDP (p. 21) [IC]. The plan is to be reviewed every five years (p. 105) [IC]. The draft was advertised for comment in a local newspaper, on the municipal website and on Facebook, and the plan records that no comments had been received (p. 105) [IC].

**What it covers.** It reviews the waste services the municipality delivers, the needs it has not met, and the goals and projects for the next five years (p. 3) [IC]. It finds that the collection fleet is very old, that more staff and training are needed at the waste sites, and that landfills must meet their licence conditions (p. 3) [IC].

**Waste growth.** It projects waste for every town with growth of 3 per cent a year and about 0.5 kg per person a day (pp. 33 to 34) [IC]. For Genadendal it gives 3.3 tons a day in 2021, rising to 4.4 by 2030; for Greyton, 1.6 rising to 2.0 (p. 34) [IC].

**Collection.** It cites the IDP as saying that all formal housing receives refuse removal, and says each informal settlement has its own challenges (p. 50) [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**The Genadendal landfill.** The plan records the following [IC]:

- The site stands on the "Remainder of Farm no. 39, Genadendal" (p. 43).
- It is about 15,000 m², shares access control with the treatment works, keeps no records and has only informal recycling (p. 43).
- A licence to decommission it was issued on 3 December 2019, for ten years (p. 36). The plan gives it two licence numbers: 19/2/5/4/E4/5/WL0180/19 (pp. 36, 43) and 19/2/5/4/E4/9/WL0079/18 (p. 43).
- An external audit in 2021/22 scored its compliance with the licence at 41.98 per cent, down from 49.16 per cent (p. 43).
- It has 3,897 m³ of space left (p. 49).
- The capital plan gives R1,000,000 for its rehabilitation in 2026/27 (p. 52).

**The Greyton landfill.** It stands on the Remainder of Erf 595, Greyton, has a licence to decommission issued on 7 September 2018, and scored 62.30 per cent in the same audit (pp. 36, 44 to 45) [IC]. It has 49,198 m³ of space left (p. 49) [IC]. The capital plan gives R3,000,000 in 2023/24, R4,561,254 in 2024/25, and R3,000,000 in each of 2025/26 and 2026/27 for its rehabilitation (p. 52) [IC].

**Two budgets for the same work.** The 2023 IDP budgets the Genadendal landfill's rehabilitation at R18,585,398.51 for 2025 to 2030 [MS]. The plan gives R1,000,000 in 2026/27. Which figure the municipality is working to is not clear.

**Land the municipality does not own.** The municipality says it may not invest in infrastructure on land it does not own [MS]. Its landfill on the Remainder of Farm 39 is a long-running municipal service on such land, and rehabilitating it will be capital spending there. How the municipality reconciles the two has not been asked. See [[civic/residents-association/farm-39-and-the-valley-economy|Is the Wait for Farm 39 Holding Back the Valley's Economy?]].

**A transfer station.** The draft Spatial Development Framework says a transfer station is needed for the area once the Genadendal landfill closes [MS]. The plan's capital table lists none for Genadendal (p. 52) [IC]. Waste from Bereaville and Voorstekraal already goes to the Greyton drop-off [MS].

**Recycling in the valley.** The plan names Greyton Transition Town's swop shops, and says over 700 kg of recyclable waste is collected each week from the Genadendal swop shop alone (p. 64) [IC].

**Growth.** The plan's 3 per cent a year for Genadendal sits beside the draft Spatial Development Framework's 0.5 per cent for the same town [MS]. The two are made for different purposes, and the plan gives no reason for its rate.
