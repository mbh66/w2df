MFMA Circular 131 is guidance that National Treasury issued to every municipality on 31 March 2025 [IC]. It explains section 67 of the Municipal Finance Management Act, which sets the rules for a municipality giving money to a non-profit or any other body outside government [IC]. Its core message is that a municipality may not use such a payment to buy services. If it wants something done, it must go through its supply chain (tender) process [IC]. For the valley, this matters because the way local tourism offices, festivals and ward projects have been funded in many towns no longer passes.

## What it does

**Two kinds of payment.** The circular draws a line between two ways a municipality can pay a non-profit [IC]:

- **A transfer** (often called a grant-in-aid) supports the organisation's own work. The municipality gets nothing back. The circular says a transfer must be "truly unrequited" [IC].
- **A purchase** is any payment where the municipality receives goods or services in return. A purchase must follow section 217 of the Constitution, Chapter 11 of the MFMA and the municipality's supply chain rules, even when the seller is a non-profit [IC].

The test is simple: if the municipality gets something in exchange, it cannot pay under section 67 [IC]. The circular relies on a 2023 Constitutional Court judgment, *Agribee Beef Fund v Eastern Cape Development Agency*, which held that procurement rules apply even where the state is not the direct beneficiary [IC].

**Examples it gives.** An annexure lists payments that must be procured and not transferred [IC]:

- **Local tourism.** Promoting local tourism is a municipal function under Schedule 4B of the Constitution. Outsourcing it to a non-profit through a section 67 payment, without a procurement process, is irregular [IC].
- **Festivals and events.** If a non-profit runs a municipal event, such as a cultural or heritage celebration, it must be procured, and so must the artists who perform [IC].
- **Ward projects.** A non-profit appointed to carry out a ward project must be procured [IC]. A donation to an organisation whose main purpose is social still goes through section 67 [IC].
- **Sports sponsorships.** A sponsorship that requires the team to advertise the municipality is a purchase of a service [IC].

**What a municipality must have for transfers.** A council-approved section 67 policy, aligned with the Integrated Development Plan, covering eligibility, how to apply, selection, reporting and conflicts of interest [IC]. A recipient must normally submit audited financial statements [IC]. Organisations serving poor households may submit statements under the Nonprofit Organisations Act instead, under conditions set out in section 67(4) [IC]. Every transfer needs a written funding agreement [IC]. The names of the organisations and the amounts must appear in the municipality's draft budget, so residents can comment [IC].

**A yearly cycle.** The circular sets out a calendar [IC]:

| Month | Step |
| --- | --- |
| July | Review last year's transfers |
| August | Decide whether the policy needs changes |
| September | Advertise a call for proposals, with workshop dates |
| October | Workshop for organisations; review the proposals |
| November | Propose amounts for the next three years |
| March | Set provisional amounts for each organisation |
| April and May | Publish the list with the draft budget; public comment |
| June | Finalise the list and sign agreements. Payments can start in July |

**Consequences.** A payment for services made under section 67 is irregular expenditure, which the council's Municipal Public Accounts Committee must investigate [IC]. Officials may face financial misconduct proceedings [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**For tourism offices.** A municipality that pays a local tourism office to run visitor information or marketing is buying a service, so it must now tender for it. The organisation that has always done the work may still win the contract, but it has to compete, and the contract ends on a set date. In the Garden Route, the district municipality says some local tourism offices that relied on municipal grants could not operate fully, and that Calitzdorp Tourism closed for part of the 2025 to 2026 season [MS]. Whether Theewaterskloof pays any tourism office in Ward 2, and on what basis, is not known to this site [TBV]. The [[economic/tourism/greyton-tourism-bureau|Greyton Tourism Bureau]] is the office most likely to be affected if it does. The DevForum noted at its [[civic/governance/minutes/2026-09-16-inaugural-meeting|inaugural meeting]] that the ward has no working tourism office [MS].

**For the provincial guidelines.** The Western Cape Department of Economic Development and Tourism names Circular 131 as one reason it is drafting new guidelines on how regional and local tourism is managed and funded [MS]. The DevForum's convenor will attend the [[civic/governance/between-meetings/2026-11-23-overberg-tourism-guidelines-workshop|Overberg workshop on the guidelines]] on 23 November 2026.

**For festivals and heritage events.** A heritage celebration or festival that the municipality pays a valley organisation to run has to be procured. An event an organisation runs as its own project, with municipal support and nothing owed back, can still be a transfer. The difference lies in who owns the event and what the municipality gets.

**For organisations applying for support.** A valley non-profit seeking a grant-in-aid from Theewaterskloof would need to apply in the municipality's yearly cycle, which, if it follows the circular, opens around September. It would need audited financial statements, or statements under the Nonprofit Organisations Act if it serves poor households, and its name would appear in the draft budget for public comment.

**For proposals tabled at the DevForum.** Any proposal that expects the municipality to pay a valley organisation to deliver something, such as guide training or running a trail, should be planned as a contract the organisation tenders for. A proposal for the municipality to support an organisation's own work should be planned around the section 67 calendar.
