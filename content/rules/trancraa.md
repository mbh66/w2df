The Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act, known as TRANCRAA, is the national law that decides how the land of 23 former mission and "coloured rural" areas passes from the state to the people who live there [IC]. [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]], the land on which [[villages/genadendal|Genadendal]], [[villages/bereaville|Bereaville]], [[villages/voorstekraal|Voorstekraal]], and [[villages/boschmanskloof|Boschmanskloof]] stand, is one of those areas, and [[villages/madiba-park|Madiba Park]] stands on it too [IC]. The Act came into force on 2 November 1998 [IC]. For Farm 39, the transfer it provides for has not yet happened [IC]. This page explains the Act. The story of the process on Farm 39 since 2002 is told on [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]].

## What it does

- **It replaced an apartheid-era law.** Before 1998 the 23 areas were governed by the Rural Areas Act 9 of 1987, under which the state held most of the land in trust for the residents [IC]. TRANCRAA repealed that Act [IC].
- **It splits the land in two.** Trust land inside a proclaimed township passes to the municipality [IC]. The rest of the trust land, called the Remainder (mostly commonage and farmland), may be transferred to an entity: the municipality, a [[rules/communal-property-associations-act|Communal Property Association]] (CPA), or another body the Minister approves [IC].
- **It sets out who counts as a resident.** A resident is anyone who ordinarily lived in the area on 2 November 1998, or who was liable for rates or service charges there on that date [IC].
- **It sets a process with deadlines.** The Minister starts an 18-month transitional period by notice in the Government Gazette, which can be extended once, by six months [IC]. The municipality, or failing it a committee elected by residents, must say how the entity will be chosen, and then recommend one to the Minister in writing [IC].
- **It protects residents if the municipality receives the land.** Under section 4, a municipality that receives the Remainder must give residents a fair say and reasonable preference for access to the land, may not sell or encumber it without the consent of a majority of residents at a public meeting, and must account to residents for money it earns from the land [IC].
- **It leaves the land with the Minister if nothing is decided.** Trust land not transferred when the transitional period ends stays with the Minister, who may keep holding it in trust and transfer it later under the Act's principles [IC].
- **It is a land reform law.** In 2014 the Land Claims Court held that TRANCRAA deals with land reform and does not deal with land restitution, which is a separate legal process [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**On Farm 39.** The transitional period for Farm 39 began on 8 July 2009 and ended in January 2011 without a transfer, so the land stayed with the Minister [IC]. In 2026 it is still held in trust, and no receiving entity has been recognised by the government [MS]. A CPA is being set up to receive the land [MS]. Until a transfer happens, title to the Remainder stays with the Minister. That is the root of most of the other rules on this site that land differently on Farm 39: zoning, services, and who may decide about the land.

**For services.** Theewaterskloof Municipality's spatial plan says the municipality may not invest in infrastructure on land it does not own [IC]. In Madiba Park the municipality has said it cannot provide full services because the land is not the municipality's [MS]. Read together with this Act, that means the services question on Farm 39 waits on the transfer. See [[civic/residents-association/madiba-park-land-and-services|Madiba Park: Land Status and Basic Services]].

**For municipal accounts.** In 2019 the Western Cape High Court let two Genadendal residents defend claims for unpaid municipal accounts on the ground that their land falls under TRANCRAA and that the Minister holds title [IC]. The court left that question for trial [IC].

**For anyone planning something on the land.** An initiative that wants to use Farm 39 land (a garden, a nursery, a building, a trail) is dealing with land the Minister holds in trust for residents. The Forum reads this as meaning that use of the land needs the agreement of the trustee and the support of the residents' elected committee, and that a short-term arrangement should not be mistaken for a right to the land. The [[civic/governance/genadendal-transformasie-komitee|Transformasie Komitee]] reviews every page on this site that touches Farm 39.

**In Greyton and Heuwelkroon.** The Act does not apply. That land was sold as freehold from 1854 and has never been mission trust land [IC]. See [[villages/next-door-different-rules|Next Door, Different Rules]].
