The Tourism Act is the national law for tourism in South Africa [IC]. Among other things, it sets up a system for registering tourist guides, run by a Provincial Registrar in each province [IC]. For the valley this is its most practical rule: a person may not work as a tourist guide unless they are registered (section 57) [IC]. Ideas for the valley that would train and employ heritage and nature guides, such as the [[concepts/tourism-growth-fund-application|Valley of Grace Heritage Trail]], depend on it.

## What it does

- **Provincial Registrars.** The MEC responsible for tourism in each province must appoint a Provincial Registrar of Tourist Guides, who keeps the register, handles complaints, and disciplines guides (section 49) [IC].
- **Registration.** A person who wants to be a tourist guide applies to a Provincial Registrar and pays a fee [IC]. The applicant must show the competence the Act requires (section 51) [IC]. Registration lasts three years and must be renewed (section 50) [IC].
- **A code of conduct.** Registered guides must follow a code of conduct and ethics (section 52) [IC].
- **No guiding without registration.** Section 57 prohibits working as a tourist guide without being registered [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**For heritage and nature guides.** A resident who leads paying visitors through Genadendal's Church Square, along Greyton's leiwater, or into the nature reserve is acting as a tourist guide, and needs to be registered. Training that does not lead to registration leaves guides unable to work lawfully on their own account. The Forum reads this as a reason for any guide training tabled at the Forum, including the Heritage Trail's, to be planned around a recognised qualification from the start.

**For tourism businesses.** Businesses that sell guided experiences, such as those marketed through the [[economic/tourism/adventure-hub-greyton|Adventure Hub Greyton]], need their guides to be registered [TBV]. A host who welcomes guests to a home or a farm is not a guide simply by doing so; the line is drawn by what the guide does and is paid for [TBV].

**Learning pathways.** The rule links tourism to education: a guide qualification is a skills pathway that the [[priorities/education-skills|Education and Skills]] priority could support.
