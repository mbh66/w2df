The Municipal Asset Transfer Regulations are rules that the Minister of Finance made under the [[rules/municipal-finance-management-act|Municipal Finance Management Act]] [IC]. They were published as Government Notice R878 in Government Gazette 31346 on 22 August 2008 [IC], and took effect on 1 September 2008, according to National Treasury's guide [IC]. They set out how a municipality may transfer or dispose of its capital assets, such as land and buildings, and how it may give others the right to use, control or manage them [IC]. For the valley they matter because the municipality holds some land on Farm 39, and any sale, transfer or lease of it would follow these rules.

## What it does

**What it covers.** The regulations apply when a municipality or municipal entity transfers or disposes of a capital asset, or grants someone a right to use, control or manage one [IC]. Transfers can go to private parties, other municipalities, municipal entities or national and provincial departments [IC]. Rights to use include leases, lettings and hiring out [IC]. Short hires, such as booking a hall for a weekend, fall outside them [IC].

**High-value assets.** An asset counts as high value if it is worth "in excess of R50 million or 1 per cent of the total value" of the municipality's assets, whichever is lower, or a lower amount the council sets [IC]. If a municipality's transfers or disposals in one financial year together exceed five per cent of the value of its assets, public participation applies to each of them, whatever its size [IC].

**Public participation.** Only the council can authorise the public participation process [IC]. The accounting officer must publish the proposal and invite comment "at least 60 days before the council will meet" [IC]. The council then decides in a meeting open to the public, and must weigh, among other things, whether the asset is needed to provide a minimum basic service [IC].

**Rights that stay on the land.** Assets transferred or disposed of stay subject to all existing encumbrances, rights and servitudes (regulation 48) [IC].

**What it does not cover.** National Treasury's guide discusses municipal land leased to others. It does not deal with a municipality investing on land it does not own [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**Municipal land on Farm 39.** The Ward 2 Forum's 2017 reading of the land records shows land in Voorstekraal, including 21 residential erven, as owned by the municipality, and Erf 1353 in Genadendal (47.8 hectares) as municipal land, though another source says Erf 1353 passed to a private owner [MS]. [[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]] section 2(1) vested trust land in the townships in the municipality [IC]. If the municipality were to transfer any of this land, for example to a body chosen under TRANCRAA, these regulations and section 14 of the Act would govern how it does so, including the council decision in an open meeting.

**Garden plots and leases.** Lawyers for the transformation consultants concluded in 2010 that users of the garden plots (*tuin persele*) hold use rights that can be registered, while ownership stays with the municipality [MS]. Where the municipality lets its own land on Farm 39, a lease longer than a short hire falls within these regulations.

**Investing on land it does not own.** The municipality's rule that it may not invest on land it does not own does not come from these regulations. They deal with what a municipality does with its own assets, and say nothing about building on someone else's land.
