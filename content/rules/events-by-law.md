Theewaterskloof Municipality's By-law relating to the Holding of Events says when a gathering needs the municipality's approval, and what an organiser must arrange [IC]. It was published in the Provincial Gazette on 7 April 2017 [IC]. An event under the by-law is any gathering of more than 30 members of the public, on public or private property, whatever its purpose: sport, entertainment, culture, religion, business, charity, or politics [IC]. In a valley that hosts markets, mountain-bike races, festivals, and fundraisers, it reaches many of the initiatives on this site.

## What it does

- **Approval first.** No one may hold an event without first applying in writing and receiving the municipality's approval [IC].
- **Thirty days' notice.** The application must reach the municipality at least 30 calendar days before the event; larger or more complex events may need longer [IC].
- **What the application covers.** Details of the event and expected attendance, the venue agreement, security arrangements, an emergency medical plan, parking and traffic arrangements, proof of insurance, comments from neighbours, and, where needed, a noise impact assessment [IC].
- **The police as well.** The organiser must separately apply to the South African Police Service for the event's risk category under the Safety at Sports and Recreational Events Act 2 of 2010 [IC].
- **Exemptions.** The by-law exempts some gatherings, including funerals, weddings at established venues, and events held at a venue built for that purpose and used in the normal way, such as a school or a place of worship [TBV].
- **Penalties.** Holding an event without approval is an offence, punishable by a fine or imprisonment, and the court may order the organiser to pay the municipality's costs [IC].

## What it means in the valley

**Regular markets.** The [[economic/business/greyton-village-market|Greyton Village Market]] gathers stallholders and visitors every Saturday morning [MS]. Whether a weekly market is treated as an event, or as trading under the municipality's Informal Trading By-law, is a question for the municipality [TBV].

**Races and rides.** Mountain-bike events on Greyton's trails, such as those the [[cultural/sport-heritage-arts-culture/greyton-mtb-club|Greyton MTB Club]] supports, bring in more than 30 people and usually use public roads or land, so they fall under the by-law and under the national events safety law.

**Fundraisers and festivals.** Fundraisers by clubs and schools, church festivals, and 2038 tercentenary events can fall under the by-law. Events at a church or school used in the normal way may be exempt [TBV]. The 30-day notice is the detail most often missed, and the Forum suggests organisers in the valley plan back from it.

**Forum meetings.** A meeting of the Forum at a hall used for meetings is the kind of gathering the exemptions appear to cover [TBV].
