The Theewaterskloof Spatial Development Framework (SDF) is the municipality's plan for where growth, housing, business, farming, and conservation should go [IC]. It is a statutory policy document that guides decisions, and the municipality says it "does not grant land use rights, nor does it take land use rights away" [IC]. The current SDF was finalised in November 2019 and is known as SDF 2020 [IC]. It has a chapter for each town, including one for Greyton and one for Genadendal, Bereaville, and Voorstekraal [IC]. For the valley it matters because planning decisions must take it into account, and because its Genadendal chapter records the limit that land ownership places on municipal investment.

## What it does

- **It guides decisions, and grants nothing.** Decisions on land use applications must take the SDF into account, but it confers no rights [IC].
- **It draws urban edges.** Each town gets an edge beyond which urban development is not planned [IC]. Development outside it goes to the Province for comment under the [[rules/western-cape-land-use-planning-act|Western Cape Land Use Planning Act]] [IC].
- **It covers every town.** Part 2 has chapters on Caledon, Grabouw, Villiersdorp, Botrivier, Riviersonderend, Greyton, Genadendal (with Bereaville and Voorstekraal), and Tesselaarsdal [IC].

## What it says about the valley

**Greyton (Chapter 11)** [IC]:

- Tourism is Greyton's main economic sector.
- Development is to stay within the urban edge, and low-density housing is preferred to keep Greyton's village character. About 9.5 hectares of new land is expected to be needed over ten years.
- An urban conservation area covers the original town layout of 1854, and many buildings are named as worthy of a heritage register.
- Heuwelkroon is planned as an area for business and for medium- to high-density housing, to integrate the town.
- Boschmanskloof is described as spatially isolated, to be linked to Greyton by roads and public spaces, with two heritage precincts of Cape vernacular cottages.
- The Greyton Nature Reserve is to be expanded through the formal statutory process, and ecological corridors along the Gobos and Bosjemanskloof rivers protected.
- Madiba Park is not mentioned.

**Genadendal, Bereaville, and Voorstekraal (Chapter 12)** [IC]:

- The chapter records that the transformation process under TRANCRAA "has not been resolved and is still in process".
- It states: "In terms of applicable municipal legislation, TWKM is not allowed to invest in infrastructure, where the Municipality is not the owner of the land."
- Little new land is needed; growth is planned through denser use of existing plots, in eight planning areas.
- Five heritage precincts are identified in Genadendal, including the Moravian mission werf, and four each in Bereaville and Voorstekraal, with the aim to "maintain and conserve the cultural significance and historic sense of place".
- It proposes working with Greyton on tourism, light industry along the main roads, and a Farmer Production Support Unit.
- It suggests extending the north-south environmental corridors between the settlements for conservation.

## What it means in the valley

**Two chapters for one valley.** The SDF plans Greyton and Genadendal in separate chapters, as separate towns. The Forum's approach treats the valley as one area [MS]. Both chapters propose tourism links between the two, which is the main point where the plan already looks at the valley as a whole.

**The ownership limit.** The Genadendal chapter's statement that the municipality may not invest on land it does not own is the plan's own explanation of why services and infrastructure on [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] wait on the land transfer under [[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]]. It is a statement of the municipality's position in an official document.

**Heritage and conservation.** The precincts and the conservation area identified in the SDF are proposals for protection. Binding protection comes through the [[rules/zoning-scheme-by-law|Zoning Scheme By-law]] (an overlay zone) or the [[rules/national-heritage-resources-act|National Heritage Resources Act]].

**Using the SDF.** A proposal that fits the SDF has an easier path through the planning system than one that does not. Ideas tabled at the Forum that need land can be checked against the relevant chapter before they are taken further.
