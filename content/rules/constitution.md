The Constitution of the Republic of South Africa is the country's highest law [IC]. Any law or conduct that conflicts with it is invalid [IC]. It was adopted in 1996 and came into force on 4 February 1997 [IC]. Every other page in [[rules/index|The Rules the Valley Lives By]] describes a law, by-law, or plan that must fit within it. This page picks out the sections that bear most on life in the valley.

## What it does

The sections below are the ones that come up most in the valley [IC]:

| Section | What it says, in short |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | The Constitution is the supreme law. Law or conduct inconsistent with it is invalid. |
| 24 | Everyone has the right to an environment that is not harmful to their health or wellbeing, and to have it protected for present and future generations. |
| 25(5) | The state must take reasonable steps to help people gain access to land on an equitable basis. |
| 25(6) | A person or community whose tenure of land is legally insecure because of past racially discriminatory laws or practices is entitled, as an Act of Parliament provides, to tenure that is legally secure or to comparable redress. |
| 26 | Everyone has the right to have access to adequate housing. No one may be evicted from their home without a court order made after considering all the relevant circumstances. |
| 27 | Everyone has the right to have access to sufficient food and water. |
| 32 | Everyone has the right of access to information held by the state. |
| 33 | Everyone has the right to administrative action that is lawful, reasonable, and procedurally fair, and to written reasons when their rights are adversely affected. |
| 152 | The objects of local government include democratic and accountable government, services provided sustainably, social and economic development, a safe and healthy environment, and the involvement of communities in local government. |
| 156 | A municipality may make and administer by-laws on the matters it is responsible for. |

## What it means in the valley

**On Farm 39.** Section 25(6) is the constitutional root of the laws that deal with the mission land. The land of [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] was held under laws made for "coloured rural areas", and [[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]] is one of the Acts of Parliament through which the promise of secure tenure is meant to be kept. The [[rules/interim-protection-of-informal-land-rights-act|Interim Protection of Informal Land Rights Act]] protects rights while that promise is still being kept.

**In Madiba Park.** Sections 26 and 27 give everyone a right of access to housing and water, and the state must take reasonable steps within its available resources to realise them [IC]. Section 26(3) means that no one may be evicted from their home without a court order [IC]. These rights apply whoever owns the land. How they apply where the municipality says it cannot serve land it does not own is the kind of question the courts decide; this site does not.

**For the environment.** Section 24 protects the environment "for the benefit of present and future generations" [IC]. It sits behind the river, catchment, and invasive-clearing work listed on this site, and behind the environmental laws that regulate it.

**For dealing with the municipality.** Section 152 says local government must encourage the involvement of communities [IC]. The [[rules/municipal-systems-act|Municipal Systems Act]] and the [[rules/municipal-structures-act|Municipal Structures Act]] turn that into duties: the IDP, ward committees, and public comment on by-laws and land use applications. Section 33 means a resident affected by a municipal decision can ask for written reasons [IC]. Section 32 lets residents request records held by the state, through the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 [IC].
