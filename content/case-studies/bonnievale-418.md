> [!summary] In brief
> - **The town did it together.** In 2016, Bonnievale residents formed a trust to build a second high school [MS]. Local people gave the land, residents bought bricks, and builders and other professionals gave their work free [MS].
> - **One school with two kinds of training.** The Education Department agreed to pay 40% of the costs if the town found the land and the other 60% [MS]. The school that opened in 2018 teaches technical and farming subjects up to matric, and runs a four-year skills course in trades and food production for about 40% of its learners [MS].
> - **A plan for the town's young people.** The trust went on to start Bonnievale 418, which works with 12 schools and helps school leavers find study, training or work [MS]. A Bonnievale 418 staff member has called the town's story "a blueprint for rural development" [MS].
> - **Many kinds of people working together.** Parents and employers were asked what learners should learn [MS]. The Education Department, farmers, foundations and residents each paid a share [MS]. IPASA, which studied the case, says the team needed people with skills in finance, vision, operations and relationships [MS].

Bonnievale 418 is a non-profit programme in Bonnievale, a town on the Breede River in Langeberg Municipality, about 120km ENE of the valley [IC]. Its legal body is the JGE Funding Trust, which trades as Bonnievale 418 [MS]. Bonnievale residents set up the trust in 2016 to raise money for a new no-fee high school, Jakes Gerwel Technical School (JGT), which opened in January 2018 [IC]. The trust started Bonnievale 418 in 2020 [MS]. It works with schools, helps school leavers find study, training or work, and shares its model with other towns [MS]. It aims for "zero youth unemployment in Bonnievale by 2030" [MS]. People from Bonnievale 418 have presented at [[cultural/education/emil-weder-secondary|Emil Weder Secondary]] and the [[cultural/sport-heritage-arts-culture/genadendal-mission-museum|Genadendal Mission Museum]] [MS].

## What it does

Bonnievale 418 describes its work in a few streams [MS] :

- **Education.** It runs an Education Council of Bonnievale's school principals, set up in 2021, and says it works with 12 schools in greater Bonnievale [MS]. It also brings in reading and psychosocial support [MS].
- **Career Hub.** Career guidance, help with study and bursary applications, mentoring, and links to employers [MS].
- **Year 13.** Support in the year after matric, including places in YearBeyond [MS]. YearBeyond is a Western Cape Government programme that gives unemployed young people ten months of paid work experience [IC].
- **Work readiness.** A course that it says had 329 graduates and 313 job placements from July 2021 to May 2023 [MS].
- **Sharing.** Help for other towns that want to build a school of skills [MS].

It counts a young person as NEET when they are aged 15 to 24 and not in education, employment or training [MS]. Its staff collect school leavers' details and check on each group at set times, up to 15 months after school [MS]. Its public documents do not describe how it asks for consent or protects this data.

Its director is Curren Kühn [MS]. Its annual reports name Wilhelm de Wet, Augusta Brandt, Philip Jonker and Curren Kühn as trustees [MS]. It had 15 staff in 2021/22 and 23 in 2025 [MS]. Its main funders, according to a 2023 case study, are the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation, the Rupert Education Foundation, Lactalis and SA3 [MS]. Its annual reports do not give its income or spending [MS].

## How Bonnievale built its school

- **Before 2016.** Bonnievale had one public high school, which sources say had about 350 places [MS]. About 200 learners a year were bussed to high schools in other towns [MS]. Bonnievale had 9,092 people in Census 2011 [IC]. In Langeberg as a whole, 46.8% of learners dropped out before finishing Grade 12 in 2016 [IC], and agriculture gave 28.1% of jobs [IC]. No official figure for young people who were NEET in Bonnievale was found.
- **2016.** Residents, led by Weltevrede wine estate owner Philip Jonker, began planning a new school [MS]. They say the Education Department agreed to pay 40% of the building and the running costs if the town found the land and the other 60% [MS]. The founders set up the JGE Funding Trust [MS].
- **The land.** Sources say the Jonker family gave 12 hectares of vineyard [MS]. A 2023 case study says one trustee gave half the land and another paid for the other half [MS]. Who owns the land and buildings today is not known.
- **The money.** Sources give different totals, from R85 million to R154 million [MS]. Figures for the Education Department's share include R34 million (about 2019) and R54 million (2023) [MS]. Residents bought bricks at R2.50 each, and builders and other professionals gave their work free [MS]. The department's own figure has not been found.
- **June to December 2017.** The first buildings went up in about six months [IC].
- **January 2018.** JGT opened with Grades 8 and 9 [IC]. It added Grade 10 in 2019 and Grade 11 in 2020 [IC].
- **The rules it runs under.** JGT is a collaboration school, a public no-fee school run by the Western Cape Education Department with a non-profit partner [IC]. Its approved governing body has 16 seats: eight for the trust, three for parents, one each for a teacher and another staff member, two for learners, and one for the principal [IC]. That gives the trust half the seats. The governing body employs the staff, and the department pays most salaries [MS].
- **2021.** The first matric class wrote the National Senior Certificate [IC].
- **What it teaches.** A technical stream (electrical, mechanical and civil technology), an agricultural and consumer studies stream, and a four-year School of Skills course in trades and food production [MS]. About 40% of learners are in the School of Skills stream [MS]. Parents and employers were asked what learners should learn [MS]. No source shows apprenticeships of the Swiss kind, where learners train inside a company [MS].
- **Today.** JGT had 773 learners in 2025 and is a quintile 2, no-fee school [IC]. A schools website that cites national results gives matric pass rates of 90.9% (2023), 90.7% (2024) and 81.0% (2025) [TBV]. The school's own figures are 81% (2021), 85% (2022) and 90.6% (2023) for its academic stream [MS].

## What it reports, and what others report

Bonnievale 418's own figures, for the young people who finished school in each year [MS] :

| Matric year | What Bonnievale 418 reports |
| --- | --- |
| 2020 | 80 school leavers. 92% found study, training or work within 15 months. In May 2022, 81% were placed and about 19% were NEET |
| 2021 | 200 school leavers. 58% found an opening within six months. In April and May 2022, 50% were placed and 29% were NEET |
| 2022 | 51% placed three months after school |
| Recent years | 65% of school leavers placed in study or work (written by a Bonnievale 418 staff member, 2025) |

Its website shows "500+ learners impacted" [MS]. 

No independent source counts placements, NEETs or youth unemployment in Bonnievale. The Provincial Treasury gives figures for all of Langeberg Municipality: unemployment of 15.2% in 2022, and 65.7% of learners staying from Grade 10 to Grade 12, the lowest in the Cape Winelands district [IC]. These cover a different area and different people, so they cannot be set against Bonnievale 418's figures.

Bonnievale 418's own reports name setbacks: many jobs it found were temporary, so young people moved back into NEET; it lost contact with some school leavers; and many matric learners failed a Grade 5 maths test needed for its programmes [MS]. Its 2022/23 report says its 2030 goal is "still a far way out" [MS]. When members of the Provincial Parliament visited JGT in 2019, the school reported over-aged learners, learning gaps from primary school, and discipline problems [IC].

Equal Education and the teachers' union SADTU challenged the collaboration schools model in court, arguing that giving a partner half the governing body seats weakens parents' say [IC]. The High Court dismissed the case in July 2023, and the Supreme Court of Appeal dismissed the appeal in September 2026 [IC]. Neither ruling named JGT. No criticism aimed at JGT or Bonnievale 418 itself was found.

## Sharing the model

- **Struisbaai.** A skills facility at Struisbaai Primary School was launched on 9 May 2022 [IC]. It cost R36 million: the Education Department paid 40%, and the Struisbaai SOS Trust raised the rest [IC]. The department's statements do not mention Bonnievale. Bonnievale 418 calls it the first school of skills built on the JGT model, and says JGT trustees supported it [MS].
- **Ashton.** Bonnievale 418 reports work on Ashton Vocational Academy, due to open in 2025 [MS]. A local newspaper describes Ashton Vocational Academy as a project of the Education Department and the Ashton non-profit Ashton in Action, and does not mention Bonnievale 418 [MS].
- **Other towns.** Bonnievale 418 names Fisantekraal, Vredendal, Botrivier, De Dooms, George, Koue Bokkeveld, Ceres and towns in other provinces as places it is talking to [MS]. Botrivier is in Theewaterskloof Municipality [IC]. Its target is "10 schools in 10 communities in 10 years" [MS].
- **What it says a town needs.** It publishes no checklist. Its *Blueprint of Hope* says that "from the outset, parents and business owners were consulted about what they wanted their children to learn" [MS]. A staff member told the Financial Mail in 2022: "You can't allow it to be dominated by one big business or investor" [MS]. Its relations manager has said: "In the end, it worked because there was really the whole town involved" [MS].
