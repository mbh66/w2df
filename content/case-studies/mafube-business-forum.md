The Mafube Business Forum (MBF) is a group of business owners and farmers in Mafube Local Municipality, in the Fezile Dabi District of the Free State [IC]. It was formed in August 2019 to deal with service failures in Frankfort and the neighbouring towns [IC]. It says it is a registered non-profit [MS]. In 2019 it drafted a *Mafube Stakeholders Compact* with government, but government never signed it [IC]. In 2022 the MBF and AfriForum won a High Court order that made the province take over the running of the municipality's finances under a recovery plan [IC]. The MBF supports a private company, Rural Maintenance, which has run the municipality's electricity under a 25-year agreement since 2011 [IC]. Farmers pay a voluntary levy of R18 per hectare a year for roads and security [MS]. The case reached the valley through a June 2026 news report and a BizNews interview, and no one in the valley has been in contact with the MBF [MS].

## What it does

The High Court describes the MBF as "a non-profit organization" formed in August 2019 to address conditions in Frankfort and the neighbouring towns [IC]. Reports say it speaks for farmers, builders, engineers and local businesses [MS]. It calls itself "a properly constituted, apolitical organisation" with "a constitution and a code of ethics" [MS]. Its work found in the record falls into three kinds:

- court cases to make the municipality and province meet their legal duties [IC];
- offers to work with the municipality, written up in its Stakeholders Compact [IC];
- public support for Rural Maintenance, the private company that runs the electricity [MS].

The farmers' levy for roads and security is run by farmers' bodies, and reports differ on the MBF's part in it [MS].

## How it started and how it is run

### The municipality before the MBF

- **Place.** Mafube covers about 3,971 km² [IC]. Its towns are Frankfort, Villiers, Tweeling and Cornelia [IC]. Official records name the townships Namahadi (Frankfort) and Qalabotjha (Villiers) [IC]. Mafahlaneng (Tweeling) and Ntswanatsatsi (Cornelia) were not confirmed in the sources read [TBV].
- **People.** The municipality had 57,876 people in 2011 and 61,150 in 2022 [IC]. It had 16,896 households in 2022 [IC]. In March 2025 SALGA reported 3,680 registered indigent households, about 22% [IC]. No official unemployment figure was found.
- **Economy.** Farming drives the area: sheep, cattle, maize and sunflower around Frankfort, and maize, sunflower, wheat, sorghum, meat and dairy through Villiers [IC].
- **Sewage.** Courts ordered sewage repairs at Namahadi in 2004 and 2008, and made further orders in later years [IC]. A Rand Water report for 2019 to 2020 found that all of Mafube's towns added to pollution of the Vaal River [IC].
- **Eskom.** On 20 January 2017 the High Court let a business group pay Eskom directly to stop the power being cut [IC]. The court records call this group the MBF, but the MBF was only formed in 2019, so it may have been an earlier forum [TBV]. On 17 August 2020 Eskom obtained an order to attach R61,153,440.27 from the municipality [IC].
- **Audits.** The court recorded "repeated adverse findings" by the Auditor-General since 2014 [IC]. The Auditor-General gave a disclaimer of opinion on 1 July 2020 [IC]. A disclaimer means the auditors could not find enough records to form a view. Audit outcomes for 2010 to 2019 were not found.
- **First intervention.** A Section 139 intervention lets the province step in when a municipality cannot meet its duties. The Free State Executive Council invoked Section 139(1)(b) in Mafube on 15 March 2017 [IC]. It ended this intervention with effect from 30 March 2021 [IC]. National Treasury drafted a recovery plan on 3 November 2017, which the court later found was not carried out [IC].

### From 2019 to today

- **February to March 2019.** A Villiers resident began a clean-up, and farmers and businesses gave equipment and fuel [IC]. A public meeting in Villiers on 14 March 2019 agreed on further action [IC].
- **August 2019.** The MBF was formed [IC]. Michael Haupt's note says the Frankfort and Villiers business forums merged to form it [MS]. Court records say the Villiers Business Forum joined later [IC].
- **26 September 2019.** The MBF, the Villiers Business Forum and the District Agricultural Union met Free State CoGTA and the administrator [IC].
- **October and November 2019.** The MBF held two workshops with the municipality, Fezile Dabi District Municipality, national and provincial CoGTA, and national and provincial Treasury [IC]. In December 2019 the *Mafube Stakeholders' Compact* was drafted and sent to all of them [IC].
- **24 January 2020.** This was set as the day to sign the Compact [IC]. The MBF told Parliament that "all communication from all state participants unexpectedly ceased without reasons given" [IC]. The court later found that attempts to conclude the Compact "came to nothing" [IC].
- **2021.** The MBF registered as a non-profit company, number 2021/531386/08, by its own account [MS]. The number has the CIPC format for a non-profit company; this is our inference [TBV]. On 4 May 2021 the MBF and AfriForum went to court [IC].
- **28 April 2022.** Acting Judge Van Rhyn ordered the province to intervene under Section 139(4) and (5) of the Constitution [IC]. This is a mandatory intervention with a binding recovery plan. The court also ordered the municipality to stop sewage polluting the Vaal River [IC]. It refused the MBF's request to help run the municipality for the time being [IC].
- **June 2022.** The province invoked Section 139(5)(a) and (c) [IC]. Sources give three dates: 11 May, 1 June and 10 June 2022 [IC].
- **August to September 2022.** The MBF agreed with the province's representative to help collect municipal charges and to urge residents to pay again, from 1 September 2022 [IC]. It met the Premier and the MEC for CoGTA on 30 August 2022, and says communication then stopped [IC].
- **15 June 2023.** National Treasury's draft recovery plan reached the MBF [IC]. The MBF says it was left out of the consultation [IC].
- **5 September 2024.** The council resolved to carry out the recovery plan [IC].
- **18 March 2025.** The MBF spoke to a committee of the National Council of Provinces visiting Mafube [IC]. It said the Compact "remain[s] relevant and the basis for a Public Private Community Partnership (PPCP)" [IC]. It said the Municipal Manager had asked to meet it, and proposed a "My Town" project "aligned with CoGTA's 'Small Town Regeneration Strategy'" [IC].
- **25 March 2025.** A full bench of the High Court ruled on an appeal by the MBF, AfriForum and a resident [IC]. It declared that the province and municipality had not complied with the 2022 order [IC]. It ordered them "to finalise and implement a Financial Recovery Plan without delay", to stop the pollution of the Vaal and Wilge rivers, and to report to the court within 60 days [IC]. It did not find the Premier in contempt [IC].

- **2026.** The MBF's website and Facebook page are still active [MS]. A Mafube Investment and Jobs Summit was held in Frankfort on 26 October 2026 [MS].

### How decisions are made

No MBF constitution, membership rules, meeting schedule or spending report was found. Who sits at its table, and how it decides what to support, is not on record.

### The Compact

The Compact is called *Dawning of a New Day: The Mafube Stakeholders' Compact* [IC]. Version 2.5, dated 20 January 2020, is on the MBF's website [MS]. The court described it as "a joint venture between Government and the Mafube business community" [IC]. In 2022 the MBF asked the court to let it take on these roles under the Compact for a time [IC]:

- revenue raising and debt collection;
- running the water and sewage plants through its experts;
- public communications;
- expert help on municipal infrastructure;
- an oversight committee;
- help with the recovery plan;
- skills training for municipal staff.

The court did not grant these roles [IC]. No council resolution adopting the Compact was found. In April 2025 the parliamentary committee noted "failure to implement the Stakeholders' Compact agreed to in 2019" [IC].

### Small Town Regeneration

SALGA has run a Small Town Regeneration programme, with yearly conferences [TBV]. A CoGTA strategy under the same name is reported from 2021 [TBV]. Which document the MBF means, and what it says about partnerships, was not confirmed.

### The money

- **The levy.** Farmers pay a voluntary R18 per hectare a year [MS]. Reports say about 90% pay and that it raises about R7 million a year [MS]. Landbouweekblad reported that the District Agricultural Union collects it and pays 92% to seven farmers' associations for roads and security [MS]. Daily Maverick reported in 2020 that farmers paid R1.50 per hectare a month to the MBF [MS]. No accounts were found. No source shows how much municipal debt the levy has paid.
- **Electricity.** The municipality's royalties from the electricity agreement were R3,490,025 in 2023/24 and R4,625,820 in 2024/25 [IC].
- **Grants and business contributions.** None published.

## The electricity agreement

External service delivery means a municipality hands a service to an outside body, under sections 76 to 81 of the [[rules/municipal-systems-act|Municipal Systems Act]]. A NERSA licence is the permit the energy regulator gives to distribute electricity.

The agreement itself is not public. What the record shows:

- **Parties and date.** Rural Maintenance (Pty) Ltd and its subsidiary Rural Maintenance Free State (Pty) Ltd signed a 25-year contract with Mafube Local Municipality in 2011 to run its electricity supply [IC]. The subsidiary trades as Rural Free State [IC]. Rural Maintenance has also given the year as 2012 [MS].
- **The company.** A copy of CIPC data on a business website lists Rural Maintenance (M1994000212, registered 1994 in Paarl) with Ilze Bosch as director and Julius Chris Bosch as company secretary [TBV]. Chris Bosch speaks for the company as its CEO [MS]. Rural Maintenance had a 25-year contract with Maluti-a-Phofung from September 2013, and ended it in March 2014, citing "political interference" [MS].
- **Legal steps.** No council resolution, no notice under section 33 of the Municipal Finance Management Act and no views from National Treasury or the provincial treasury were found. Section 33 requires these for contracts longer than three years. Moneyweb reported that NERSA "has no record of having approved the agency agreement" [MS].
- **Licence and assets.** Reports say the municipality keeps the distribution licence and owns the network [MS]. Who owns it after 25 years was not found.
- **Billing.** Rural Free State bills customers, collects payment and sells prepaid electricity [MS]. It says its tariffs stay below the NERSA maximum [MS]. How free basic electricity reaches indigent households was not found.
- **What the municipality receives.** Royalties are worked out per kilowatt-hour sold, after a 12.5% allowance for losses [MS]. The municipality recorded R3,490,025 in 2023/24 and R4,625,820 in 2024/25 [IC]. It agreed with Rural Freestate to offset R29,888,790.71 of royalties against what it owed the company [IC]. Moneyweb reported totals of R29 million (March 2025) and more than R23 million (June 2025) [MS]. Moneyweb reported that Treasury's plan "questions the limited royalties of R2.5 million per year paid to Mafube" [MS].
- **Eskom.** Rural Maintenance pays Eskom's bulk account [MS]. Moneyweb reported R837 million paid at one time and R1.1 billion in 2025 [MS]. Chris Bosch said the company stopped paying for the municipality's own use because the municipality did not pay it [MS]. At 30 June 2025 the municipality owed Eskom R49,627,784 (2024: R49,012,371) and Rural Freestate R45,526,092 (2024: R51,009,438) [IC]. It has applied to have Eskom debt written off under National Treasury Circular 124 [IC].
- **Losses.** Chris Bosch told Moneyweb: "when we took over the electricity losses were at 50%" [MS]. Moneyweb reported that Treasury's plan puts "the real losses" at 5.5% [MS]. Michael Haupt's note says "under 6 percent" [MS]. The municipality's 2024/25 accounts list "high levels of distribution losses" among the reasons for doubt about its future [IC]. These figures may measure different things and cannot be compared.
- **Network work.** In 2024/25 the municipality reported a new 42 km medium-voltage line from Villiers to Cornelia, and 417 new household connections in Tweeling extension [IC]. Who paid for and built these was not stated.
- **Solar.** Daily Maverick reported that FF Renewables, owned by 21 shareholders "including Rural Maintenance", began supplying Frankfort at the end of 2021 [MS]. Moneyweb reported four solar farms with "a joint generation capacity of 3.7 megawatts (MW), which will increase to 4.2MW" [MS]. Smaller plants are reported at Villiers (980 kVA) and Tweeling (480 kVA) [MS]. The cost was not published.
- **Eskom case.** In 2023 Rural Maintenance and the MBF went to court against Eskom over the solar plants [IC]. On 20 April 2023 the court dismissed the urgent case because Rural Maintenance "could not prove Mafube municipality had authorised it to proceed with litigation" [IC].
- **The recovery plan.** National Treasury's recovery plan of August 2023 proposes reassessing the legality of the contract and possibly cancelling it [IC]. Rural Maintenance asked the court to review the plan [IC]. On 30 December 2024 Judge Daniso ordered Treasury to hand over the record and reasons for the plan [IC]. The main review had not been decided in the sources found.

### The municipality's own record

- The Auditor-General gave qualified opinions for 2021/22, 2022/23, 2023/24 and 2024/25 [IC]. A qualified opinion means the accounts are mostly reliable, with some parts the auditors could not confirm.
- At 30 June 2025 the municipality owed the Department of Water and Sanitation R588,440,433 (2024: R521,543,050) [IC]. Its trade and other payables were about R1.23 billion [IC].
- In March 2024 the South African Human Rights Commission found the municipality in breach of water and environmental law over sewage spills into the Wilge and Vaal rivers [IC].
- In March 2025 SALGA reported revenue collection of 20% and said "the municipality is not collecting revenue on electricity" [IC].
- Courts ordered payments to workers' pension funds of R16,249,515.75 in 2016 and R14,723,639.52 in 2025 [IC].

### Who takes part

The MBF's deputy chair is a township resident [MS]. The MBF publishes in English and Afrikaans [MS]. Representatives of the Namahadi Business Forum and the Zithande Women Organisation spoke separately to Parliament in 2025 [IC]. How residents who are not business owners or farmers take part in the MBF was not found.

### Views recorded by Parliament, March 2025

Each party below spoke to the National Council of Provinces committee on 18 March 2025 [IC].

- **The Mayor** said the collapse came from several causes, including "the privatisation of electricity though the contract that was concluded with Free State Rural Maintenance in 2011" [IC].
- **SALGA** urged a review of the contract "so that the local municipality could be able to collect revenue from electricity" [IC].
- **The MBF** said the province had stopped communicating with it, that the municipality ignored 21 requests, and that it "still insist[s] on the implementation" of the Compact [IC]. It disputed the claim that it urges residents not to pay [IC].
- **SAMWU** called for a review of the contract, saying it believes the contract "was fraudulently drafted and signed" [IC]. This is the union's allegation; no court has found this.
- **IMATU** raised job security, appointments and late salaries [IC].
- **The EFF** called for Rural Maintenance to be removed as the electricity supplier [IC].
- **The DA** welcomed the intervention, and said the recovery plan "was based on inaccurate information on electricity and contract with Rural Maintenance" [IC].
- **The FF Plus** raised the finance department and revenue collection of 10% to 20% [IC].
- **The ANC** noted progress under the interventions and asked for a review of how local government is funded [IC].
- **The Namahadi Business Forum** called for the contract to end "because of the lack of benefits by the local municipality" [IC].

Outside Parliament, the MBF has said it would be "an absolute disaster" if Rural Maintenance left [MS]. In the SAHRC's findings as Parliament summarised them, the municipality blamed civil society groups for low payment [IC].

The committee recommended that the Mayor meet the business forums every quarter, and that it meet the province and Rural Maintenance "to discuss the nature of the contract, benefits and social responsibility programmes" [IC].

### Still active in 2026

The MBF published on its website and Facebook page during 2026 [MS]. A new local party, Mafube First, is contesting the November 2026 elections in Mafube [MS]. The Citizen names MBF founding member Hans Pretorius among its people [MS]. The MBF describes itself as apolitical [MS].
