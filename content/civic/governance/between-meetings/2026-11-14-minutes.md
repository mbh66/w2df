On 14 November 2026, fourteen people met in Genadendal for a site visit that followed the inaugural [[2026-10-27-places-conversation|Places Conversation]] held in Stellenbosch on 27 October 2026. Ranyaka Community Transformation convened both meetings. Michael Haupt, convenor of the Ward 2 Development Forum (W2DF), presented the valley's case for restoring land and water as the base of its economy. Johan Olivier presented Ranyaka's model of clubs, hubs, networks and a platform. The group then discussed data, local organising, investment, the valley's history and student support [MS].

## What the introductions said

- Sara Grobbelaar said Stellenbosch University could offer 30 to 40 students per year, working on food and quality of life [MS].
- Danie du Plessis works on settlements and systems planning [MS].
- Geordie Ractliffe described Sustainability Matters as a non-profit working on nature-positive work, guided by a principle of care [MS].
- Marli Hoffman said her foundation is exploring a small-town revitalisation model. She also named her work on the Emil Weder dignity centre, where a feeding scheme could run alongside space for skills development [MS].
- Laura Bergh worked with an organisation that held about 250,000 data points on 160 organisations, much of it over time. She is now between roles [MS].
- Qhamani Tshazi works on infrastructure and town planning, and researches network governance and community systems [MS].
- Michelle de Bruyn said Wild Restoration clears invasive plants in the valley and works on biodiversity restoration and local green livelihoods [MS].
- Keith Radmore described the valley's food gardens, Greyton Transition Town, the change-makers network and Rotary Greyton, which started about seven years ago. He said Rotary can offer small grants, a global network and capacity to hold a larger space, and named a possible pilot within Rotary [MS].
- Johan Olivier said Ranyaka has worked since 1994, across many scales, and wants a clear model for place-based economic development [MS].
- Stephan May said his work includes bringing companies in the corporate space together. He named the difficulty of backing holistic work when each company carries its own targets, and said many people are wary of corporates [MS].
- Marshall Rinquest spoke of reviving the Western Cape food basket and food sovereignty [MS].

## What Michael Haupt presented

Michael presented slides titled *Restoring the Valley of Grace to rebuild its economy*. The slides state the following [MS].

- Nature restoration is attracting investment worldwide, and a funding gap remains. The slides cite UNEP's 2026 *State of Finance for Nature*, the Kunming-Montreal framework, the European Nature Restoration Law and South Africa's draft biodiversity plan for 2026 to 2035.
- The slides cite a US Geological Survey study of 21 restoration case studies: $2.2 million to $3.4 million of economic output, and 13 to 32 job-years, for every $1 million invested.
- In the Western Cape, Working for Water (1995), the Greater Cape Town Water Fund (2018) and the FirstRand water bond (2026, R2.5 billion, paying investors partly on checked clearing results) already pay for catchment restoration.
- The valley holds 11,194 hectares, mostly in trust, with about 30,000 people in six towns and one settlement. Michael said this is higher than official estimates [MS].
- A Political Economy Analysis (PEA) would map the valley's assets across five capitals (human, social, natural, physical, financial), using the DFID Sustainable Livelihoods Framework and the DevForum's Challenges page as its record.
- The slides set out a ridge-to-river sequence: clear the ridge, a leopard corridor, springs and wetlands, leaky dams, farms and a co-operative, and riverbanks. The leaky-dam designs by the Silverwood Primary Class 6 were on display.
- Tourism rests on a restored landscape and a purpose-filled local population. Michael said the valley is not there yet, and named substance abuse and a lack of reasons to get up in the morning as signs of it.
- Michael named the unresolved TRANCRAA process for Farm 39 (22 years, by his count), Erf 595, and the water held in the mountain as a store.
- Funding routes named: Rotary Global Grants, ESG and CSR budgets, government funds, family offices and water outcome funds. Other performance bonds discussed included Green bonds and Social bonds.
- He described the W2DF's five priorities and its machine-readable register, where every fact is tagged by source, and the digitising of handwritten diaries.

The last slide, which wasn't presented, closed with four questions for the group:

1. What would it take to treat the valley as one investable catchment?
2. Which one or two things could be tested together in 2027?
3. Who can be included who was not at the meeting?
4. What would each organisation need to see before backing the valley?

## What Ranyaka presented

Johan Olivier presented Ranyaka's approach. The figures below are Ranyaka's statements [MS].

- Ranyaka works through a strategy it calls SWARM: many small, connected productive places, in place of one large programme. He described a target of about 50 connected places and 7,500 enterprises.
- The model has four parts. 
	- Clubs organise people, enterprises and local action around a shared local calendar. 
	- Hubs activate shared facilities. 
	- Networks connect markets, knowledge, partners and resources. 
	- A platform links them.
- Ranyaka said it has about 130 network partners and worked with R4.2 million in 11 communities.
- Johan said clubs could ask members for a small monthly contribution, and mentioned R50 a month as a possibility, with about 20,000 people taking part in clubs.
- Johan gave reasons why past efforts fall short: fragmented work, short-term funding, limited local capacity, small organisations that struggle to reach the larger system, and work in silos.
- The Places platform is in beta. Ranyaka described its intention as a mix of Facebook, WhatsApp and TikTok features: share businesses, follow them, join an action, volunteer time. It records in the background how many people take part.
- Johan said the framework needs basic rules so that places can compare and learn from one another, with room inside it for each place to work in its own way.

## What was discussed

Notes name the speaker only where the convenor recorded one.

**The platform and local data.**

- Participants asked whether the platform could take in existing knowledge and activity, such as Google Maps listings. Ranyaka gave the Kayamandi example: 16 companies appeared on the map, and the clubs found about 250 by walking the streets [MS].
- Johan said the platform can connect to other systems through APIs where they allow it [MS].
- A participant noted that the government's Thusong platforms are mostly inactive [MS].
- Keith Radmore said the valley already has a Talent Exchange System and a barter practice that could connect in [MS].
- Michael described a place-based knowledge base with an AI layer over it (this platform) [MS].
- Ranyaka compared its recognition idea (awards, vouchers, a record of volunteering, especially for youth) with the Discovery Vitality model. It noted that Vitality drew criticism as social engineering [MS].

**Organising locally.**

- Geordie Ractliffe asked whether Open Space methods, or appreciative inquiry, would show where local energy lies and what matters to people. She mentioned the Human Learning Systems work of Toby Lowe [MS].
- Johan said Ranyaka's role is to convene a platform within which things can happen [MS].
- Participants discussed stokvels. One view was that stokvels are not part of the valley's local culture, and that trust may be an issue. Laura Berg pointed to women-run stokvels in KwaZulu-Natal that held communities together during Covid [MS].
- Participants said churches matter in the valley, and that some existing clubs and forums have legal structures while others do not [MS].
- Participants noted the many initiatives already in the valley, and asked how minutes, offers and needs could be gathered into one record. Michael said the W2DF register does this [MS].

**Money and investment.**

- Participants asked how a place-based bond could work, and whether a social or green-infrastructure bond could build on the water bond. One participant asked who could anchor such a bond when the municipality is not a solid financial partner, and whether a well-organised community could do so [MS].
- A participant said other places are looking at registering a catchment as a municipal asset to raise money against it, and added that municipal asset registers are in a poor state [MS].
- Stephan May said corporate involvement could rise if the community has something at stake, such as matching funding [MS].
- Participants asked how a place-based forum could influence the allocations of social fund managers [MS].
- Laura Berg described a blockchain-based tool for showing how money flows, to build trust [MS]. We didn't catch the name of the platform.
- Participants named Rotary Global Grants and funding for process work, with DG Murray Trust given as an example of a funder of process [MS].

**Place and boundaries.**

- Danie du Plessis said a water catchment is one of the best functional levels for spatial planning, and raised the question of how a place is bounded [MS].
- Johan noted a Stellenbosch, Genadendal and Stanford learning triangle with common ground [MS].

**History, identity and healing.**

- Participants spoke of the Group Areas removals, around 1970, as a major trauma whose pain is still present, and said trauma is hard to fund [MS].
- Laura Bergh said that across about 200 organisations she saw the largest impact where healing and identity work was part of the programme: belonging, being wanted, and asking who you are and what you want to heal [MS].
- Geordie Ractliffe said this work should sit in the foundation of the whole effort, with peer coaching as one method. She raised the Khoi and San heritage of the place and said one organisation had stepped back from a piece of work on it [MS].
- A participant said the Forum feels different from a year ago, and others agreed there had been a shift [MS].

**Evidence and measurement.**

- Laura Bergh said her organisation's data work covered culture and context as well as outputs, and asked how the valley would capture the foundational layer [MS].
- Michael described the [[political-economy-of-the-valley|Political Economy Analysis]] as a way to combine the five capitals with Ranyaka's work [MS].

## What was offered

These are offers as stated at the meeting [MS].

- **Stellenbosch University**: 30 to 40 students next year, in shorter and longer pieces of work; a PhD student available; process mapping and one-page overviews to help build the evidence base; a plan to focus on two or three places over three to four years. Sara and Danie said coursework projects and second-year theses could be drawn from the valley's questions.
- **Rotary Greyton**: small project funding, access to the Rotary network and Global Grants, and a possible pilot within Rotary.
- **Ranyaka**: a place on its platform, in beta, and the club, hub and network structure.
- **Marli Hoffman**: space at the Emil Weder Dignity Centre where a feeding scheme and skills development could run [MS].

## What was agreed or planned

- Johan will make the platform available to all participants and encouraged everyone to create a profile [TBV].
- Sara and Danie will propose a research program centred on the Valley of Grace, to be announced before the end of the year [TBV].
- Stepha, Johan and Michael to form a working group of program finance. Michael will share the work he's done on bonds applicable to a [[bonnievale-418|Bonnievale418]]-type approach [TBV].
- Michael planned to send slides, a write-up and a recording to everyone within a week, and to hold a second visit in 2027 for those who could not come [MS].
- The DevForum meets on 18 November 2026 to discuss the PEA. The DevForum meets every two months [MS].
- A summit on place-based development was mentioned for March 2027 [TBV].
- The Overberg Municipality is planning a tourism event in the Overberg on 23 November [MS].
