The Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act 94 of 1998, known as TRANCRAA, covers 23 areas in four provinces where the state held land in trust for the people living there [IC]. This page records what has happened in each area between 2 November 1998, when the Act came into force, and November 2026 [IC]. It draws on Parliament's records, the Department's own reports, court rulings and published research. [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] is one of the 23 areas, and its own process is told on [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]]. What the DevForum might do with this record is a separate idea, set out on [[concepts/can-the-valley-rely-on-trancraa|Can the Valley Rely on TRANCRAA?]].

## In short

- Twenty-eight years after the Act gave each area 18 months to choose who would own its land, no transfer was found in 11 of the 23 areas [TBV], and the Department transferred none of the 11 areas it targeted in 2021/22 [IC]. In 2025 Parliament's committee found that the Department had "failed to set any targets" [IC]. In Steinkopf about 800 residents signed a petition against the deed they were given [MS], and a Madiba Park resident told GroundUp in 2024: "nothing happens, we protest and then they promise the same thing" [MS].
- Four areas have a dated official record of land being transferred: Haarlem, Mamre, Ebenhaeser (in part) and Enon/Bersheba [IC]. A Department notice is reported to record a fifth, Zoar, in 2023/24 [TBV].
- Seven more had title deeds listed for handover in August 2025, or reported then, and how much land those deeds cover has not been published [IC].
- The earliest firmly dated transfer, at Haarlem in February 2020, came more than 21 years after the Act [IC].
- In October 2024 the Department told Parliament that 603,000 of about 1.4 million hectares had been transferred [IC].
- No court ruling was found that made a Minister or a municipality act under the Act [TBV].

## How this record was put together

The record was gathered in November 2026 from Government Gazette notices, Parliament's records on its own site and on the Parliamentary Monitoring Group (PMG), the Department's annual reports and media notices, court rulings on SAFLII, and research from the University of the Western Cape. News reports fill gaps where no official record was found, and are tagged as single sources. Where sources disagree, the page gives each one. Where nothing could be found, the page says so.

Several key figures were checked against their sources a second time before this page was written. The register holds a copy of the Department's *A to Z* guide to the Act (revised July 2010), shown below under *The Department's guide*. One official document could not be opened: the Department's April 2024 notice on Zoar. Figures that depend on it are marked.

## What the Act set out

Section numbers refer to the Act as published on 2 November 1998.

- Trust land inside a township vested in the municipality on the day the Act came into force (section 2) [IC].
- The rest of the trust land, called the Remainder, "may be transferred to an entity at any time prior to the expiry of the transitional period" (section 3(1)(a)) [IC]. An entity can be a municipality, a communal property association (CPA) or another body the Minister approves (section 1) [IC].
- A resident is anyone who ordinarily lived in the area on the day the Act came into force, or was liable for rates, rent or service charges there (section 1) [IC].
- The transitional period lasts 18 months from a date the Minister sets in the Gazette (section 9(1)(a)), and may be extended once by six months (section 9(2)) [IC].
- Within three months of that date, the municipality must tell the Minister how and when it will decide which entity should receive the land (section 3(4)(a)) [IC]. If it does not, an elected committee may (section 3(4)(b)) [IC]. The notice may set a special meeting or a written survey of residents (section 3(4)(c)) [IC].
- The municipality or committee then sends the Minister a written report recommending an entity (section 3(5)) [IC]. If the Minister is satisfied, he or she "must take steps to transfer" the land (section 3(6)) [IC]. If not, the Minister must give reasons and say how a new recommendation should be made (section 3(7)) [IC].
- If residents make no recommendation within the period, the Minister "may decide" which entity receives the land (section 3(12)(b)) [IC].
- Trust land not transferred by the end of the period vests in the Minister, "who may continue to hold such land in trust and may at any time thereafter dispose of that land in accordance with the principles of this Act" (section 3(13)) [IC]. The Act sets no date by which the Minister must decide after that [IC].

### The Department's guide

The Department's *A to Z of the Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act*, revised in July 2010, sets out five phases that overlap [IC]:

1. Preparation for transformation.
2. A land rights enquiry, which lists the residents who qualify and compiles a voters' roll.
3. Land use planning.
4. A tenure management plan.
5. Choice of an entity to hold and manage the land, and its implementation.

The Ward 2 Forum's 2017 series lists six phases, with implementation as a phase of its own [MS]. The guide joins the two in its fifth phase [IC].

The guide adds steps that the Act does not set out [IC]. Each community elects a Land Development Forum to speak for it [IC]. The municipality sets up a Transformation Committee of officials: the Department, the provincial department for local government, the local and district municipalities, and the consultants [IC]. Residents choose the entity in a referendum, with the Independent Electoral Commission presiding [IC]. The guide says the Department "wishes that the process may be shortened" [IC]. In 2010 the guide also recorded that no resident in any of the 23 areas held an individual title deed for a residential plot [IC].

*Inference:* the land rights enquiry and the referendum are steps the Act does not require, so each one adds a point where a process can stall. In the guide, a Transformation Committee is a body of officials. At Genadendal the name belongs to the committee residents elected in 2004, which matches what the Act calls an "elected committee" (section 3(4)(b)).

### Notes on each area

**Western Cape**

- **Genadendal (Farm 39).** The period began on 8 July 2009 [IC]; the Ward 2 Forum dates the notice 17 July 2009 [MS]. It ended in January 2011 with no transfer [IC]. Other records give the land as 4,515.53 hectares (2019 judgment) and 4,772.97 hectares (1952 Deed of Grant) [IC]. The Department's 2010 guide gives 4,641.3832 hectares [IC]. The full record is on [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]].
- **Mamre.** The Deputy Minister told Parliament in May 2021 that Mamre was transferred in 2020/21 [IC]. The Mamre Residents Association lists a deed handover in October 2013 [MS]. The two dates conflict.
- **Haarlem.** Portions 4 and 8 of the farm Welgelegen No. 230 went to the Haarlem CPA, and the deed was handed over at the Haarlem Community Hall on 15 February 2020 [IC]. *Inference:* that date falls in the 2019/20 financial year, while the Deputy Minister counted Haarlem in 2020/21.
- **Ebenhaeser.** The Deputy Minister said the "biggest portion" was transferred in 2020/21 [IC]. The receiving entity and the hectares were not stated.
- **Kranshoek.** Bitou Municipality's land and land held by Public Works had to be released before transfer [TBV]. The area was still among the areas left to transfer in 2022/23 [TBV].
- **Zoar.** A Department notice of 21 April 2024 is reported to record three properties of 5,664.8776 hectares going to the Zoar CPA in 2023/24, with a handover set for 23 April 2024 [TBV]. The register could not open the notice. No report that the handover took place was found [TBV].
- **Slangrivier, Pniel, Saron and Suurbraak.** The Minister started their transitional periods by one notice, Gazette 40312 of 12 October 2016 [IC]. The guide had said in 2010 that Pniel's 55 hectares fall under township establishment law and that no transformation process was planned there [IC]. The register's main page records Slangrivier's transfer documents as in progress in 2021/22 [IC]. In October 2024 the provincial government handed over the last of 578 housing title deeds at Saron [IC]. *Inference:* these are township erven, which vested in the municipality in 1998, so they say nothing about Saron's Remainder. Phuhlisani NPC reports that Suurbraak's land is in Swellendam Municipality's name and that a CPA was registered in September 2025 [MS].
- **Friemersheim and Rietpoort.** No record of a transitional period or transfer was found in PMG, on gov.za, in Gazette indexes or in news reports.

**Northern Cape**

- **The six Namakwa areas.** In April 2018 the Minister told Parliament that none of Leliefontein, Pella, Concordia, Steinkopf, Komaggas and Richtersveld had been transferred [IC]. Leliefontein had chosen the municipality [IC]. Pella, Concordia and Steinkopf had chosen CPAs that were "not yet established" [IC]. At Richtersveld a "CPA conflict" was "hindering" transfer [IC]. Komaggas had chosen no entity, and its community had refused the services of the service provider appointed to help it [IC]. The Department's guide had recorded in July 2010 that Leliefontein's land was held by Kamiesberg Municipality and that the other five areas had CPAs as their legal entities [IC]. *Inference:* the 2010 guide and the 2018 reply do not agree on whether the CPAs of Pella, Concordia and Steinkopf existed.
- **The August 2025 handover.** A government notice listed title deed handovers on 28 and 29 August 2025 to the Pella CPA, Eksteenskuil Farmers, the Concordia CPA, the Leliefontein CPA, the Rooiwaal CPA, the Steinkopf CPA (with Goodhouse and Bulletrap), the Schamboua CPA and "Kommaggas TRANCRAA" [IC]. The notice gives no hectares [IC]. African Farming reported that the Richtersveld CPA received title deeds on 28 August 2025 [TBV].
- **Concordia.** A report that the CPA received a title deed in February 2023 could not be traced to its source [TBV].
- **Steinkopf.** In July 2026 Cape Coast News reported residents disputing the deed for Farm 22, saying it leaves out mineral and farming land, and a petition with about 800 signatures [MS]. In Parliament on 20 May 2026 a member of the PA called the Steinkopf title deed process "a textbook example of political interference gone wrong" [IC].
- **Mier and Eksteenskuil.** The guide describes each as a project covering a group of rural areas, with Mier's including informal settlements, and says the Siyanda Municipality held the land for both [IC]. The Act's Schedule repeals the Mier Rural Area Act of 1990 [IC]. No transfer record was found for Mier.

**Eastern Cape**

- **Enon/Bersheba.** In October 2023 the Minister told Parliament that the area, estimated at 10,000 hectares, had a final remaining extent of 8,314.2843 hectares, which "has since been transferred to the community through the Witrivier CPA" [IC]. About 2,000 hectares holding schools and clinics "is currently vested with the Eastern Cape Provincial Government" [IC]. The guide describes the area as Farm No 40 in the Uitenhage division, in the Sundays River Valley municipality, at about 11,000 hectares [IC].

**Free State**

- **Oppermansgronde.** The guide records that a land claim was lodged on the area, and that the Land Claims Commission has dealt with it since [IC]. No transfer record was found.
- **Thaba Phatsoa.** The area lies near Tweespruit, in Mantsopa Local Municipality [IC]. In July 2010 the guide recorded that service providers were still to be appointed for a land rights enquiry and a land management plan [IC]. The register's main page describes the enquiry as in progress in 2010, which the guide does not support [IC]. Nothing later was found.

In its 2021/22 annual report, the Department explained the missed target by "delays in passing council resolutions or reluctance by municipalities to release land to communities" and "ongoing community disputes" [IC]. In October 2024 it told Parliament of "a lack of dedicated human resource capacity to implement the programme" [IC].

Others name the state's own planning. In 2019 Parliament's portfolio committee found that TRANCRAA issues "have not found space into the targets" [IC]. In July 2025 it found that the Department "has failed to set any targets for the transfer of land" under TRANCRAA, and that real-terms budget cuts in tenure reform weaken work under the Act [IC]. Wisborg and Rohde wrote in 2003 that "the time, funding and institutional support required to carry out tenure reform have been seriously under-estimated" [IC].

*Inference:* the Department's account points at municipalities and communities, and the committee's points at the Department's targets and budget. Both can be true at once.

## After transfer

- **Enon/Bersheba.** In October 2023 the Department was helping the Witrivier CPA elect a new committee and arranging governance training [IC].
- **Steinkopf.** Residents disputed what the deed covers in 2026 [MS].
- **Ebenhaeser.** On 12 June 2021 the national Minister, Thoko Didiza, and the Western Cape Minister of Agriculture, Ivan Meyer, commissioned the upgraded Ebenhaeser irrigation scheme, which gives 153 farmers access to irrigation water [IC]. The national Department upgraded the bulk water works, and the Western Cape Department of Agriculture developed the farmland [IC]. The provincial government presents the scheme as part of the 2020 Ebenhaeser restitution settlement and does not mention TRANCRAA [IC]. The building contractor dates the work from October 2018 to August 2020 [MS]. *Inference:* state spending on Ebenhaeser's irrigation began before most of its land was transferred under TRANCRAA in 2020/21, and came through the restitution route. Restitution is a separate law from TRANCRAA.
- **Mamre.** The City of Cape Town's August 2021 draft spatial plan for Mamre still listed the "Formal transfer of Section 2 land in terms of TRANCRAA" to the City as outstanding [IC]. The same plan says lack of tenure security holds back investment on the allotment gardens, and that industrial erven had not been taken up [IC].
- **The other transferred areas.** No record was found of how Haarlem or Zoar have fared since transfer [TBV].
- **Individual rights.** No record was found of any resident in a transferred TRANCRAA area holding registered individual rights, or using land as security for a loan [TBV].
- **Research.** Wisborg and Rohde wrote in 2004 that strengthened tenure rights "appear vulnerable if isolated from training, finance and integrated development initiatives" [IC].

Figures for CPAs in general cover all of South Africa's CPAs, most of them created by other land reform programmes. They cannot be read as figures for the TRANCRAA areas.

- In October 2024 the Department told Parliament's committee that 82% of 1,742 registered CPAs were not complying with the CPA Act in 2023/24 [IC].
- The Ward 2 Forum reported in 2017, from the Department's CPA report for 2015/16, that over 1,300 of 1,483 registered CPAs were not complying [MS].

Apart from the Ebenhaeser irrigation scheme and Mamre's spatial plan, no figures were found on services, rates or investment in any transferred area, and no evaluation by the Department, a university or a funder [TBV].

## Court cases and changes to the law

| Case | What it decided | What happened next |
|---|---|---|
| Genadendal Transformation Committee v Minister of Land Affairs and Others, High Court case 926/07, order of 22 October 2008 | Made the Houw Hoek settlement an order of court [IC] | The period began in 2009 and ended in 2011 with no transfer [IC] |
| [Genadedal Transformation Committee and Others v Theewaterskloof Municipality and Others [2014] ZALCC 14](https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZALCC/2014/14.html) | Struck from the roll; the Land Claims Court has no jurisdiction over TRANCRAA disputes outside restitution [IC] | No movement found [TBV] |
| [Adendorff and Another v Theewaterskloof Municipality [2019] ZAWCHC 95](https://www.saflii.org/za/cases/ZAWCHC/2019/95.html) | Two Genadendal residents may defend municipal account claims because their land falls under TRANCRAA [IC] | No movement found [TBV] |

All three cases found concern Genadendal [IC]. No case was found in any other TRANCRAA area, and no ruling that made a Minister or a municipality act under the Act [TBV]. TRANCRAA is a land reform law, separate from restitution, as the Land Claims Court held in 2014 [IC].

- **The Communal Land Rights Act 11 of 2004** dealt with the ownership and administration of communal land. On 11 May 2010 the Constitutional Court declared it invalid in its entirety, because Parliament did not pass it by the procedure the Constitution requires (*Tongoane and Others v National Minister for Agriculture and Land Affairs and Others* [2010] ZACC 10) [IC].
- **The Land Court Act 6 of 2023** came into force on 5 April 2024 and replaced the Land Claims Court with a permanent Land Court [IC].
- **A new communal land law.** On 20 May 2026 the Minister said the Communal Land Tenure and Administration Bill would go to public consultation "by the end of June this year" [IC]. On 30 September 2026 the Department told Parliament's committee that the Communal Land Bill is "expected in 2027" [IC]. No bill to amend, replace or repeal TRANCRAA itself was found [TBV].
- **The 2025 split of the department.** No source was found that says what the split of agriculture from land reform in 2025 changed for TRANCRAA [TBV].
