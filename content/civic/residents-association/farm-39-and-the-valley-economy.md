## In short

- The municipality says it may not invest in infrastructure on land it does not own, so investment on Farm 39 "remains a challenge" [MS].
- No law was found that bans this outright. The finance law has no ownership test, and accounting guidance asks for control of the land or rights such as a servitude [IC].
- The wait clearly limits land for housing and growth. Every plan since 2019 freezes the urban edge and leaves the commonage out until the process ends [MS].
- It also limits services in Madiba Park. The municipality says the land is not municipal, and the Komitee said in 2019 it would go to court over pipes and toilets there [MS].
- It matters less for roads, sewers and water inside the townships, where the municipality has kept upgrading and planning [MS].
- Other causes weigh heavily. The Western Cape Cabinet decided on 6 November 2024 to step in to rescue the municipality's finances, and Provincial Treasury said in August 2025 that the collection rate had fallen to 64 per cent [IC]. Eskom supply and debt, low incomes and a cancelled sewer pipeline also play a part [IC] [MS].
- The record supports reading B: the wait is one cause among several, strongest for land and growth.

## How this record was put together

The test was written down first: what would count for the municipality's claim, what would count against it, and three possible readings. Then the evidence was gathered. Sources were the 2019 and 2023 Spatial Development Frameworks (SDFs), the 2023 and 2026 Integrated Development Plans (IDPs), the provincial Financial Recovery Plan, TRANCRAA, two court rulings, Census 2011 data, news reports and the valley's own documents. Some records could not be found: the 2012 SDF, year-by-year capital spending, Census 2022 small-area data, planning tribunal decisions, the valuation roll and water licences. No Deeds Office search was done.

Some news reports call the Farm 39 process "restitution". TRANCRAA is a land reform law, separate from restitution (Land Claims Court, 2014) [IC].

## What the municipality says

The SDF of November 2019 says the municipality "is not allowed to invest in infrastructure, where the Municipality is not the owner of the land" [MS]. It said the unfinished process was "the reason" little of the 2012 plan was carried out [MS]. The June 2023 draft repeats the rule and calls the process "one of the main" reasons [MS]. On 30 May 2024 the municipality's communications officer told GroundUp the municipality "cannot provide services because the settlement is not on municipal land" [MS]. By the municipality's count Madiba Park had more than 175 shacks; residents said more than 220 homes, and the relocation plan for Erf 595 in Greyton names 548 homes [MS].

The SDF does not name the law. This research checked the main laws:

- The [[rules/municipal-finance-management-act|Municipal Finance Management Act]], section 19(1), lets a municipality spend on a capital project once the council approves it and money is available [IC]. It sets no ownership test.
- Accounting guidance from July 2021 says land is recognised "based on control", and building on land owned by others needs "certain rights from the landowner", such as a servitude [IC].
- The national guide to the Municipal Infrastructure Grant (2004 to 2007) says infrastructure is usually not put on private land "unless a servitude is established" [IC].
- TRANCRAA section 2(1) says trust land in a township "must vest in the municipality" [IC]. Section 3(13) leaves the rest with the Minister, who "may continue to hold such land in trust" [IC].
- A 2014 paper by a land surveyor in the Department reads section 2 to include "all public open space within the township, all public roads" [MS].

Inference: on these sources the rule is a question of control and rights. A servitude, a lease or an agreement with the Minister could give the municipality those rights.

## The five channels

### Public infrastructure

The municipality upgraded the Genadendal treatment works in 2007, and connects all Genadendal homes to sewers [MS]. Its 2023 plan lists street upgrades in Genadendal (R1,510,378), bridges in Boschmanskloof (R1,300,000) and water pipe work (R3,500,000) [MS]. Its Genadendal landfill stands on the Remainder of Farm 39, and has held a licence to close it down since December 2019 [MS]. Bereaville still has no sewer network [MS]. The plan to pump Greyton's sewage to Genadendal was cancelled "due to community objection", by the municipality's account, and Greyton got its own package plant [MS]. Minutes of an April 2016 meeting on Greyton's heritage survey already recorded that "Genadendal is not yet willing to setup a pipeline" [MS]. In Madiba Park, the R1.6 million electrification budget was cut in February 2023, and the municipality says it cannot provide full services there [MS].

**The other side:** spending inside the townships went ahead without a transfer, and Greyton and Botrivier have the same road problems [MS].

### Land for housing and growth

Every plan since 2019 keeps the urban edge at the 2012 line until the residents list is final, and leaves the commonage out of housing plans [MS]. Nothing has been built outside the old layouts except one subsidised project [MS]. The 2023 draft assumes growth of 0.5 per cent a year, after 2.4 per cent from 2011 to 2018, and gives no reason [MS]. Inference: at 2.4 per cent, Genadendal would reach about 8,650 people by 2033 (6,824 × 1.024^10), against the SDF's 7,173. The municipality's own waste plan of October 2023 projects waste for every town, Genadendal included, with growth of 3 per cent a year [MS].

**The other side:** many erven inside the old layouts are still empty, so some growth could happen without new land [MS].

### Land for business

The plans propose a mixed-use zone on the main road and light industry, and call a formal commercial area "very unlikely" [MS]. The 2023 map shows one small business area near the werf [MS]. No refused rezoning was found.

**The other side:** Botrivier, with settled ownership, also has "very little economic activity" [MS].

### Farming and water

Studies from 1997 and 2000 found 1,300 ha of irrigable land and water for 625 ha with two dams [MS]. The Overberg Agri-Park named Genadendal as a site for a Farmer Production Support Unit, which the SDF says needs study "due to the private ownership of the settlement" [MS]. No dam, licence or support unit was found on record. Most leases on the Remainder had lapsed by the Ward 2 Forum's reading [MS].

**The other side:** in Ebenhaeser, another TRANCRAA area, the national and provincial departments built an irrigation scheme for 153 farmers, commissioned in June 2021 by National Minister Thoko Didiza and Western Cape Agriculture Minister Ivan Meyer [IC].

### Private investment and credit

A 2008 High Court order placed "an immediate moratorium on the sale of Trust Land" [IC]. In 2019 the High Court found that residents' defence against rates claims, based on TRANCRAA, was "fit for trial" [IC]. The municipality reports Genadendal's debt at R108,357,195 on 31 May 2026 [MS]. It says the delays and a rates boycott harm its finances [MS]. Residents in court said the land is held by the Minister and they should not pay rates [IC]. A 2014 paper by a land surveyor in the Department says a municipality must issue a rates clearance certificate before trust land is transferred, and that the Department should pay outstanding rates first [MS]. It gives Mamre as an example, where rates billed on unregistered development on the commonage reached about R4 million [MS]. This page takes no side.

**The other side:** some erven carry registered private title, and the Ward 2 Forum reports over 200 sales [MS].

## Other explanations

- **Municipal finances.** Unfunded budgets for 2024/25 and 2025/26, and weak credit control in Eskom areas [IC].
- **Income and jobs.** Many Genadendal workers depend on seasonal fruit farm work [MS].
- **Greyton's growth.** A 2009 study by Ronnie Donaldson found Greyton's wealth rests on gentrification, tourism and retirement [IC].
- **The "low need" rating.** The 2014 study rates Genadendal "low socio-economic need" while the SDF finds high poverty [MS]. No source shows that the rating steered spending.
- **Disputes within the community.** The pipeline was stopped by community objection, by the municipality's account [MS].

## Judged against the test

| Channel | What the record shows | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Public infrastructure | Blocked in Madiba Park; spending went ahead in the townships | Moderate |
| Housing and growth | Edge and commonage held back by the plans because of the process | Strong |
| Business | No blocked application found | Thin |
| Farming and water | Support unit held for study; leases lapsed | Thin to moderate |
| Investment and credit | Sale moratorium; rates disputed in court | Moderate |

Inference: the record supports reading B, with moderate confidence. Reading A would need records of named township projects refused because of land status, or much more investment in Botrivier and Riviersonderend. Reading C would need proof that the edge and commonage limits made no difference, and Madiba Park serviced without a transfer.

- A capital project for a Farm 39 settlement in the adopted budget, and whether it is spent.
- A change to the Genadendal urban edge.
- A farmer support unit on the ground.
- A rezoning approved in Genadendal.
- Services installed in Madiba Park.
