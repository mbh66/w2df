> [!warning] For Theewaterskloof Municipality and the Komitee to review
> This page sets the public record on the unresolved transfer of Farm 39 against each of the DevForum's five priorities. Theewaterskloof Municipality has not yet checked it. The Genadendal Transformasie Komitee, which reviews every page about TRANCRAA, has not seen it either. Until they have, the text may change. This page proposes nothing. Questions:
>
> 1. For the municipality: Has any tourism, business or skills project in a Farm 39 settlement been refused or delayed because of the land's status?
> 2. For the municipality: Which rehabilitation budget for the Genadendal landfill is current: R1,000,000 in the waste plan, or R18,585,398.51 in the 2023 IDP?
> 3. For the Komitee: Who can give consent for work such as clearing invasive plants on the Remainder of Farm 39 while the transfer is open?
> 4. For the Komitee: Does this page describe the garden plots, the leases and the Badsberg dam correctly?
> 5. For the Department: Has any support for farmers on Farm 39, such as the planned Farmer Production Support Unit, been held back because of the land's status?

The DevForum agreed on five priorities at its first meeting on 16 September 2026 [MS]. This page takes the findings of [[civic/residents-association/farm-39-and-the-valley-economy|Is the Wait for Farm 39 Holding Back the Valley's Economy?]] and sets each priority against them. That page tests, channel by channel, how far the unresolved transfer of [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] has held back the valley's economy. This page applies its record to the priorities and proposes nothing.

## In short

- Agriculture and Food Sovereignty is the priority where land status touches farming most directly, through land, leases and water. The record of actual refusals is thin.
- Business Development and Local Procurement is held back by the lack of business erven, and the 2008 court order stops the sale of trust land.
- Conservation and Environment meets the land question in sewage and in the Genadendal landfill on the Remainder of Farm 39.
- Tourism Development is touched mainly through premises and business land near the werf.
- Education and Skills is touched least directly.

## Tourism Development

[[priorities/tourism-development|Tourism Development]] is the DevForum's first priority [MS].

- The Spatial Development Framework (SDF) supports more business next to the Genadendal werf and along Strydom Street, and its 2019 plan marks the werf as a tourism node [MS].
- The same plan calls a formal commercial area "very unlikely" [MS]. No rezoning for the mixed-use zone on the main road was found.
- The 2023 map shows one small business area, near the werf [MS]. In Ranyaka's 2024 sessions, residents scored business premises and support for small businesses low [MS].
- Greyton's tourism sector "responded positively" to the 2012 plan, by the SDF's account [MS]. Donaldson's 2009 study ties Greyton's growth to tourism and incomers [IC].
- The tourism problems raised at the DevForum's first meeting (a working tourism office, trained guides, transport permits) are not tied to land status in any record found.

Inference: for tourism, the wait works mainly through premises and business land near the werf. No tourism project refused because of land status was found.

## Education and Skills

[[priorities/education-skills|Education and Skills]] is the DevForum's second priority [MS].

- The 2019 SDF says the municipality "can only invest in community facilities on properties it owns", so facilities in the Farm 39 settlements are "problematic" [MS].
- No school, training centre or skills project stopped by land status was found.
- Most leases on the Remainder had lapsed by the Ward 2 Forum's reading, and no Farmer Production Support Unit was found on the ground [MS].

Inference: this priority is touched least directly. The link runs through community buildings, and through whether people learning to farm can get land to farm on.

## Agriculture and Food Sovereignty

[[priorities/agriculture-food-sovereignty|Agriculture and Food Sovereignty]] is the DevForum's third priority [MS].

- Studies from 1997 and 2000 found 1,300 ha of irrigable land, 600 ha of it high potential, and water for 625 ha if two dams are built [MS]. No dam, water licence or support unit was found on record.
- The SDF holds the support unit back for further study "due to the private ownership of the settlement" [MS].
- A 2021 provincial meeting noted that no land was available for small farmers [MS]. Most leases on the Remainder had lapsed [MS].
- The consultants' lawyers concluded in 2010 that users of the garden plots hold use rights that can be registered, while ownership stays with the municipality [MS]. The DevForum's first meeting noted that many community gardens lie fallow [MS]. No record found ties the fallow gardens to land status.
- The Badsberg dam belongs to the Genadendal agricultural community, by the municipality's account [MS].

**The other side:** in Ebenhaeser the departments built irrigation on TRANCRAA land [IC]. In Mamre, the City's 2021 draft plan says lack of tenure security holds back investment on allotment gardens [IC].

Inference: this is the priority where land status touches the means of farming most directly: land, leases and water. The record of actual refusals is thin.

## Business Development and Local Procurement

[[priorities/business-procurement|Business Development and Local Procurement]] aims to keep more money in the ward [MS].

- The SDF says a lack of industrial and business erven "limits economic development and growth" [MS]. The 2019 plan set aside 14.2 ha for light industry in Genadendal, and none was built [MS].
- No rezoning or planning decision for a business in Genadendal was found.
- Ranyaka counted about 20 local businesses taking part in its 2024 sessions [MS].
- The 2008 court order stops the sale of trust land [IC]. Inference: land that cannot be sold or titled cannot be offered as security for a business loan. No figures on bonds were found.
- No record was found that links land status to local procurement.

**The other side:** Botrivier, with settled ownership, also has very little business activity [MS]. Some erven carry private title [MS].

## Conservation and Environment

[[priorities/conservation-environment|Conservation and Environment]] covers the valley's land, water and wildlife [MS].

- The 2023 SDF map says the Greyton works discharges substandard effluent into the Sonderend River [MS]. The 2023 Integrated Development Plan (IDP) says the Greyton works runs over its design capacity [MS]. The link to the Genadendal works was cancelled after community objection, by the municipality's account, and Greyton got a package plant [MS].
- Bereaville uses septic and conservancy tanks, emptied by tanker [MS]. Many Boschmanskloof homes still use conservancy tanks [MS]. In 2022 a provincial legislature member said 161 Madiba Park families lived without sanitation [MS].
- The Genadendal landfill on the Remainder is nearly full, with 3,897 m³ of space left against 49,198 m³ at Greyton's, and has a licence to close [MS]. The 2023 IDP budgets its rehabilitation at R18,585,398.51 for 2025 to 2030 [MS]. The waste plan of October 2023 gives it R1,000,000 in 2026/27, while Greyton's landfill gets R3,000,000 to R4,561,254 a year from 2023/24 [MS]. An external audit in 2021/22 scored the Genadendal site's compliance with its licence at 41.98 per cent, against 62.30 per cent for Greyton's [MS].
- The SDF proposes extending the north-south environmental corridors between the settlements [MS].
- Most of Farm 39 lies outside the townships and is held by the Minister in trust [IC].

Inference: work on that land, such as clearing invasive plants, depends on who can give consent while the transfer is open. No record of consent refused was found.
