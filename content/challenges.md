The challenge that reaches furthest into daily life in Ward 2 in 2026 is land title. Twenty-eight years after the Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act ([[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]]) came into force, the trust land of [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]], on which [[villages/genadendal|Genadendal]], [[villages/bereaville|Bereaville]], [[villages/voorstekraal|Voorstekraal]], [[villages/boschmanskloof|Boschmanskloof]] and [[villages/madiba-park|Madiba Park]] stand, is still held by the national Minister, and no receiving body has been recognised [MS]. On the [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] side, [[villages/erf-595-greyton|Erf 595]] measures 1,404.89 hectares in the Surveyor-General's records [IC], and only a small part of it has a published use: 333 homes authorised in July 2025 [IC]. This page traces these and the valley's other challenges from the first people to 2026, records what the Forum has heard so far, and arranges the 2026 evidence for the second research run.

- Sections 1 to 3 tell where the challenges came from and where they stand.

- Sections 5 to 7 describe how the valley is seen, the questions still open, and who must be asked.

## The official picture in short

Government documents describe the valley through poverty, teenage pregnancy, substance abuse and crime. This section records what could be confirmed.

Every figure below is for the whole of [[organisations/theewaterskloof-municipality|Theewaterskloof Municipality]], which has fourteen wards [IC]. Wards can be explored using [this link](https://arcg.is/jOKyf0). No official source reached gives these figures for Ward 2 or for any single settlement.

![[x_____xXp4L_hzZ-GadK1RgE-QpDQ..x_____x_ags_eac4fe0e-d432-11f1-a4bf-0affdf986f91.jpg]]

- **Population.** The Provincial Treasury's 2024 Socio-economic Profile gives 128,320 people in 33,088 households in 2024, from Stats SA's mid-year estimates [IC]. The 2026 IDP amendment gives 124,050 people in 35,990 households in 2021 [IC]. Sources citing Census 2022 give 139,563 people in 36,239 households [TBV]. The latest settlement counts found are from Census 2011: Genadendal 5,663 people and Greyton 2,780 [TBV]. From our own informal counts, we believe these numbers are significantly lower than the actual number of people in the valley, so we generally refer to 30,000 residents.
- **Poverty.** In 2023, 69.0% of the municipality's people lived below the upper-bound poverty line of R1,684 per person per month (2024 value) [IC]. The IDP gives 53.33% for 2021, measured against a line of R1,227 in 2019 prices [IC]. The two figures use different line values and should not be read as a trend. The number of indigent households receiving free basic services rose to 6,396 in 2023 [IC]. Earnings for farm labour stayed between R3,200 and R6,400 a month in 2022 and 2023 [IC].
- **Ratings of need.** The 2026 IDP amendment repeats a 2004 rating: Greyton has "low human needs and low development potential", and Genadendal "medium human needs and low development potential" [IC]. The IDP records that residents and the municipality disagree with Greyton's rating [IC]. The 2023 draft spatial plan, as quoted by Ranyaka, says a "high level of poverty exists" in Genadendal, Voorstekraal, Bereaville, Heuwelkroon and Boschmanskloof [MS].
- **Work.** Unemployment (narrow definition) was 12.2% in 2023 [IC], down from 13.9% in 2021 in the IDP [IC].
- **Teenage pregnancy.** In 2023/24, 15.3% of deliveries in the municipality were to women aged 10 to 19, which was 263 births, up from 14.9% the year before [IC]. The IDP gives 16.2% for 2021/22 [IC]. The IDP's summary table labels that figure "U18" and its text says "under 20 years" [IC].
- **Crime.** Reported cases in the municipality, from the 2024 profile [IC]:

| Crime | 2022/23 | 2023/24 | Rate per 100,000, 2023/24 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Murder | 77 | 57 | 44 |
| Sexual offences | 116 | 87 | 68 |
| Drug-related crime | 1,152 | 1,213 | Not given |
| Residential burglary | 633 | 659 | 513 |

The profile's summary page gives 59 murders and 84 sexual offences for 2023/24, which differs from its own table [IC]. The IDP gives the 2021/22 figures: 59 murders, 87 sexual offences, 923 drug-related crimes, 624 residential burglaries [IC].
- **Housing and services.** In 2024, 80.1% of households lived in formal housing and 19.6% in informal dwellings, against 13.7% informal across the Overberg District [IC]. 96.8% had piped water, 93.2% electricity for lighting, 83.9% a flush or chemical toilet, and 75.4% weekly refuse removal [IC].
- **Health and learning.** The municipality has six fixed clinics, 13 mobile or satellite clinics and one district hospital, in Caledon [IC]. It had 27 severely malnourished children under five in 2023/24, the most in the district [IC]. The matric pass rate was 87.1% in 2023, and only 71.6% of learners stayed from Grade 10 to Grade 12 [IC].
- **Gender-based violence and substance abuse.** No municipal or settlement figure for gender-based violence was found. Ward 2's needs list asks for a safe house for abused women and children [IC], and Ward 4's asks for help with gender-based violence and substance abuse [IC].
- **Where to get help.** SAPS Crime Stop: 08600 10111. Gender-Based Violence Command Centre: 0800 428 428 [TBV].

Residents also scored the valley themselves. In the 2024 Ranyaka sessions, seven groups of about eight to ten people gave the valley an overall score of 48% [MS]. Social cohesion scored highest, and well-being ("healthy, educated and active") lowest [MS].

## 1. The historical record

This section follows the eras of [[history/history-of-the-valley|History of the Valley]]. For each era it gives the challenges that arose and what produced them, who bore them, what people did, and what was lost or carried through.

### The first people (before 1652)

- **Challenges.** People lived in this area more than 200,000 years ago [IC]. The [[history/tribes/soaqua|Soaqua]], the San of this part of the Cape, lived by hunting and gathering [IC]. When [[history/tribes/khoekhoe|Khoekhoe]] herders arrived about 2,000 years ago, the Soaqua moved higher into the mountains [IC].
- **Who.** By the 1600s the [[history/tribes/chainoqua|Chainoqua]] and the [[history/tribes/hessequa|Hessequa]] were the two main Khoekhoe peoples of the Overberg [IC]. Some Chainoqua kraals stood near where Genadendal is today [IC].
- **How land was held.** Land was held by the group as a whole, and nobody could divide it for themselves [IC]. Herders moved with the seasons, so the land looked empty when they were away [IC].
- **What carried through.** Names such as Gobos [TBV]. Rock art survives in the mountains [IC]. Its locations are not given on this site.

### Contact with the Dutch (1652 to 1737)

- **Challenges and causes.** The Chainoqua first met the [[history/institutions/dutch-east-india-company|Dutch East India Company]] (VOC) in 1657 [IC]. For about fifty years they and the Hessequa were the main suppliers of cattle to the Cape [IC]. In 1669 Dutch travellers found rich grazing at the Huis River, where Greyton stands, and settlers took land that looked empty as free to take [IC]. Smallpox in 1713 killed many Khoekhoe, and more settlers moved onto their land [IC]. By 1727 the VOC was farming timber and cattle between present-day Greyton and Riviersonderend [IC]. See [[history/markets/loan-farms-and-quitrent|Loan Farms and Quitrent]].
- **Who.** Khoekhoe herding communities of the Overberg [IC].
- **Response.** Greyton sources say the Hessequa leader [[history/people/stoffel-koekson|Stoffel Koekson]] moved his people to Boschmanskloof [TBV].
- **What was lost.** Herds, grazing and people, through trade, disease and the spread of farms [IC]. Independent Khoekhoe kraals were still recorded near Genadendal in 1803 [IC].

### Georg Schmidt at Baviaanskloof (1737 to 1744)

- **Challenges and causes.** [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Schmidt]] settled at Baviaanskloof on 23 April 1738 [IC]. Local Dutch Reformed ministers objected that he had no right to baptise, and farmers were unhappy to lose cheap Khoekhoe labour [IC]. He left in 1744 and was never allowed back [IC].
- **What carried through.** Teaching under the pear tree, a water furrow and gardens [IC]. Five baptised converts, among them Vehettge Tikkuie, known as [[history/people/magdalena|Magdalena]] [MS].

### Magdalena and the pear tree (1744 to 1792)

- **Challenges.** For 48 years there was no missionary at Baviaanskloof [IC].
- **Response.** Magdalena kept the small congregation together and read to them from the Dutch New Testament Schmidt had left her [IC]. She kept it wrapped in sheepskin [MS].
- **What carried through.** The congregation, and the place. She died on 3 January 1800 [IC].

### The mission grows (1792 to 1838)

- **Labour, debt and the contest over children.** The [[history/the-genadendal-diaries|missionaries' diaries]] of the 1790s show farmers keeping back children who went to the mission school "against their parents' will" [MS]. Workers came home from the harvest owing the farmers as much as they had earned [MS]. See [[history/from-farm-to-classroom|From Farm to Classroom]].
- **The Caledon Code of 1809.** It required every Khoekhoe person to have a fixed, registered place of abode and to carry a pass, and anyone without one could be treated as a vagrant [IC]. See [[history/markets/caledon-code|Caledon Code]].
- **The apprenticeship law of 1812.** It let a colonist who had kept a Khoekhoe child on his farm for its first eight years keep the child in unpaid service for ten more [IC]. One historian counts at least 2,295 children apprenticed between 1812 and 1823 in the districts he studied [TBV].
- **Ordinance 50 of 1828.** It repealed these laws, removed the pass requirement, let people choose their employers and own land, and gave free people of colour equality before the law [IC].
- **Who.** Khoekhoe families on the farms and at the mission [IC].
- **Response.** The mission became one of the few places where Khoekhoe people could live without working for a farmer [TBV]. In 1794 the missionaries used the government's orders to stop a farmer taking two girls from their mother [MS].
- **What carried through.** A forge and water mill (1797), Genadendal knives sold across the Cape, a school from 1793 that still runs today as [[cultural/education/lr-schmidt-primary|L.R. Schmidt Primary]] [MS]. In 1813 a surveyor mapped the mission's boundary, and residents were given no proof that they owned their land [MS].

### Emancipation and the busy years (1834 to 1860s)

- **Challenges and causes.** Slavery ended in law in 1834, and people were fully free on 1 December 1838 [IC]. The Masters and Servants Ordinance of 1841 again made breaking a labour contract a crime, and was replaced by Act 15 of 1856 [IC]. Their effect in the valley has not been traced [TBV].
- **Who.** Freed people arriving at the mission. Genadendal grew from 1,446 people in 1837 to 2,187 in 1840 [MS].
- **The land grant of 1858.** In November 1857 the surveyor J. Kuys measured the mission land at 5,644 morgen and 289 square roods [MS]. In 1858 it was granted to the [[history/institutions/moravian-church|Moravian Church]] "for the use of and in trust for such persons as may from time to time be lawfully resident at the institution of Genadendal" [MS].
- **Response, in residents' terms.** Balie's history says many residents rejected the grant because the church's superintendent was the only trustee, and no resident sat beside him [MS]. It says they held that the land had belonged to their Khoi ancestors [MS].
- **What carried through.** South Africa's first teachers' training college opened in 1838 [IC]. The press printed *De Bode van Genadendal* from 1859 [IC]. The brass band dates from 1856 [MS].

### Greyton is founded (1793 to 1910)

- **Challenges and causes.** Hessequa grazing land on the Gobos River was granted to [[history/people/marthinus-theunissen|Marthinus Theunissen]] as the farm Weltevreden in 1793 [IC]. [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]] bought it in 1846 and laid out Greyton, with the first plots sold in 1854 [IC].
- **What the plots held.** Each plot came with a title deed, a water right and grazing rights, and plots were sold to buyers of any race [TBV]. Plot owners shared grazing on the commonage around the village [MS]. The leiwater furrows have carried Gobos water to plots with a water right since 1854 [MS]. See [[history/leiwater|Greyton's Leiwater]].
- **The commonage.** Greyton's histories say Vigne left the land around the village to the plot owners as a commonage [TBV]. A formal record of his will or deed of gift, recognised by the state, has not yet been sourced [TBV].
- **What carried through.** Greyton had 276 residents in 1891 [IC] and became a municipality in 1910 [IC].

### Hard years: land, work and law (1860s to 1950)

- **The crafts decline.** Cheap factory-made goods arriving by steamship undercut Genadendal's knives, furniture and other crafts, and many people left to find work in towns and on farms [TBV]. Balie's history says Sheffield knives were sold at the Cape with the Genadendal mark, and the last of Genadendal's knife-makers died in 1880 [MS]. From 1877 the mission tried castor oil, silkworms and buchu for export [MS].
- **The water sources.** In 1899 the Moravian Church bought the mountain farm [[villages/farm-32-langgezocht|Langgezocht]] (Farm 32) to protect Genadendal's water sources [MS].
- **The Act of 1909.** The Mission Stations and Communal Reserves Act (Act 29 of 1909) placed mission land under management boards [IC]. Residents kept the right to live on the land, but could not own it [IC]. Balie's history says the Act gave the mission title to its church, school and houses, reserved the rest for registered occupiers, and set up a board of six elected and three nominated members [MS]. It says many residents left the Moravian Church over the Act [MS].
- **The land cases.** Balie's history records a case over land ownership brought against the mission in the Supreme Court at the end of the 1800s, and another brought in 1924 by a group of residents known as the Magermans [MS]. What either court decided has not been found [TBV].
- **Who governed.** In 1924 a secular board of management replaced the missionaries [IC].
- **The college closes, 1926.** The government closed the teachers' college, arguing that coloured people did not need higher education because they were suited to work on farms [IC]. It had trained 236 teachers [MS].
- **The Glebe Lands, 1927.** Title to the core mission areas of Genadendal and Bereaville, known as the Glebe Lands, was granted free of charge to the Moravian Church in 1927 [IC]. The [[civic/governance/ward-2-forum-2016-2019|Ward 2 Forum]]'s 2017 series describes them as "nine portions of land" of 68 hectares [MS], and [[organisations/urban-dynamics-western-cape|the consultants]]' 2010 report gives 70.7795 hectares [MS]. They were cut out of the mission land before the state's 1952 deed of grant for Farm 39, so the Deeds Office record of the farm does not include them [IC]. They sit outside the TRANCRAA process [IC]. In the Surveyor-General's records the Glebe Land is Farm 827, Caledon, named "GLEBE LAND", 61.11 hectares [IC]. The Deeds Office report records Portions 3 to 7 of Farm 39, about 2.34 hectares, as consolidated into Farm 827 [IC]. The parcel covers Genadendal's mission core, and where the Bereaville portions of the 1927 grant are recorded is still open [TBV]. See [[villages/farm-827-glebe-land|Farm 827: The Glebe Land]].
- **What carried through.** A high school opened in the old college building in 1938, and later became [[cultural/education/emil-weder-secondary|Emil Weder Secondary]] [MS]. Balie's history says Genadendal stayed an undeveloped village of about 5,000 people under a series of government departments from 1926 to 1997 [MS].

### Apartheid (1950 to 1994)

- **The Group Areas removals.** Under the Group Areas Act of 1950, coloured families in Greyton, many of whom owned plots and small farms, were forced to sell and leave [IC]. Most were moved to [[villages/heuwelkroon|Heuwelkroon]] [IC]. The 2022 heritage study says some went to Genadendal [TBV].
- **When.** Sources differ. Some Greyton histories say the 1950s [TBV]. An essay in *Herri* gives 1968 to 1974 [MS], and the 2022 heritage study says Heuwelkroon was built in 1970 [TBV]. The two later sources agree with each other.
- **In the words of those affected.** The *Herri* essay says that before the removals Greyton's residents "lived together irrespective of 'race'", and that afterwards "a community of subsistence farmers who owned their own farms were overnight reduced to having to work as gardeners and domestics for white people" [MS].
- **In Genadendal.** Balie's history says fear of removal spread to Genadendal, and many residents stopped maintaining their houses [MS]. Members of the Teachers' League met in the parsonage loft to oppose apartheid, and one was placed under house arrest in 1962 [MS].
- **The water mill.** In the 1950s co-operative laws forced farmers to send their wheat to licensed co-operatives, and the water mill closed [MS]. It was restored in 1992 [MS].
- **The mountain.** On 13 May 1977 the [[economic/farmers-land-water/greyton-conservation-society|Greyton Nature Reserve]], about 2,200 hectares, was proclaimed on the mountain commonage [IC].
- **Act 9 of 1987.** The Rural Areas Act 9 of 1987 took over the former mission areas, with most of the land held by the state in trust for residents [IC]. Genadendal was added in 1989 [IC].
- **What carried through.** The [[cultural/sport-heritage-arts-culture/genadendal-mission-museum|Mission Museum]] opened in the 1838 college building in 1987 [MS].

### Democracy and the land question (1994 to 2011)

- **Local government.** In the mid-1990s transitional councils replaced Greyton's white council and the Heuwelkroon management committee [IC]. In December 2000 the valley became part of Theewaterskloof Municipality [IC].
- **TRANCRAA.** The Act came into force on 2 November 1998 [IC]. Trust land inside a township passes to the municipality, and the Remainder may go to the municipality, a [[rules/communal-property-associations-act|Communal Property Association]] (CPA) or another body the Minister approves [IC].
- **The process on Farm 39.** The municipality began it in 2002 through consultants it appointed [IC]. Residents elected the [[civic/governance/genadendal-transformasie-komitee|Transformasie Komitee]] on 9 November 2004 [IC]. The Komitee rejected the 2005 list of residents as incomplete [IC]. On 31 January 2007 it applied to the High Court (case 926/07) to review the municipality's actions, including the "sale and transfer of trust immovable property" [IC]. The settlement became an order of court on 22 October 2008, with "an immediate moratorium on the sale of Trust Land" [IC]. The 18-month transitional period began on 8 July 2009 and ended in January 2011 without a transfer [IC]. Full story: [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]].
- **The printing works.** The Genadendal printing works closed in 2003 after more than 170 years [MS]. Balie's history says the presses moved to Cape Town in 2002 to 2003 [MS].

### Recent years (2011 to 2026)

- **2011.** Greyton residents started Greyton Transition Town in response to rising fuel and food costs [MS].
- **2014.** The Land Claims Court struck the Komitee's application from the roll for lack of jurisdiction [IC].
- **2016.** The municipality wrote that "a hostile environment has over the years developed between the TWK and the Transformation Committee" [MS], and asked the Minister to transfer the land to it [MS].
- **2017.** The Ward 2 Forum published *Farm 39 Transformation: The Lost 18 Years* [IC]. The municipality named Madiba Park as a possible site for families displaced in Bereaville [MS].
- **Madiba Park, 2019.** Chemical toilets were removed on 13 January and the water was cut off on 14 January, and residents protested [IC]. GroundUp reported "about 100 shacks on the land, housing about 300 people" [MS]. The municipality promised four flush toilets, a septic tank, and serviced plots elsewhere within 18 months [MS].
- **The 2019 ruling.** The High Court let two Genadendal residents defend claims for unpaid municipal accounts on the ground that their land falls under TRANCRAA, and left the question for trial [IC].
- **The fire of December 2019.** About 5,950 hectares burned on the mountain slopes above Greyton [IC]. A firefighter interviewed on a podcast described the fire as burning 16,500 hectares, including parts of McGregor, Genadendal and Voorstekraal [MS]. The two figures may describe different areas [TBV].
- **2020.** Residents halted a proposed R100 sale of an eight-hectare parcel in Greyton [MS]. Small-scale and commercial farmers set up a community feeding scheme during the lockdown [MS].
- **2022 to 2023.** The municipality budgeted R1.6 million to electrify Madiba Park, then cut the project in the February 2023 adjustment budget, with R73,135 spent [IC].
- **2024.** The province intervened in the municipality under [[rules/constitution|section 139 of the Constitution]] on 11 November 2024 [IC].
- **2025.** The province authorised 333 homes on Erf 595 on 10 July 2025 [IC]. The Financial Recovery Plan was approved on 7 October 2025 [IC]. The Komitee aligned with AfriForum and the Kaapse Forum, and began setting up a CPA to receive the land [MS].
- **2026.** The municipality signed a new repayment agreement with [[organisations/eskom|Eskom]] in March 2026 [IC]. Council amended the IDP on 28 May 2026 [IC]. [[civic/governance/ward-2-development-forum|The Forum met for the first time]] on 16 September 2026 [MS]. Local government elections are set for 4 November 2026 [IC].

## 3. Three deep reports

### 3a. TRANCRAA and Farm 39, to October 2026

This updates [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]].

**Where it stands.** The Remainder of Farm 39, about 4,430 hectares outside the four township areas, is held in trust by the national Minister [IC]. No entity has been recognised [MS]. The 2008 order has not been set aside as far as the public record shows [TBV]. A Komitee office-bearer told GroundUp in 2024 that the community had set up a CPA and was waiting for the Department to finalise it [MS]. The Department's spokesperson said "certain issues" were still outstanding [MS]. No record of a CPA registration was found [TBV].

**Who cannot get title, credit, approval or services.** No resident of the Remainder can hold title to land there until a transfer happens [MS]. *Inference:* without title, land cannot be offered as security for a bank loan. Residents raised building-plan approvals and sewage at the Forum's first meeting [MS]. In 2024 the municipality said it could not provide full services at Madiba Park because "the settlement is not on municipal land" [MS]. It had agreed with the Komitee to put five toilets and water tanks on the settlement's edge [MS], and GroundUp found one toilet working [TBV].

**Rates and the municipality's debt.** The Financial Recovery Plan says "Genadendal is particularly challenging as services are rendered and billed, but the community feels that the land does not belong to the municipality and that they therefore have no obligation to pay for said services" [IC]. In 2016 the municipality listed "the Rates boycott and the poor collection rate in Genadendal" as the first issue to resolve [MS]. The municipality's own quarterly reports show Genadendal's share of debt rising [IC]:

| Date | Genadendal debtors | Share of all debtors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 28 February 2025 (Recovery Plan) | Not given | About 20% |
| 31 October 2025 | R97,783,258 | 23.0% |
| 31 January 2026 | R103,966,817 | 23.2% |
| 31 March 2026 | R104,916,378 | 23.8% |
| 31 May 2026 | R108,357,195 | 23.7% |

Greyton's municipal-supply area stood at R7,981,241 (1.75%) in May 2026 [IC]. Across the municipality, Eskom-supplied areas, where meters cannot be blocked, held 86% of all debt [IC]. The 2019 High Court ruling leaves open, for trial, whether residents of TRANCRAA land can be held liable for municipal accounts [IC].

**The rule against investing on land the municipality does not own.** The 2019 Spatial Development Framework states: "In terms of applicable municipal legislation, TWKM is not allowed to invest in infrastructure, where the Municipality is not the owner of the land" [IC]. The 2023 draft says "the tenure transformation process should be concluded before the TWKM may invest in engineering infrastructure within the settlements" [MS]. On Madiba Park the rule shows in the record: electrification budgeted at R1.6 million and cut in 2023 [IC], and the municipality's two letters to the national Minister asking permission for interim services, which the DA said went unanswered [MS]. No figure was found for the value of infrastructure not built on Farm 39 since 2000 [TBV]. In its 2016 letter to the Department, the municipality named three issues to settle in the meantime: "The Rates boycott and the poor collection rate in Genadendal", "The Waste Water treatment pipeline from Greyton to the Genadendal Waste Water Treatment Works", and "Various issues relating to Town Planning and Building Control" [MS]. It wrote that the delays were "having a serious negative impact on the sustainability of the Municipality" [MS].

**The properties said in 2017 to have been sold.** The Komitee's 2007 application objected to the "sale and transfer of trust immovable property" [IC]. The Ward 2 Forum's 2017 series says over 200 residential properties on Farm 39 had been sold and transferred "with the knowledge and assistance of TWKM", and that the consultants' land rights report does not record them [MS].

The Deeds Office search report attached to the 2008 court order (Annexure GT1) is the fullest record found of what has been taken off the farm [IC]. It shows:

- The state holds Farm 39 under Deed of Grant G19/1952, dated 6 February 1952, at 5,572 morgen and 261 square roods, or 4,772.97 hectares [IC].
- Portions 1 and 2 of the farm became Erf 1555 (Gr130/1953) and Erf 68 (T47138/1984) [IC]. Portions 3 to 7, about 2.3 hectares together, were consolidated into Farm 827 [IC].
- Eight erven totalling 206.2451 hectares carry title deeds, and a further 134.994 hectares of surveyed erven are unregistered [IC]. Handwritten notes on the report name the "Gemeenskap van Genadendal" against most of the registered erven [MS].
- The registered remainder of Farm 39 on 25 April 2007 was 4,566.72 hectares, and the unregistered remainder 4,431.73 hectares [IC].

The consultants' 2010 report puts the land in the process as the Remainder of Farm 39 (G19/1952, 4,614.56 hectares) and Erf 387 (T75345/95, 26.89 hectares) [MS]. The two sources disagree on Erf 1353: the 2017 series says the municipality owns it (T13058/1996) [MS], and the consultants' lawyers say it was transferred to a private owner [MS]. What has happened to the 200 properties since 2017, and whether those transfers are valid, was not found [TBV].

**Garden plots and leases.** The consultants' lawyers concluded in 2010 that users of the garden plots (*tuin persele*) hold registerable use rights, that ownership stays with the municipality, and that the "grondbriewe" the housing department issued for the plots were not in accordance with the law [MS]. They found that the farm leases on the Remainder had expired with no record of renewal, and the consultants could not verify the lease records [MS]. The 2005 and 2010 lease surveys do not match each other [MS].

**Who counts as a resident.** TRANCRAA counts anyone who ordinarily lived in the area on 2 November 1998, or was liable for rates there on that date [IC]. In July 2010 the municipality, the Department and the consultants decided that updating the residents list was "not further required" [IC]. The consultants wrote that an updated list "will have no material impact on the Transformation Process" [MS]. The Ward 2 Forum's 2017 series says that people who moved in after the qualifying date, "who are considered by a majority of the Transformation Committee members to be in good standing, may be offered the opportunity to be considered as legitimate beneficiaries" [MS]. No later decision on this was found [TBV].

**What other TRANCRAA areas show.**

- Parliament was told in October 2024 that 603,000 of about 1.4 million hectares had been transferred, and that staff shortages, a lack of municipal co-operation and "disputes within communities about the land to be transferred" were the main obstacles [IC].
- Haarlem, Mamre and most of Ebenhaeser were transferred in 2020/21 [IC]. Enon/Bersheba went to the Witrivier CPA (8,314 hectares) [IC]. In August 2025 the Minister handed over title deeds to Namakwa bodies including the Pella, Concordia, Leliefontein, Steinkopf and Komaggas areas [IC].
- Every transfer found came more than twenty years after the Act [IC].
- In October 2024, 82% of South Africa's 1,742 registered CPAs were not complying with the law that governs them [IC]. The Department's 2024/25 CPA report says CPAs "are institutionally complex" and hold land for communities "fractured by the history of dispossession" [IC].
- Research in Namaqualand found that new tenure rights stay "vulnerable if isolated from training, finance and integrated development initiatives" [TBV].
- How Mamre, Haarlem, Ebenhaeser and Enon/Bersheba have fared since transfer was not found [TBV].

**Statements by each side on Madiba Park.** Each is that party's own account.

- A Madiba Park resident, 2024: "When it comes near to the time, nothing happens, we protest and then they promise the same thing" [MS].
- The Komitee's treasurer, 2019: "We want [the occupiers] off our land, onto municipal land" [MS]. GroundUp reported that the Komitee wanted room for "about 70 coloured families with a history of living in Greyton and nearby Genadendal" [MS].
- The Komitee's treasurer, 2024: "We don't want to evict people, we want to relocate them" [MS].
- Theewaterskloof Municipality, 2024: "most of Genadendal and Greyton land is under the flood line" [MS].
- An ANC member of the provincial legislature, 2019: "This site does not belong to the municipality yet they settled people here" [MS].
- No public statement on Madiba Park by a Greyton or Heuwelkroon residents body was found.

GroundUp called the process a "land restitution process" [MS]. TRANCRAA is a land reform law, separate from restitution [IC].

### 3b. Erf 595

**Size.** 1,404.89 hectares in the Surveyor-General's records [IC]. The 2022 Environmental Management Programme says "approximately 1425 ha" [MS].

**Owner and manager.** The environmental authorisation was granted to Theewaterskloof Municipality as the applicant [IC]. It does not say who holds title. A 2022 national Department tender states that "the Remainder of Erf 1723 (Portion of Erf 595) was purchased by the Department of Rural Development", and seeks a consultant to subdivide and rezone it from "Undetermined Zone" [TBV]. A second tender covers Erf 1724, also a portion of Erf 595 [TBV]. Only parts of the tenders were read. *Inference:* parts of the original erf have been subdivided, and at least one part may be held by the national Department. Who manages the land outside the housing site was not found [TBV].

**Is it the Greyton commonage?** Greyton's histories say Vigne left the land around the village to the plot owners as a commonage, and that it passed to Theewaterskloof Municipality in 2000 [TBV]. No will, deed or title condition has been found [TBV]. No source reached says whether Erf 595 is that commonage, in whole or in part. The nature reserve was proclaimed in 1977 on the "mountain commonage" [IC]. Whether any terms of the commonage bind the erf's use today cannot be tested until the deed or will is found.

**Zoning, use and condition.** The housing site lies inside the urban edge and is earmarked for urban development [IC]. It holds a sports field, alien trees, tracks and patches of indigenous vegetation, and the rest of the site is "significantly degraded or devoid of any vegetation" [IC]. The Boesmanskloof tributary "flows within a deeply eroded river channel" and is "largely to seriously modified" [IC]. The botanical study placed the site in an ecological support area "recommended for restoration" [MS]. No record was found of zoning, leases, grazing or invasive plant mapping for the rest of the erf [TBV].

**Reserve, biodiversity and vegetation.** The authorisation says the 2018 vegetation map shows Central Rûens Shale Renosterveld (Critically Endangered) and Greyton Shale Fynbos (Endangered) on the site [IC]. Greyton Shale Fynbos was listed Critically Endangered in the national list of November 2022 [IC]. How much of the erf lies inside the nature reserve or a mapped critical biodiversity area, and how much is Greyton Shale Fynbos, was not found [TBV].

**What the 2022 studies found.** The heritage study found "very little mitigation required" and no archaeology on the site [MS]. [[organisations/heritage-western-cape|Heritage Western Cape]] approved the urban design on 18 July 2024 [IC].

**Why 548 became 333.** The 2022 programme described "approximately 548 residential erven" [MS]. Layouts of 345, 377 and 380 erven "did not accommodate the aquatic constraints, specialist recommendations and input received during the public participation process" [IC]. The approved plan keeps 35 and 25 metres from the two streams and infills about 1 hectare of degraded wetland [IC]. A municipal spokesperson said in 2024 that the impact assessment and the flood line had delayed the work [MS]. No study was found on the flood line or heritage of the rest of the erf [TBV].

**Beneficiaries and timing.** The homes are for "beneficiaries on the municipal housing list for Greyton", as GAP and subsidy housing [IC]. The 2022 botanical study mentions "over 200 individuals on the municipal waiting list" [MS]. GroundUp linked the Madiba Park relocation to this site in 2024 [MS]. The authorisation does not name Madiba Park [IC]. The Ward 2 Community Needs List in the 2026 IDP puts the housing backlog, including "Madiba Park and backyard dwellers", first, and says the municipality "must look at the possibility to use erf. 595, Greyton" [IC]. Whether Madiba Park households are on the beneficiary list for the 333 homes is still not confirmed [TBV].

**The money for building.** The municipality's 2025/26 capital programme lists the project as "Greyton Erf 595 (538)(Yield reduced)(393)", with R10 million from the Human Settlement Grant [IC]. In October 2025 the municipality wrote that the implementing agent had completed the conceptual layout, and that the tender would go to the Bid Specification Committee in December 2025 [IC]. The March 2026 report says the budget "was removed with the February 2026 Adjustment Budget", after R1,204,753 had been spent [IC]. A separate in-kind provincial allocation of R2 million appears in the same report, with nothing spent [IC]. The project's yield of 393 differs from the 333 homes authorised in July 2025 [IC]. The authorisation allows ten years from 10 July 2025 to begin [IC]. No new start date was found [TBV].

**What it earns and costs.** No figure was found for Erf 595 [TBV]. The Recovery Plan says the municipality's 67 rental properties earn "only R1,2m per annum" and that "assets are underutilised to generate alternative income streams" [IC].

**How much is idle.** The authorised housing site is about 6 to 7 hectares of a 1,404.89-hectare erf [IC]. *Inference:* more than 99% of the erf has no use recorded in any source reached for this page. Why is not recorded.

### 3c. Municipal finance and governance

**The Financial Recovery Plan.** See [[rules/theewaterskloof-financial-recovery-plan|Theewaterskloof Financial Recovery Plan]]. It records unfunded budgets, "weak credit control, especially in Eskom-supplied areas", 43% of water lost or unbilled, and "only 9 of the 14 Ward Committees" working [IC]. Unpaid consumer accounts stood at R384 million on 28 February 2025 [IC]. The plan projects cash shortfalls until a surplus "by around 2030" [IC]. **Electricity.** The municipality sells electricity in Greyton, and Eskom supplies Genadendal directly [IC]. *Inference:* the municipality cannot cut electricity to collect debt in Genadendal, because it does not sell it there.

**Debt by service.** No breakdown of Genadendal's debt between rates, water, sanitation and refuse was found [TBV].

**Two ways of being governed.** Greyton was a freehold village with title deeds, and a municipality from 1910 [IC]. Genadendal was run by the missionaries, then by boards under the 1909 Act, then from 1987 under Act 9 [IC]. Both came under Theewaterskloof Municipality in December 2000 [IC]. *Inference:* the Farm 39 settlements entered municipal government without owning their land, while Greyton entered with freehold title. See [[history/institutions/municipality|The Municipality]] and [[villages/next-door-different-rules|Next Door, Different Rules]].

**The elections of 4 November 2026.** The date was gazetted on 7 August 2026 [IC]. Under the Municipal Finance Management Act, "all revenue, expenditure and budget decisions must be taken within the framework of, and subject to the limitations of, the recovery plan" [IC]. The plan lists "lack of political and administrative stability" as its first risk [IC]. The ANC said debt had passed R400 million and Eskom debt had risen to more than R110 million [MS]. The DA said cash rose from R10.33 million to R34.33 million in one quarter [MS].

**The 2026 Community Needs List.** Its Ward 2 entries are set out in Section 2 [IC]. Housing, including Madiba Park, comes first, then services, flooding, roads and bridges, a safe house, and jobs for young people [IC].
