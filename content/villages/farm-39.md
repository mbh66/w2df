Farm 39 is the name used in law for the land on which [[villages/genadendal|Genadendal]], [[villages/bereaville|Bereaville]], [[villages/voorstekraal|Voorstekraal]], and [[villages/boschmanskloof|Boschmanskloof]] stand [MS]. [[villages/madiba-park|Madiba Park]] also stands on this land [MS]. Farm 39 is not a separate village. Residents seldom use the name, and it appears mainly in legislation and in the work of the Transformasie Komitee [MS].

## Why the name matters

Farm 39 is part of a process under the Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act of 1998, known as TRANCRAA [MS]. The process involves the proposed transfer of the land to a Communal Property Association, and it has not been completed [MS]. The Surveyor-General's records give Farm 39 an area of 4,498.09 hectares [IC]. Older documents give other figures, including about 4,800 hectares. See [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]]. The Transformasie Komitee is the community's mandated body for these matters [MS].

The status of the land also affects services. The municipality does not provide basic services in Madiba Park [MS].

## History of the land

- **1737 to 1857.** Georg Schmidt settled at Baviaanskloof in 1738, and the mission reopened in 1792 [IC]. In November 1857 the surveyor J. Kuys measured the mission land at 5,644 morgen and 289 square roods [MS].
- **1858.** The land was granted to the Moravian Church "for the use of and in trust for such persons as may from time to time be lawfully resident at the institution of Genadendal" [MS]. Balie's history says many residents rejected the grant because no resident was made a trustee [MS].
- **1899.** The church bought the mountain farm [[villages/farm-32-langgezocht|Langgezocht (Farm 32)]] to protect Farm 39's water sources [MS].
- **1909 and 1924.** The Mission Stations and Communal Reserves Act placed the land under a management board, and residents could live on it but not own it [IC]. In 1924 a secular board replaced the missionaries, and Proclamation 98 of 1924 described the extent of Genadendal [IC].
- **1927.** The Glebe Lands at the mission core were granted to the Moravian Church [IC]. They now form [[villages/farm-827-glebe-land|Farm 827]] [IC].
- **1952.** The state's deed of grant for Farm 39, G19/1952, gives an extent of 4,772.97 hectares, excluding the Glebe Land [IC].
- **1983 to 2001.** Township layouts were approved for Genadendal, Bereaville, Voorstekraal and Boschmanskloof, about 342 hectares in all [IC]. By 2007 eight erven, 206.2451 hectares, carried title deeds [IC].
- **1987 to 1989.** The Rural Areas Act 9 of 1987 took over the former mission areas, and Genadendal was added in 1989 [IC].
- **1998 to 2011.** TRANCRAA came into force on 2 November 1998 [IC]. The transfer process ran from 2002, and the transitional period ended in January 2011 without a transfer [IC].
- **2026.** The land outside the townships is still held in trust by the Minister, and no receiving entity has been recognised [MS].

## How this site uses the name

- Entries are listed under the settlement where they take place. Farm 39 is never used as a location.
- Entries about the land itself (its ownership, tenure, use, water rights, zoning, or services that depend on its status) carry the TRANCRAA theme and are reviewed by the Transformasie Komitee before they are published.
- This site describes the Komitee's positions only as its own materials state them.
