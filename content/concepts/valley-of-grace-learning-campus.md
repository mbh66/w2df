> **This is an idea, not a plan.** Nobody has agreed to it yet. It shows what the valley could build if we worked together. It will be tabled at the Forum's November 2026 meeting, and it will change as people respond.

## The goal

By 2038, every young person in Ward 2 has a path from school into study or work in the valley, paid for in large part by families from outside it, and governed for the whole ward.

## Why the valley needs it

- The valley has one high school, [[emil-weder-secondary|Emil Weder Secondary]] in Genadendal, which teaches in Afrikaans [IC]. English-speaking families send their children out of the valley for high school [MS].
- At the Forum's first meeting, farmers and tourism businesses said they need more skilled workers: mechanics, machine operators, and people with computer and hospitality skills [MS]. The meeting also asked for life skills [MS].
- There is no place in the valley where school leavers or adults can train for work in tourism and conservation [MS].
- The valley does not have enough English-speaking children to fill a new day school on its own [MS].

## The idea

A campus with four parts, sharing land, buildings, and staff:

| Part | What it is | Who it serves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An English-medium boarding school | A fee-paying school that rents its site on the campus. Its rent helps pay for the rest of the campus. | Families from across South Africa and beyond, plus valley learners on bursaries |
| A school of skills | A public school with a practical, hands-on curriculum | Valley learners who want a practical path |
| A vocational training centre | Courses and qualifications for work in tourism, conservation, and farming | School leavers, adults, and high school learners |
| A Bioregional Learning Centre | Further study and research about the valley's land, water, and heritage | Young people from all the valley's schools after they finish school |

Emil Weder stays as it is: the valley's Afrikaans high school, with its own heritage and governing body. Its learners would be welcome to use the training centre and the learning centre. Any link with the campus would be Emil Weder's choice.

## Who would pay

- The boarding school would pay rent and a fee for each learner into a campus fund. That fund would pay for bursaries for valley learners, the training centre, and the learning centre.
- The state would pay teachers at the school of skills and the cost of some training courses.
- Local employers would offer work placements, mentors, equipment, and jobs.
- Residents, donors, and companies would help pay for the buildings.

Nothing has been costed yet [MS].

## Who would decide

A trust representing the whole ward would own or hold the land and buildings and run the campus. Genadendal and Greyton would both be on its board. The boarding school would be a tenant. It could have one seat on the board, but never control it [MS].

## How it fits the Forum's goal

The Forum's [[long-term-goal|long-term goal]] groups the valley's life into three spheres, each with its own rule. The campus would follow all three:

- **Freedom (cultural).** Each school keeps its own language and character. The community decides what the campus is for.
- **Equality (civic).** The trust holds the campus for the whole ward. Every valley learner has the same access to the training centre and the learning centre. Bursary places go to learners from every village. No path is second class.
- **Cooperation (economic).** The boarding school hires and buys in the valley first. Employers work with the training centre as partners.

It also serves three of the Forum's [[priorities/index|priorities]]: [[priorities/education-skills|Education and Skills]], [[priorities/tourism-development|Tourism Development]], and [[priorities/conservation-environment|Conservation and Environment]]. The boarding school's kitchen and suppliers could also support [[priorities/agriculture-food-sovereignty|Agriculture and Food Sovereignty]] and [[priorities/business-procurement|Business Development and Local Procurement]].

## Learning from Bonnievale

In Bonnievale, the community built a public high school that opened in 2016 [IC]. It teaches academic subjects and practical skills on one campus, and a community trust holds half the seats on its governing body [IC]. The Western Cape Education Department helped pay for the buildings, and the town raised the rest together [TBV]. The team that set up that school has given early support to part of this idea [MS].

## What is not decided

- Where the campus would be. No site has been chosen.
- Whether families would pay to send their children to a boarding school here.
- Which organisation would run the boarding school.
- Whether the Western Cape Education Department would open a school of skills here.
- How the campus would work with Emil Weder, if Emil Weder wants to.
- What it would cost.

## What happens next

1. The idea is tabled at the Forum's November 2026 meeting.
2. The Transformasie Komitee and Emil Weder's governing body are asked for their views.
3. If the Forum wants to go further, it forms a working group to test whether the idea can work.

The first parts could start small. Training courses for adults could begin in 2027 or 2028 in borrowed rooms [MS]. A boarding school could open in 2030 at the earliest [TBV].
