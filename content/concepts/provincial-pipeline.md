The Western Cape Government plans its roads, water, hospitals and other infrastructure up to 2050, and keeps one list of the projects it wants to build, called the project pipeline. This page reads that pipeline from the valley's side. It asks what the province's plans will depend on that the valley can supply, starting with one project: a proposed new dam on the Breede River near Swellendam, called the Overberg Dam. The idea is that the valley comes to the province with an offer, and asks to be counted as part of the plan.

## The goal

> By 2038 the valley is written into the province's 2050 plans as a place that supplies something those plans depend on: water from healthy catchments, with the people and the records to prove it.

## Why the valley needs it

### The province plans to 2050, and keeps one list

In 2025 the province's Cabinet endorsed a draft Western Cape Infrastructure Framework 2050 [IC]. It sits with a strategy and an implementation plan for the same year [MS]. The implementation plan keeps a single pipeline of projects, sorted into five sectors: Social, Energy and Water, Economic, Technology, and Ecological [MS].

A project enters the pipeline in four steps [MS]:

1. The department looks for existing projects.
2. It ranks them against its list of sector priorities.
3. It tests them against a set of principles it calls the Panoptic Principles.
4. It packages the ones that pass into portfolios that banks and investors can fund.

Projects that pass get a one-page summary, called an elevator pitch, for investors [MS]. In July 2026 the Minister of Infrastructure said the pipeline held 52 priority projects worth R132 billion [MS]. Earlier in 2026 the province gave other totals: R131 billion in February, and R152 billion in the Premier's State of the Province Address [MS].

### Ward 2 is not on the list

The published pitches include three from Grabouw and Elgin, in the west of Theewaterskloof: Elgin Village, the Elgin City Sports Complex and the Grabouw Industrial Park [MS]. None names a place in Ward 2 [MS]. The pipeline list is updated as projects come in [MS].

Theewaterskloof Municipality is under a [[rules/theewaterskloof-financial-recovery-plan|Financial Recovery Plan]] overseen by the province [IC]. A municipality in that position has little room to bring new projects of its own. If the valley wants a place in the province's plans, it will probably have to show what it brings.

### The Overberg Dam

The first pitch under Energy and Water is the Overberg Dam, a "potential water security project in the Overberg District" [MS]. What the pitch says [MS]:

| Question | What the pitch says |
| --- | --- |
| Where | On the Breede River. Its map is headed "Proposed Swellendam Dam Site 3". |
| How big | About 123 million cubic metres on the map. The text gives the same number as an area. |
| Who is asking | The Overberg District Municipality, with the province's Department of Infrastructure. |
| Other parties named | Theewaterskloof, Swellendam, Cape Agulhas and Overstrand municipalities, private partners, and national government. |
| What it is for | Water security, more irrigated farming, agri-processing, tourism, logistics near the N2, and possibly hydroelectricity. |
| Bigger plan | Three dams in all. The Breede River site comes first. |
| What is asked for now | Approval and money for a pre-feasibility study. The full cost will only be known after a feasibility study. |
| Timeline | Water resource studies in 2025 and 2026. Feasibility, design and regulatory approvals in 2027 and 2028. Construction after that. |

The pitch also says that national authorities have confirmed there will be no new dams in the next decade, and presents the Overberg Dam as the district's answer to that [MS]. The project appeared in the province's Investment Summit deal book in October 2025 [MS]. In July 2026 the province named "the proposed new Overberg Dam" among "market-ready" projects at its first Infrastructure Ministerial Roundtable [MS].

No public document gives the exact site, the height of the wall or a completion date [IC]. For scale, Theewaterskloof Dam holds about 480 million cubic metres [IC]. The Overberg Dam, at 123 million, would hold about a quarter of that.

### The study will weigh more than water

The pitch says the pre-feasibility study will test whether the dam is viable, environmentally compliant and technically possible [MS]. It also says the study will show "value-for-money and inclusive returns across six capitals" [MS]. The pitch does not list the six. Frameworks that use the term usually count money, built things, people's skills, social ties, knowledge and nature [TBV]. A study that counts nature and people has to look at the land the water comes from, and at the people who live there.

### The province wants water from catchments too

The province's Water Resilience Strategy, launched in October 2025, aims to secure an extra 310 million cubic metres of water a year by 2035 [IC]. Ecological restoration is one of the ways it lists to add water [MS]. In August 2026 the Premier's office said removing invasive plants from waterways is a key part of the strategy, with two projects under way through the Department of Agriculture [MS].

The pipeline's Ecological sector has its own pitches, including one called Ecological Infrastructure Investment and one for a Provincial Public Offset Bank [MS]. Ecological infrastructure means working landscapes, such as mountain catchments, wetlands and rivers, that deliver services the way a pipe or a dam does [TBV].

### The valley's water reaches the Breede

The Gobos and Baviaans rivers run from the valley's mountains into the Riviersonderend, below Theewaterskloof Dam [IC]. The Riviersonderend joins the Breede just upstream from Swellendam [IC]. The "Swellendam Dam Site 3" lies downstream of the river confluence, which means the valley's water would flow into the new dam [TBV].

A 2016 study estimated that invasive plants take more than 6% of the natural flow of rivers in the Breede and neighbouring catchments [TBV]. Clearing them puts water back into the streams [IC].

### One plan, one budget

The province and its districts plan together through the Joint District and Metro Approach, which aims at "One Plan, One Budget and One Implementation" [MS]. In September 2026 the Overberg was one of six groups that met to list priority projects, quick wins and projects needing provincial support [MS]. The Overberg Dam pitch calls the dam a "flagship" intervention under this approach [MS].

## The idea

### Come with an offer

The BioConomy work describes two ways a place can approach government and investors [MS]. A needy place asks for help. A needed place offers something others already depend on, and proves that it delivers. See [Needed vs. Needy](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/glossary/m-s/needed-vs-needy).

The province's pipeline is a list of what the province will depend on leading up to 2050. The idea is to read it closely and find where the valley can supply something. The valley would then offer that supply through the Forum, with numbers.

### What the valley could offer the Overberg Dam

If the valley's streams feed the new dam, the valley would hold part of its catchment. The valley could offer:

- **Water in the river.** Restored mountain catchments, cleared of invasive trees, could send more water down the Gobos and the Baviaans. This is the same work set out in [[concepts/from-water-access-to-water-agency|From Water Access to Water Agency]] and [[concepts/restoring-the-river|Restoring the River]].
- **Measured results.** The valley would measure its own streams and publish the records, so that the study and any later investor can see what the catchment gives.
- **Trained people.** The [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Learning Campus]] would train young people to clear, restore and measure. A dam that takes ten years to plan and build will need that work for its whole life.
- **A partner on the ground.** The Forum would be one body that the study's team could talk to about Ward 2, with the [[concepts/the-yearly-valley-survey|Yearly Valley Survey]] as its evidence about the people.

The ask would be modest at first: that the valley be named a stakeholder in the pre-feasibility study, and that the study count the catchments above the dam as part of the project.

### Beyond the dam

The same reading could be done for every sector of the pipeline. Two pitches look closest to the valley's work:

- **Ecological Infrastructure Investment.** The valley's mountains are ecological infrastructure for the Riviersonderend and the Breede.
- **Provincial Public Offset Bank.** When a project damages nature in one place, an offset pays for nature to be protected or restored somewhere else. Whether land in or near the valley could ever be part of such a bank is unknown [TBV]. Any offer about land on Farm 39 would go to the Transformasie Komitee first.

### 2038 inside 2050

The Forum's [[long-term-goal|long-term goal]] looks to 2038, when Genadendal turns 300. The province looks to 2050. The dam's planning years, 2025 to 2028, fall inside the valley's own planning years. That gives the valley a reason to bring its 2038 goal into the province's plans now, while the study is being set up.

## Who would benefit

- **Workers in the valley,** who could be paid to restore and measure the catchments.
- **Landowners,** who could be paid for caring for land that feeds the river.
- **The province and the dam's sponsors,** who would have a partner in the catchment and records to show investors.
- **Farmers and towns downstream,** if more water reaches the river in dry months.

## Who would pay

Nothing has been costed. The dam itself would be paid for by government and investors, once a feasibility study sets the cost [MS]. The valley's part could be paid for in three ways:

- As part of the dam project's budget for its catchment.
- Through provincial programmes for ecological infrastructure and invasive plant clearing.
- By buyers of measured water results, such as those described on [[organisations/how-the-cape-water-bond-works|How the Cape Water Bond Works]].

## Who would decide

- The Western Cape Government decides what enters its pipeline and how projects are packaged [MS].
- The Overberg District Municipality is the sponsor named on the Overberg Dam pitch [MS].
- Any dam needs approval from the Department of Water and Sanitation under the [[rules/national-water-act|National Water Act]], including a water use licence [IC].
- The Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency manages water use in the Breede catchment [MS].
- Theewaterskloof Municipality would be the valley's route into the district plan.
- The Forum would decide whether to make the offer. Anything on Farm 39 land, or involving Farm 39's water, would go to the Transformasie Komitee first.

## How it fits the Forum's goal

This idea follows the three values of the [[long-term-goal|long-term goal]]:

- **Cooperation (economic).** The valley offers a service the province's plans depend on.
- **Equality (civic).** Ward 2 asks for the same place in provincial planning that Grabouw and Elgin already have.
- **Freedom (cultural).** The valley speaks for its own land and learns the skills to care for it.

It serves three of the Forum's [[priorities/index|priorities]]: [[priorities/conservation-environment|Conservation and Environment]], [[priorities/education-skills|Education and Skills]] and [[priorities/business-procurement|Business Development and Local Procurement]].

## What is still open

- **Where Site 3 is.** Whether the dam would sit on the Breede below the point where the Riviersonderend joins it. If it sits above, the valley's water would not reach it, and this page's offer to the dam falls away.
- **The size.** The pitch gives 123 million in its text as an area and on its map as a volume. The pitch's own figures need checking with the sponsor.
- **The timeline.** The third stage on the pitch reads "2020-Onwards", which may be a printing error. No completion date has been published.
- **"Market-ready" and "potential".** The province called the dam market-ready in July 2026, while the pitch still calls it a potential project awaiting a pre-feasibility study.
- **The other two dams.** The pitch mentions three dams. Where the other two would be, and whether either is on the Riviersonderend, is not known. The Overberg Water Board has also planned new storage dams at Buffeljags and Duivenhoks, and how these relate to the Overberg Dam is not known [MS].
- **The estuary and the Reserve.** The Breede estuary at Witsand receives about 42% of its natural flow [MS]. A new dam on the Breede would take more water out of the river. How the dam would meet the Reserve set for the river and estuary, and whether water from restored catchments could help, has not been studied.
- **The Panoptic Principles.** The province has not published what they are, so the valley cannot yet test its own offer against them.
- **Who in the valley would make the offer.** The Forum, a valley body, or Theewaterskloof Municipality on the valley's behalf.
- **Each named party's view.** None of the bodies listed at the top of this page has been asked.

## What happens next

1. The idea is tabled at the Forum's November 2026 meeting, with the other water ideas.
2. The moderators ask the province's Department of Infrastructure for the full Overberg Dam pitch, the district's July 2026 presentation, and the current pipeline list.
3. The Forum asks the Overberg District Municipality where Site 3 is, and whether the catchments of the Riviersonderend are in the study area.
4. If Site 3 lies below the Riviersonderend, the Forum asks through Theewaterskloof Municipality and the district plan to be named a stakeholder in the pre-feasibility study.
5. The valley begins measuring its streams, so that it has its own records when the study starts.
6. The Forum reads the rest of the pipeline, sector by sector, for other places where the valley can offer something.
