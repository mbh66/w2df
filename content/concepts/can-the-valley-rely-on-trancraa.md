> **This idea is not yet agreed.** Nobody named on this page has been asked about it, including the Genadendal Transformasie Komitee, Theewaterskloof Municipality and the Department of Land Reform and Rural Development. It goes to the Komitee in writing first, and to a DevForum meeting once the Komitee responds or 21 days after it was sent, whichever comes first. At that meeting, anyone can question it, change it or reject it. It concerns how the DevForum plans its own work. It takes no part in the TRANCRAA process, which the DevForum agreed on 16 September 2026 to leave to the bodies already dealing with it.

## The goal

> The DevForum would decide, from the record of all 23 TRANCRAA areas, what its own plans for Farm 39 land should assume about a transfer, and would check that assumption every year.

## Why the valley needs it

The Remainder of Farm 39 is held in trust by the national Minister [IC]. Genadendal, Bereaville, Voorstekraal, Boschmanskloof and Madiba Park stand on it [IC]. The Surveyor-General records 4,498.09 hectares [IC]. Its transitional period ran from July 2009 to January 2011 and ended with no transfer [IC].

Under the Act, trust land not transferred by the end of the period vests in the Minister, "who may continue to hold such land in trust and may at any time thereafter dispose of that land in accordance with the principles of this Act" (section 3(13)) [IC]. So the law still allows a transfer at any time. It sets no date by which the Minister must decide [IC]. No receiving entity has been recognised [MS].

The municipality's 2019 spatial plan says it is "not allowed to invest in infrastructure" on land it does not own [IC]. In 2024 it said it could not give Madiba Park full services because the settlement is not on municipal land [MS]. See [[civic/residents-association/madiba-park-land-and-services|Madiba Park: Land Status and Basic Services]].

Several proposals would use Farm 39 land: the [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Valley of Grace Learning Campus]], the [[concepts/valley-of-grace-agri-heritage-co-operative|Valley of Grace Agri-Heritage Co-operative]], the [[concepts/tourism-growth-fund-application|Valley of Grace Heritage Trail]] and the [[concepts/community-built-dams-on-the-baviaans-and-gobos|community-built dams on the Baviaans and Gobos]]. Each assumes something about when the land will change hands, and most do not say what.

## What the record shows

TRANCRAA covers 23 areas: 12 in the Western Cape, 8 in the Northern Cape, 1 in the Eastern Cape and 2 in the Free State [IC]. The full record, area by area, is on [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-national-record|TRANCRAA: The National Record]].

| Status in November 2026 | Areas |
|---|---|
| Dated transfer on record | Haarlem (2020) [IC], Mamre [IC], Ebenhaeser (largest portion, 2020/21) [IC], Enon/Bersheba (by 2023, 8,314 ha) [IC]; Zoar (2023/24, 5,665 ha) reported [TBV] |
| Title deeds listed or reported for August 2025, extent unclear | Pella, Concordia, Leliefontein, Steinkopf, Komaggas, Eksteenskuil [IC]; Richtersveld [TBV] |
| No transfer found | Genadendal, Kranshoek, Slangrivier, Pniel, Saron, Suurbraak, Friemersheim, Rietpoort, Mier, Oppermansgronde, Thaba Phatsoa |

- **Time.** The earliest firmly dated transfer, Haarlem in February 2020, came more than 21 years after the Act [IC]. One source dates Mamre to 2013, which conflicts with the Department's date of 2020/21 [TBV].
- **Hectares.** On 30 October 2024 the Department told Parliament's portfolio committee that 603,000 of the 1.4 million hectares in the 23 areas had been transferred [IC]. Its figures that same year also included 473,938 and 599,000 hectares [IC].
- **Targets.** In 2021/22 the Department aimed to transfer 11 areas and transferred none [IC]. In 2025 Parliament's committee found it had set no TRANCRAA target [IC]. For 2026/27 it set 13,077 hectares, without naming areas [IC].
- **Where processes stop.** Most stalled areas stopped when an entity had to be agreed, or when a municipality or Public Works had to release land [IC].
- **Causes the Department gives.** Parliament reported on 31 October 2024 that the Department lacked dedicated staff for the programme, that municipalities did not pass council resolutions or sign transfer documents, and that communities disputed which land should be transferred [IC].
- **Causes others give.** Researchers in Namaqualand found the time and money needed were "seriously under-estimated" [IC]. The Ward 2 Forum's 2017 series *Farm 39 Transformation: The Lost 18 Years* gives the valley's own account [MS]. It says the Act was passed without defining the areas or setting objectives for community benefit [MS]. It says the consultants' 2010 report was incomplete on existing rights and land transfers, and holds no record of residents being told what each choice of entity would mean for them [MS]. It also says the Department underplayed its own role in the process [MS].
- **After transfer.** In October 2023 the Department was helping the Enon CPA elect a new committee [IC]. In 2026 Steinkopf residents disputed what their deed covers [MS]. The Department told Parliament that 82% of all 1,742 CPAs were not complying with the law in 2023/24 [IC]. The Ward 2 Forum reported over 1,300 of 1,483 CPAs as non-compliant in 2015/16 [MS]. Both figures cover all CPAs, most created by other land reform programmes, and cannot be read as TRANCRAA figures.
- **Individual rights.** The Ward 2 Forum's series argues for each household to hold title it can use as security for loans [MS]. No record was found of any resident in a transferred TRANCRAA area holding registered individual rights or using land as security [TBV].

## The test and how the evidence meets it

The test was set before the evidence was gathered, using the Act's own time limits, the Department's own targets and what Parliament has been told.

| Criterion | What the record shows | Strength of evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Completion | 5 to 12 of 23 areas have some transfer; 43% of hectares by October 2024 | Moderate |
| Time | 15 to 27 years after 1998, against an 18-month period; targets missed | Strong |
| Steps | Most stalled at the entity decision or at land release | Moderate |
| After transfer | Governance trouble on record; no sign of individual rights or investment | Thin |
| Remedies | No court case moved a stalled process | Moderate |
| Direction | No target in 2025/26, one in 2026/27; a communal land tenure bill is pending | Moderate |

The after-transfer criterion assumed records would exist. Few do, so the finding there is thin.

## The idea

The DevForum would plan on reading B: a transfer of Farm 39 may come, and nobody can name the year. Before any proposal on Farm 39 land is tabled, it would state:

1. Which piece of land it uses, and whether that land is township land or the Remainder.
2. What it assumes about who owns that land now and who may own it later.
3. What it can do while the land stays in trust, and whose written permission that needs.
4. What happens to the work if no transfer comes within ten years.

Each year the DevForum would check the signs listed below and record whether reading B still holds.

The other two readings, and what would have to be true for each:

- **Reading A** (plan for a transfer within a few years) would need a recognised entity for Farm 39 and a Minister's decision published in the Gazette.
- **Reading C** (plan for the land to stay in trust for the foreseeable future) would need transfers elsewhere to stop, or the Department to say that Farm 39 is off its plans.

## Who would benefit

Residents asked to back a proposal on Farm 39 land would see what it assumes about the land before they decide. Champions, partners and funders would see the land risk stated in the proposal itself, and could plan for delay from the start.

## The money

Nothing has been costed. The only cost is the yearly check, which volunteers could do from public records.

## Who would decide

The DevForum decides its own planning reading, after the Komitee's written response. This page names no party as deciding anything in the TRANCRAA process itself.

## The signs to watch

- A Gazette notice or Minister's decision on Farm 39.
- A CPA registration for Genadendal in the Department's CPA annual report.
- A TRANCRAA target naming Genadendal in the Department's Annual Performance Plan.
- The Department's hectare total in its annual report.
- Any change to the 2008 High Court order.
- How the coming communal land tenure bill treats TRANCRAA.

## What is still open

- Which areas make up the Department's hectare totals.
- Whether Mamre was transferred in 2013 or 2020/21.
- Whether any TRANCRAA CPA meets its legal duties.
- Whether a Genadendal CPA is registered. A Komitee office-bearer said in 2024 it was waiting on the Department [MS].
- What became of the interdict the Ward 2 Forum says the Komitee sought in 2010 against the consultants' report going to the Minister [MS].
- Whether the 2008 settlement kept the consultants on or provided for their withdrawal. The order lets them keep their contract under a new steering committee [IC]. The Ward 2 Forum's series says they agreed to withdraw [MS].
- The views of the Komitee, Theewaterskloof Municipality and the Department on this page. None has been asked.

## What happens next

1. The moderators send this page to the Genadendal Transformasie Komitee in writing.
2. The page is tabled at a DevForum meeting once the Komitee responds, or 21 days after it was sent.
3. The meeting decides whether to adopt reading B, change it or reject it.
4. If it is adopted, proposals on Farm 39 land state the four points above before they are tabled.
5. The DevForum checks the signs each year and records the result on this page.
