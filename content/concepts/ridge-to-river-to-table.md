> **This idea is not yet agreed.** It draws together eight ideas already on this site, and none of those are agreed either. It answers a concept paper by Ranyaka Community Transformation, and will be discussed with Ranyaka at a meeting at the end of October 2026 [MS]. Everything in it that touches Farm 39 land goes to the Transformasie Komitee in writing first. Kwaxala has said it wants to work with the valley on water (see [[concepts/from-water-access-to-water-agency|From Water Access to Water Agency]]). Rand Merchant Bank (RMB) and the other organisations named on this page have not been approached. They include the Cape Leopard Trust, the Landmark Foundation, CapeNature, the Department of Water and Sanitation, the Breede-Olifants Catchment Management Agency, the Zonderend Water Users Association, Wild Restoration, the Greyton Conservation Society, Emil Weder Secondary's governing body, and Theewaterskloof Municipality. **Please do not share this page publicly yet.** The idea will be tabled at the Forum's November 2026 meeting.

## The goal

> By 2038, the valley earns a living from the mountain slopes and the rivers, and each rand invested in it makes the next rand easier to raise.

## Why the valley needs it

### Eight good ideas, eight separate asks

[[concepts/index|Ideas for the Valley]] lists eight ideas [MS]. Seven of them have not been costed [MS]. Each names its own funders and partners, and each would report in its own way. If each is funded on its own, the valley would build the same things eight times over: a coordinator, a bank account, a set of records, a round of village meetings, and a report to a funder.

The ideas also lean on each other. [[concepts/restoring-the-river|Restoring the River]] depends on the [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Learning Campus]] for training. The [[concepts/overberg-leopard-corridor|Overberg Leopard Corridor]] depends on the same clearing crews for camera work. The [[concepts/valley-of-grace-agri-heritage-co-operative|Agri-Heritage Co-operative]] and the [[concepts/tourism-growth-fund-application|Heritage Trail]] depend on the same visitors. [[concepts/the-yearly-valley-survey|The Yearly Valley Survey]] would measure them all.

### A third way of investing in places

In September 2026 Ranyaka Community Transformation, a non-profit working in Stellenbosch and other Western Cape towns [MS], wrote a concept paper called *A Third Way of Investing in Places* [MS]. The paper makes these points [MS]:

- Money for development in South Africa is organised by institutions, programmes, and funding streams, and seldom around a place.
- Communities are fragmented too. Local groups are often poorly connected to each other and to the institutions that hold resources.
- The paper proposes a third way. The place itself would become the platform that joins these two systems, supported by a thin shared layer of place intelligence and professional capability, with technology as the enabler.
- Early modelling of an existing R8.5 million programme suggests that buying the same functions separately might cost R11.5 million to R14 million. The paper says this still has to be tested.
- It proposes following each R100 from the funder to the place, and asking what remains once it is spent, including whether the place is now better able to raise the next R100.

Ranyaka is exploring Stellenbosch as its first demonstration place, with Nedbank and local partners, around an indicative budget of R4.5 million a year [MS]. The paper names Stanford and Genadendal/Greyton as other areas it is looking at [MS].

### A guide for landscapes

The [1000 Landscapes for 1 Billion People](https://landscapes.global/) initiative, founded by Commonland, Conservation International, EcoAgriculture Partners, the Landscape Finance Lab, Rainforest Alliance, Tech Matters and UNDP, publishes a practical guide to integrated landscape management [MS]. [The guide](https://landscapes.global/guide/) sets out five elements: a landscape partnership, shared understanding, vision and planning, taking action, and impact and learning [MS]. It says a full process needs a timeframe of at least 20 years [MS]. Its centrepiece is a landscape investment portfolio of two kinds of investment: asset investments, which produce direct benefits, and enabling investments, which make the asset investments possible [MS].

### A buyer for the valley's water already exists

In April 2026 FirstRand listed a R2.5 billion bond on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange that pays investors partly according to checked results from clearing invasive plants in Cape Town's catchments [IC]. RMB, part of FirstRand, arranged and structured it [MS]. The Nature Conservancy and the Greater Cape Town Water Fund carry out the work, and Conservation Alpha checks it independently [MS]. RMB describes the bond as the first in a series for South Africa's strategic water source areas [MS]. The valley is not directly part of it, but does play a downstream role below Theewaterskloof Dam [MS].

> [!important] A drop of water in the river below the valley is a drop the dam can keep. 
> Theewaterskloof Dam sends its water two ways: west through a tunnel to Cape Town, and east down the Riviersonderend to farmers, the Overberg towns, and the river itself [IC]. By law, the river must keep enough water to stay healthy down to its estuary at Witsand. This share is called the Reserve [IC]. Cape Town's households and businesses pay for the water they use. The people putting this idea forward say nobody pays for the water released to meet the Reserve [MS].
>
> If the valley's restored wetlands and soils hold the winter rain and let it out slowly through the dry months, the river below the valley would carry more water when the dam is releasing. Where that water meets part of the river's needs, the dam could release less and keep more for Cape Town. The valley would then be supplying water that someone pays for, and it could be paid for each cubic metre it adds, once the flow is measured and checked.
>
> This works only if the dam's operators agree to count the valley's water, and only for water that arrives below the valley in the months when the dam is releasing. The full argument, and what is still open, is at [[concepts/from-water-access-to-water-agency#How the valley's water could ease the dam|From Water Access to Water Agency]].

### One mountain slope holds all four strands

- **The ridge.** Leopard has been recorded on trail cameras at [[wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]]'s clearing sites in the Riviersonderend mountains [MS]. The same mountains feed the Baviaans and Gobos rivers, which join the Riviersonderend below Theewaterskloof Dam [IC].
- **The valley floor.** Greyton's leiwater furrows have carried water from the Gobos to plots since 1854 [MS]. Growers in Genadendal have proposed a co-operative [MS].
- **The villages.** The valley holds what is described as the oldest school in South Africa, and the country's first teachers' college opened at Genadendal in 1838 [IC]. Genadendal turns 300 in 2038 [IC].

The ILM guide asks a landscape to plan for three kinds of area: natural habitat, production, and settlements [MS]. In this valley they lie one above the other on the same slope.

## The idea

### One slope, four uses

The valley would ask investors to fund one place in four linked strands. Picture one hectare on the mountain cleared of pines. It gives water to the river and cover to a leopard. It gives a guide a trail to walk visitors along, and it gives a Grade 10 class a field site. The same hectare earns four returns.

| Part of the slope | Strand | What it would do | Where the detail is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The ridge | Leopard corridor | A camera survey of the valley's mountains, trained local camera technicians and guides, and a visitor plan built around leopards | [[concepts/overberg-leopard-corridor\|The Overberg Leopard Corridor]] |
| The ridge and the streams | Water retention | Clearing, wetland and spring restoration, gauging the streams, and preparing the valley to be paid for the water it adds to the river | [[concepts/restoring-the-river\|Restoring the River]] and [[concepts/from-water-access-to-water-agency\|From Water Access to Water Agency]] |
| The valley floor and the villages | Agri-heritage tourism | A growers' co-operative with a farm shop and kitchen, heritage homes and guides, and one trail linking Greyton and the mission settlements | [[concepts/valley-of-grace-agri-heritage-co-operative\|Agri-Heritage Co-operative]] and [[concepts/tourism-growth-fund-application\|Valley of Grace Heritage Trail]] |
| The whole slope | Learning | A training centre that turns the other three strands into places of work for learners, and a costed plan for the full Learning Campus | [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus\|Valley of Grace Learning Campus]] |

### The leopard corridor links two known areas

The Cape Leopard Trust surveyed the Boland mountains, west of the valley, in 2025 and identified 38 leopards [MS]. To the south, the Trust, the Endangered Wildlife Trust and the Grootbos Foundation are building the Agulhas Green Corridor, which joins the Walker Bay Protected Environment to Agulhas National Park and the Nuwejaars area [MS]. No published survey covers the mountains between Villiersdorp, Genadendal and Riviersonderend [TBV].

The corridor strand would treat the valley's mountains as the missing middle piece between the Boland and the Agulhas corridor. The camera survey paid for in this investment would test whether leopards use it that way.

### The shared place layer

Ranyaka's paper describes a thin shared layer that holds knowledge of the place and the skills to turn money into lasting value [MS]. The valley already has parts of that layer:

- **Knowledge of the place.** This site's [[register|Register]] records initiatives in the ward with what each needs and offers [MS]. The Valley of Grace Nature Realm has covered about 10,000 hectares since July 2026, with records of plants, animals and heritage [MS].
- **A landscape partnership.** The Forum first met on 16 September 2026, agreed five priorities, and receives administrative support from Theewaterskloof Municipality [MS].
- **A way to measure change.** The Forum plans a household [[w2df/concepts/the-yearly-valley-survey|Annual Survey]] from 2027 [MS]. Its questions on where money is spent would show how much of each rand stays in the valley.

What the valley lacks is the professional side: contracting, financial management, independent monitoring, and a registered body able to sign a funding agreement.

The proposal is that Ranyaka would host these functions for the first three years, under a written agreement, and hand them over step by step to bodies in the valley as they form. The Agri-Heritage Co-operative, the trust proposed for the Learning Campus, and a clearing enterprise are the likely candidates. The handover schedule would be published on this site, and each year's report would show which functions had moved.

### Three layers of money

1. **An enabling grant, years one to three.** This would pay for the shared layer: coordination, the yearly survey, stream gauges, the cost of consulting the Komitee and each village, independent monitoring, and the finance work that prepares the water strand.
2. **Asset investment, grant and concessional.** This would pay for the heritage trail and guide training, the co-operative's farm shop and kitchen, the training centre's first courses, and the leopard camera survey.
3. **Outcomes finance, from year three.** The first two layers would get the valley ready for a later issue in RMB's series of [water bonds](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/performance-based-water-bonds): measured flows, a method an independent checker accepts, signed stewardship agreements, and a trained local team. Repayable money would come in only once there is a checked result to pay for. Kwaxala, which has said it wants to turn water rights into rights to regenerate flow, could invest alongside [MS].

The ask to RMB would therefore join grant money with the markets work RMB has already done on the Cape Water bond.

### The Learning Campus in this investment

The full [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Learning Campus]] would include an English-medium boarding school whose rent helps pay for the rest of the campus. The boarding school is a building project with its own investors, so it sits outside this ask. This investment would pay for the parts that can start first: the training centre in borrowed rooms, a registered course from clearing to contract, training for survey fieldworkers, and a costed feasibility study for the whole campus, including the boarding school.

### How the investment would be measured

The investment would report against the seven tests Ranyaka's paper proposes for a demonstration place [MS], in valley terms:

| Test | What the valley would measure |
| --- | --- |
| Cost | The cost of delivering four strands through one shared layer, against a costed estimate of buying the same work separately |
| Access | How many residents and valley enterprises reach a grant, a contract, a buyer, or a training place |
| Coordination | How many initiatives on the Register share data, staff, sites, or equipment |
| Localisation | The share of spending that reaches valley suppliers and workers, and the functions handed from Ranyaka to valley bodies each year |
| Capital formation | Change in the seven kinds of local capital the paper names (human, organisational, economic, social, physical, knowledge and natural), village by village |
| Regeneration | Whether the first years' gauging and stewardship make the step to outcomes finance cheaper and quicker |
| Technology | The cost of reporting for each rand invested, using the Register, the Nature Realm, and the phone survey |

Each year the valley would publish a R100 Resource Journey on this site: of every R100 committed, how much reached the valley, how much reached local people and businesses, and what remains.

## Who would benefit

- **Young people from every village**, trained as clearing and monitoring crews, camera technicians, guides, and survey fieldworkers.
- **Growers and heritage home hosts**, through a shared shop and kitchen, and through visitors who stay overnight.
- **Landowners and farmers**, through paid stewardship, more water in the ground, and a plan for leopards that take livestock.
- **Water users downstream**, including farmers along the Riviersonderend and, through Theewaterskloof Dam, Cape Town water users.
- **Funders**, who would see one place-based record of what their money did.
- **Leopards and fynbos**, through connected habitat and fewer invasive trees.

## The money

Nothing has been costed [MS]. The figures below are indicative. They show the scale of the ask and would be replaced by a costed plan.

| Strand | What three years would pay for | Indicative figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared place layer | Coordinator and finance lead, three rounds of the yearly survey, upkeep of the Register and data, consultation with the Komitee and each village, independent monitoring, and the yearly R100 report | R3.6 million |
| Water retention | Gauges and a baseline study on the Baviaans and Gobos, clearing with the valley's existing teams, wetland and spring restoration, design of a checking method, and stewardship agreements | R6.0 million |

| Agri-heritage tourism | The co-operative's registration and first year, a farm shop and kitchen, and heritage home upgrades and guide training, building on the Heritage Trail application | R3.9 million |
| Learning | Start-up of the training centre, a registered course from clearing to contract, training for survey fieldworkers, and a costed feasibility study for the Learning Campus | R3.6 million |
| **Total** | | **About R19.5 million over three years** |

Other money already requested or open to the valley would sit beside this:

- The Heritage Trail application asks the Western Cape Tourism Growth Fund for R691,350 of a project costing about R1.8 million [MS]. The Rotary Club of Greyton has budgeted R70,000 towards it [MS].
- SETA grants and learner stipends, once courses are registered.
- The Comprehensive Agricultural Support Programme, for the co-operative's growers.
- Theewaterskloof Municipality's administrative support to the Forum [MS].
- Outcomes payments for water, from year three, if the valley's water can be counted.

## Who would decide

- **The Forum**, whether to put this idea to RMB, and which strands to include.
- **The Transformasie Komitee**, first, for anything on [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] land: stewardship agreements, trails, camera sites, co-operative plots, and water.
- **Each strand's own decision-makers**, as set out on its page: the co-operative's members, the Learning Campus trust, landowners, CapeNature for its reserves, and the water authorities for anything that touches water rights.
- **Ranyaka**, under a written agreement, for the functions it would host, until they are handed over.
- **RMB and any other funder**, for what they fund and on what terms.

## How it fits the Forum's goal

The Forum's [[long-term-goal|long-term goal]] plans a [BioConomy](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/bioregional-economics) that keeps more of what the valley has in the valley: money, skills, water in the ground, and healthy soil. This idea would pay for the things the goal says nobody pays for today, such as clean rivers and healthy veld, and would measure whether more value stays.

It follows the three values of the long-term goal:

- **Cooperation (economic).** Growers, crews, guides, and landowners work to one plan and share one support layer.
- **Equality (civic).** Every village has a route into the work; the survey reports each village on its own, and the Komitee decides first on Farm 39 land.
- **Freedom (cultural).** The valley tells its own story of its land and heritage, and decides what its learners study.

It serves all five of the Forum's [[priorities/index|priorities]]: [[priorities/tourism-development|Tourism Development]], [[priorities/education-skills|Education and Skills]], [[priorities/agriculture-food-sovereignty|Agriculture and Food Sovereignty]], [[priorities/conservation-environment|Conservation and Environment]], and [[priorities/business-procurement|Business Development and Local Procurement]].

## What is still open

- **The Komitee's view.** Much of the ground in this idea is Farm 39 land in the unfinished [[trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA]] process. Stewardship agreements, co-operative plots, trails and camera sites all depend on the [[genadendal-transformasie-komitee|Transformasie Komitee]]. It has not been asked.
- **The eight ideas themselves.** Each goes to the Forum on its own. If one is dropped or changed in November, this idea changes with it.
- **Ranyaka's view.** Whether Ranyaka would host the shared layer, on what terms, and how the handover would work. To be discussed at the end of October.
- **RMB.** RMB has not been approached.
- **Counting the water.** The Cape Water bond pays for water that reaches the dams supplying Cape Town. The valley's streams join the river below Theewaterskloof. Whether that water can be counted, and whether the valley's slopes fall inside the Boland strategic water source area, has not been confirmed [TBV]. The Department of Water and Sanitation and the catchment agency have not been asked.
- **Leopard movement.** Whether leopards move between the Boland and the Agulhas corridor through the valley's mountains is unknown. The researchers working on each area have not been asked.
- **A body to sign in the valley.** Which valley body would take over contracting, and when.
- **The figures.** Every figure on this page needs costing.
- **Other partners.** The views of CapeNature, the Landmark Foundation, the Zonderend Water Users Association, Wild Restoration, the Greyton Conservation Society, Emil Weder Secondary's governing body, and Theewaterskloof Municipality. None has been asked.
