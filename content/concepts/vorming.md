*Vorming* is the Afrikaans word for *formation*. This page proposes it as the name for the valley's own variation of *Bildung*, the German word for the way a person forms themselves over a lifetime, taking on wider responsibility as they grow. Vorming would be rooted in this valley. Its school would be the land itself, and the work of bringing its rivers and mountain slopes back to life. It responds to the record on the [[challenges|Challenges]] page, and it is the design principle behind [[concepts/the-palmiet-principle|The Palmiet Principle]].

## The goal

> By 2038, when Genadendal turns 300, people across the valley are being formed by caring for it, and the valley's restored rivers and slopes show the change in them.

## What Bildung means

The writers of *The Nordic Secret*, Lene Rachel Andersen and Tomas Björkman, describe Bildung as "the way that the individual matures and takes upon him- or herself ever bigger personal responsibility towards family, friends, fellow citizens, society, humanity, our globe, and the global heritage of our species, while enjoying ever bigger personal, moral and existential freedoms" [MS]. They call it "a constant process that never ends" [MS].

Jonathan Rowson, writing for the Centre for the Understanding of Sustainable Prosperity in 2019, translates it as "transformative civic education" [MS]. He writes that it "entails a dynamic world view that values independence of mind and spirit grounded in ecological and social interdependence" [MS].

Rowson describes how Denmark, Norway and Sweden spread Bildung from the 1850s through folk high schools, set up by teachers, pastors, farmers and others who wanted to educate the poor [MS]. By 1900 there were about 100 in Denmark, 75 in Norway and 150 in Sweden [MS]. Courses ran for three to six months in small groups of 20 to 40 people, where conversation mattered as much as instruction, and they included practical lessons in farming [MS]. Rowson suggests that this may have helped build the high levels of social trust in those countries today [MS].

Rowson also gives a warning. Bildung has a past tied to religion and nationalism, and he writes that it "would have to be reinterpreted and perhaps renamed for our fractious times" [MS]. He holds that its stress on "roots, place, and belonging is fundamentally sound" [MS]. That is the opening this page uses.

The general idea, and how it relates to a place-based economy, is set out on the BioConomy wiki: [Bildung: a BioConomy perspective](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/bildung).

## Formation is not new here

The valley has its own history of people forming themselves and each other, often against the odds.

- **Magdalena.** For 48 years after [[history/people/georg-schmidt|Georg Schmidt]] left, there was no missionary at Baviaanskloof [IC]. [[history/people/magdalena|Magdalena]] kept the small congregation together and read to them from the Dutch New Testament he had left her [IC].
- **The first school and the first college.** A school opened in 1793 that still runs today as [[cultural/education/lr-schmidt-primary|L.R. Schmidt Primary]] [MS]. South Africa's first teachers' training college opened in Genadendal in 1838 [IC].
- **The college closed.** In 1926 the government closed it, arguing that coloured people did not need higher education because they were suited to work on farms [IC]. It had trained 236 teachers [MS]. See [[history/from-farm-to-classroom|From Farm to Classroom]].
- **The removals.** Under the Group Areas Act, coloured families in Greyton, many of whom owned plots and small farms, were forced to sell and leave [IC]. Most were moved to [[villages/heuwelkroon|Heuwelkroon]] [IC].

Each of these shows a community forming itself, or being denied the chance. Vorming would build on the first and take account of the rest.

## Why the valley needs it

The [[challenges|Challenges]] page shows that the valley's most difficult challenges sit in people and in their relationships, as much as in land and budgets.

- **How residents scored the valley.** In the 2024 [[organisations/ranyaka-community-transformation|Ranyaka]] sessions, residents gave the valley an overall score of 48% [MS]. Social cohesion scored highest, and well-being ("healthy, educated and active") lowest [MS].
- **What the Forum heard.** At its [[civic/governance/minutes/2026-09-16-inaugural-meeting|first meeting]], several sectors asked for "life skills, communication, respect and violence prevention" [MS]. Farmers asked for "mentorship between commercial and emerging farmers" [MS].
- **Young people.** Across the municipality, only 71.6% of learners stayed from Grade 10 to Grade 12 in 2023 [IC]. In 2023/24, 15.3% of deliveries were to women aged 10 to 19 [IC].
- **Work residents already do.** At the 2025 IDP meeting, residents asked for a record of the work they do themselves, such as fire breaks, pothole repairs and baboon monitoring [IC].
- **How the valley is seen.** Official plans describe Genadendal mainly as a debt problem, and its economy as "dominated by 'development' projects" [IC]. See [[challenges#5. How the valley is seen|How the valley is seen]].
- **Damaged land.** Both the Baviaans River on [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] and the Gobos River on [[villages/erf-595-greyton|Erf 595]] are deeply eroded [IC], and the gullies around Genadendal and Greyton, as well as the stretch of the Riviersonderend in the Valley, are in Category A (see [[concepts/the-palmiet-principle|The Palmiet Principle]]).

Money alone is unlikely to change these things. [[organisations/the-shift|The Shift]] made this point to the Forum on 7 October 2026 and again during a site visit on 29 October 2026: capital speeds things up, and where people are not ready, it can also speed up dependency and poor stewardship [MS]. Its slides close with "Formation precedes flourishing" [MS].

## Vorming: our own version

We are forming our own version of Bildung, and we call it Vorming. It is based on care for our valley and on ecological restoration at a large scale. Rowson suggests that Bildung may need a new name for our times [MS], and a word from the valley's own language lets residents take the idea up as theirs. The Nordic tradition grew in folk high schools. In the valley it would grow on the riverbanks and the mountain slopes, in the work of returning the valley's land and water to health.

### Formed by the land

Restoration work asks for patience and cooperation over many years. Planting palmiet, clearing a slope or building a leaky dam forms the people who do it, as well as the land. The [[concepts/the-palmiet-principle|Palmiet Principle]] is the first practice built on this idea. It rewards each act of care with recognition, and lets the river show what changed.

### Circles of responsibility that follow the water

Andersen and Björkman describe responsibility widening from family outward to the globe [MS]. In the valley, that circle can follow the water: from a household and its garden, to a stretch of river, to the Gobos or the Baviaans, to the Riviersonderend, and on to the dams that supply Cape Town. A child who cares for one stretch of river learns where its water goes and who depends on it.

### Formation before funding

[[organisations/the-shift|The Shift]]'s Formation First Economy model asks three questions: 
- "Who are we becoming?", 
- "What are we ready to carry?" and 
- "What can we be trusted to steward?" [MS]. 

Vorming would ask them of every large project before the money arrives. The funding section of [[concepts/the-palmiet-principle|The Palmiet Principle]] sets out why large nature funds look for this readiness.

### Mentorship built in

[[organisations/meritus-international|Meritus International]] includes mentorship in every project it runs, with a mentee paid for two years while working beside a mentor before taking on a management role [MS]. The valley could do the same in restoration work: experienced clearing teams, farmers and nursery workers paired with young people, so that skill and responsibility pass on together.

### Fellowship across the valley

Hanzi Freinacht's *Nordic Ideology* (2019) proposes a "Gemeinschaft Politics", which "aims to improve the quality of human relationships across all aspects of society", and an "Existential Politics", which "aims to support all people on their life's journey and spur inner growth" [MS]. Shared work on a shared river is one of the few things that brings people from every settlement in the valley to the same place. A stretch of the Gobos or the Baviaans belongs to no single village.

### A principle, not a course

Vorming is a design principle for every initiative in the valley [MS]. Nobody needs to be taught the word. The children in the [[cultural/education/project/index|Class 6 Project]] are not taught it; they work with their hands on the river and on a map of the valley [MS]. Each initiative on this register could ask one question of itself: does it help the people who take part grow in responsibility for the valley?

## How it would show

Vorming happens inside people and cannot be scored. The valley would not grade or rank anyone. The signs would be outward and shared: rivers moving from Category A to Category B, the river log filling with acts of care, more people at the yearly river walk, and young people taking on work their mentors once did. The concept is explained further in [[concepts/the-palmiet-principle|The Palmiet Principle]].

## Sharing what the valley learns

We intend to offer what we learn through Vorming to the world, through the [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Valley of Grace Learning Campus]]. Many places face the same damaged land, and the same money that leaves faster than it arrives. A valley that can show how its people formed themselves by restoring their own land would have something of value to teach.

The campus could share it in several ways:

- **Through its Bioregional Learning Centre.** The river log, the seasonal photographs and the scorecards of each stretch would build a long record of a valley moving from Category A to Category B. The centre would keep that record and open it to researchers.
- **Through short residential courses.** The Nordic folk high schools ran courses of three to six months for small groups, with farming lessons and conversation at their heart [MS]. The campus could host people from other places, at home and abroad, for courses in the same spirit, working on the valley's rivers and slopes beside residents.
- **Through partner schools.** The campus page proposes exchanges with schools abroad. Visiting learners would join the restoration work, and valley learners would take what they know to other places.
- **In writing.** What the valley learns would be written up and published openly. Lessons that apply anywhere would go to the [BioConomy wiki](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/bildung), and the valley's own story would stay on this register.

Fees from visitors and courses would help pay for the campus, and so for bursaries for the valley's own young people. Teaching others would also form the people who teach: residents who guide visitors on a river they restored take on the next circle of responsibility.

## Who would benefit

- **Young people**, who would grow up doing real work on their own valley, beside adults they trust.
- **Adults across the settlements**, who would have shared work that brings them together.
- **The land and its rivers.**
- **Funders and partners**, who would find people ready to steward what they fund.

## Who would pay

Nothing has been costed. As a design principle, the idea costs little in itself. The practices built on it, such as [[concepts/the-palmiet-principle|The Palmiet Principle]], carry their own costs, and that page sets out where money could come from.

## Who would decide

Each person forms themselves; no one can do it for them. The Forum would decide whether to adopt Vorming as a design principle for the initiatives it brings together. Schools, congregations, organisations and partners such as The Shift and Meritus International would keep their own programmes, and decide for themselves whether and how to take part.

## How it fits the Forum's goal

The idea serves the [[priorities/education-skills|Education and Skills]] priority, because it treats the valley's land as a place of lifelong learning. It serves the [[priorities/conservation-environment|Conservation and Environment]] priority, because restoration is its main practice. It fits [[long-term-goal|Our Long-Term Goal]] for 2038, and it gives a reason for the order in which things are done: people first, then the money.

## What is still open

- **Other languages.** How Vorming is explained in isiXhosa and English, so that every resident can take it up.
- **The tradition's past.** How Vorming takes up Bildung while leaving behind its old ties to nationalism, which Rowson warns about [MS].
- **The Shift and Meritus International.** Whether they see their work in this way, and whether they would take part. Neither has been asked directly, although the similarities came through strongly during a site visit in October 2026.
- **The Moravian Church and the schools.** Whether they see the valley's history of education as this page describes it, and how they would want to be involved.
- **Measuring without scoring people.** Which outward signs the Forum would track, and how often.
- **The settlements.** How each settlement would shape its own part of the work, and how residents of every village would be invited in.

1. The Shift and Meritus International are asked for their views on this page.
2. The Moravian Church and the valley's schools are asked how the history is told here.

5. Vorming is built into the design of the [[concepts/valley-of-grace-learning-campus|Valley of Grace Learning Campus]], which would carry what the valley learns to others.
