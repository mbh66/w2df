## The goal

> Hold the land of Farm 39 in a form that no one can sell or mortgage, so that the argument about who owns it can give way to agreement on who cares for it.

## Why the valley needs it

The Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act of 1998 (TRANCRAA) provides for the Remainder of Farm 39, mostly commonage and farmland, to pass from the national Minister to an entity [IC]. The Act names the municipality and a Communal Property Association (CPA), or another body the Minister approves [IC]. The Minister may only transfer the land once satisfied that the receiving body's rules balance the tenure and use rights of residents, members, and present and future users [IC].

The process for Farm 39 began in 2002 [IC]. Its 18-month transitional period ended in January 2011 without a transfer, so the land stayed with the Minister [IC]. The record on [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39: The Land Transfer Process]] shows the parties divided on several points that bear on this idea:

- **Municipality or community entity.** The municipality recommended itself as the entity in 2010 and 2016 [IC]. The Transformasie Komitee is setting up a CPA to receive the land [MS].
- **Land sales during the process.** The 2008 court order placed "an immediate moratorium on the sale of Trust Land" [IC]. The Ward 2 Forum's 2017 analysis says over 200 residential properties on Farm 39 had been sold and transferred [MS].
- **Whether new owners will look after the land.** In October 2024, 82% of South Africa's 1,742 registered CPAs were not complying with the law that governs them [IC].

The Ward 2 Development Forum agreed on 16 September 2026 that other platforms already deal with the TRANCRAA process, and that it will avoid duplicating them [MS]. This page is therefore offered to the Komitee as an idea for its consideration. It does not propose that the Forum take part in the process itself.

## The trust deed

Die Bevryde Grond Trust ("the Freed Land Trust") was first set up on 14 September 1995 as the Leerkrans Trust, which owns a farm called Hoekiesdam [MS]. Its founder and trustees adopted a new deed in October 2012 "to create a vehicle to conserve and safeguard access to land for public benefit", and renamed the trust [MS]. The copy held by the Forum bears the stamp of the Master of the Western Cape High Court, dated 15 November 2012, and the stamp of [[organisations/cullinan-and-associates|Cullinan & Associates Inc]] [MS]. Cormac Cullinan, a director of that firm, helped draw up the deed [MS].

The deed's main provisions are these:

- **Custodian in perpetuity.** The trust's vision is "to be the custodian in perpetuity of land that is in its care, for the benefit of those who are able and willing to accept the responsibility of working and caring for the land" [MS].
- **Land made available to those who care for it.** The trust's primary object is to make its land available "to those who are willing and able to protect and care for it in perpetuity" [MS]. Anyone given the right to occupy or use the land must conserve its ecosystems, and anyone who farms it must use agro-ecological practices [MS]. The trust must prevent its land being degraded by urban and other development [MS].
- **No sale and no mortgage.** The trustees have no power to transfer ownership of the trust's land, or to mortgage, pledge or encumber it in any way [MS]. The one exception is a transfer to another owner, which needs all the trustees to agree that the land will go on being protected and used in line with the trust's vision [MS].
- **Clauses that cannot be changed.** The clauses setting out the vision, the objects and the ban on sale "shall not be capable of amendment" [MS]. Other clauses can be amended by 75% of the trustees [MS].
- **No private gain.** No trustee may acquire any right in the trust's assets, and no assets may be paid to a trustee except as reasonable pay for services or repayment of costs [MS]. If the trust ends, its assets may pass only to another public benefit organisation, a tax-exempt body with public benefit aims, or the state [MS].
- **Consensus first.** The board decides "in preference by consensus" [MS]. It goes to a vote only if all trustees present agree that a decision is imperative [MS].
- **Disputes go to mediation.** A dispute between trustees goes to a mediator within 15 days, and to binding arbitration if mediation fails within a further 15 days, through the Arbitration Foundation of Southern Africa [MS].
- **Nominations from outside.** Before appointing a trustee, the trustees must ask named organisations to nominate candidates, though they need not appoint those nominated [MS]. There must be between three and seven trustees [MS].

The deed's preamble says private land ownership "fosters a culture of trading land as if it is a mere commodity" and "distorts how people relate to land and natural communities" [MS]. [[long-term-goal|Our Long-Term Goal]] makes a related point: problems start "when money decides who gets land or water" [MS].

## How it could help with Farm 39

The deed was written for a single farm in private hands, and nothing in it was designed for TRANCRAA. It offers two things the valley could use.

**As a set of clauses.** Whichever entity receives the land, its founding rules could borrow the deed's strongest provisions: a ban on selling or mortgaging the land that no later vote can remove, conditions of care attached to every right of use, consensus decisions with mediation and arbitration when consensus fails, and assets that can pass only to another public body. A CPA's constitution, or the agreement under which a municipality holds land in trust, could carry clauses of this kind. This route leaves the choice of entity where it is.

**As the receiving entity.** TRANCRAA allows the Minister to approve a body other than a municipality or a CPA [IC]. A trust on these lines, with the residents of Farm 39 as its beneficiaries, could be put to the Minister as that body. It could also sit alongside a CPA, for example holding the commonage and farmland while the CPA holds other rights. Either form would put the land beyond sale by any party, including the entity itself.

Each route speaks to one of the points in dispute. A permanent ban on sale would answer the concern behind the 2008 moratorium. Conditions of care, built into every lease, would link a right to use the land with a duty to look after it.

## What the deed would need before it fits

The deed in its present form would not meet TRANCRAA's test, and could not be copied as it stands:

- **Who appoints the trustees.** The present trustees appoint new ones, after consulting named organisations [MS]. TRANCRAA requires rules that balance residents' tenure and use rights [IC]. A Farm 39 version would need residents to elect or confirm trustees, and a way to remove them.
- **Trustees' discretion.** The deed gives trustees "a complete and unfettered discretion" over the assets, and frees them from giving reasons for their decisions [MS]. Residents would need written rights the trustees could not override.
- **Public benefit or residents' benefit.** To keep its tax status, the trust must carry out its activities "for the benefit of the general public" [MS]. Farm 39 is held in trust for its residents [IC]. A lawyer would need to say whether one trust can do both, or whether the valley would need a different form.
- **Existing rights.** Users of the garden plots and holders of farm leases would each need a settled place in the new arrangement, as would the owners of properties already transferred off the farm.

## Who would benefit

The residents of Farm 39, as the TRANCRAA process defines them, and the people who farm or graze its land. The land itself would be protected against sale and development.

## Who would pay

Nothing has been costed. A legal opinion would cost money, as would drafting a deed or constitution and consulting residents as TRANCRAA requires. No one has offered to pay.

## Who would decide

The Minister decides which entity receives the land [IC]. The municipality, or failing it a committee elected by residents, recommends the entity [IC]. The Transformasie Komitee speaks for residents in the process [IC]. The Forum has no role in that decision [MS].

## How it fits the Forum's goal

The idea touches the [[priorities/conservation-environment|Conservation and Environment]] priority, through conditions of care on all land use, and the [[priorities/agriculture-food-sovereignty|Agriculture and Food Sovereignty]] priority, through secure access to land for those who farm it. It also bears on the Civic sphere's value of equality in [[long-term-goal|Our Long-Term Goal]], because land that cannot be bought cannot pass to whoever has the most money.

## What is still open

- **The Komitee's view.** Whether the Transformasie Komitee wants to consider this idea at all, given its work towards a CPA. It has not been asked.
- **The Forum's decision of 16 September 2026.** Whether tabling this page at the Forum fits that decision, or whether it should go only to the Komitee.
- **The trust's consent.** Whether the trustees of Die Bevryde Grond Trust agree to their deed being described here. They have not been asked.
- **Cormac Cullinan's view.** Whether he or his firm would advise on a version for Farm 39. Neither has been asked.
- **The law.** Whether a trust can be approved as a receiving entity under TRANCRAA, and whether a trust for residents can also be a public benefit organisation.
- **The municipality's and the Department's views.** Neither has been asked.
- **How the trust deed has worked.** How Die Bevryde Grond Trust has run since 2012, and whether it registered as a public benefit organisation as it intended [MS].
