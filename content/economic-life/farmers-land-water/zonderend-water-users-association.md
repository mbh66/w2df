The Zonderend Water Users Association (ZWUA) brings together the people who use water from the Riviersonderend [MS]. Its aim is to manage the river's water responsibly, so that all water users have fresh water for generations to come [MS]. It works with water users and other stakeholders to restore and conserve the river, and to create jobs for local people while doing so [MS].

The Riviersonderend flows into Theewaterskloof Dam, the largest dam in the Western Cape [TBV]. Its banks were once lined with palmiet wetlands, which hold water, slow floods, and stop erosion. Invasive trees such as pines, Port Jackson, blue gums, and black wattle have crowded out the palmiet in many places [MS].

WWF South Africa started a project to clear invasive plants along the river in 2013, with funding from government and private partners. At the end of 2017 it handed the project to ZWUA, which named it Restoring the Riviersonderend [MS].

## Offers
- **Clearing along the river.** Removing invasive trees from the riverbanks. ZWUA clears with Wild Restoration in the Greyton Sandpit, and works with the Greyton Conservation Society [IC].
- **Restoration.** Replanting cleared areas with indigenous plants, and rescuing indigenous plants from building sites along the river to replant them later [MS]
- **The Genadendal nursery.** An indigenous plant nursery in Genadendal that grows palmiet and other local species for restoration. It is open to the public, but ZWUA asks visitors to get in touch first [MS].
- **Jobs.** A team of 14 workers, employed through the Expanded Public Works Programme (EPWP), runs the nursery [MS]
