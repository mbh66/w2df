The DevForum agreed five development priorities at its first meeting on 16 September 2026 [MS]. The [[challenges|Challenges]] page records why progress in Ward 2 has been hard for a long time, from land title on [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] to the finances of [[organisations/theewaterskloof-municipality|Theewaterskloof Municipality]]. This page takes the next step. For each priority, it will ask what has held the valley back, who decides whether anything changes, and what would need to happen first. It uses a method called political economy analysis, which looks behind a problem to find out who wants what, and why.

## The question

The five priorities lead, and the analysis is built around them.

For each of the DevForum's priorities:

- [[priorities/tourism-development|Tourism Development]]
- [[priorities/education-skills|Education and Skills]]
- [[priorities/agriculture-food-sovereignty|Agriculture and Food Sovereignty]]
- [[priorities/conservation-environment|Conservation and Environment]]
- [[priorities/business-procurement|Business Development and Local Procurement]]

the run will ask:

1. What has held this priority back in Ward 2, and for how long?
2. Who decides whether anything about it changes, in the formal rules and in practice?
3. What does each party stand to gain or lose if it does change?
4. Which parts can move now, and which wait on something else, such as land title or municipal services?

Then, across all five:

5. Where do the priorities depend on each other?
6. What order of steps would allow all five to change, and what would show that they are changing?

## How it will be done

Political economy analysis is a practice development agencies use to understand why plans stall. It looks at four things: 
1. The slow background forces in a place, 
2. How deals get made or blocked, 
3. Who is involved, and 
4. What drives each party. 

The method is explained on the BioConomy wiki at [Political Economy Analysis](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/frameworks/political-economy-analysis), drawn from a short guide by Alan Whaites.

Two other frameworks go with it:

- The [Sustainable Livelihoods Framework](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/frameworks/sustainable-livelihoods-framework) describes what households and villages have to work with: skills, relationships, land and water, infrastructure, and money. It starts from what the valley holds, which is the stance behind [[concepts/the-yearly-valley-survey|The Yearly Valley Survey]].
- The [Impact Measurement Guidelines](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/frameworks/impact-measurement-guidelines) describe how a program would show that it's working.

The [[challenges|Challenges]] page arranged its 2026 evidence for the [Metabolic Study Template](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/templates/metabolic-study-template). That template's carrying budget sets the limits of land, water, and people's well-being that any plan has to stay within, and this run uses it for the background forces.

The work has four parts:

1. **Reading what is already on this site.** The history pages, the rules, the challenges record, the concepts, the case studies, and the DevForum's own record.
2. **Conversations.** About an hour each, in Afrikaans or English, with people across the valley, starting with those least often asked. Each person decides whether they are named.
3. **Checking back.** What the conversations seem to show is played back to some of the people who spoke, before anything is written down as a finding.
4. **Review.** The draft goes to every party it names, to the Komitee in writing, and then to a DevForum meeting.

Every party will be described only through what it has said or done, as on the Challenges page. Where Greyton and Genadendal interests meet, each side's statements will be given in its own words.
