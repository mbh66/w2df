## Your question

Who looked after the valley's water over the centuries, and how did they decide how much could be used?

You are the class expert on the valley's history of water. A steward is someone who looks after something that belongs to others as well. Your hands-first job is to collect one living story and tie a timeline in string.

## Facts to meet

Choose three of these for your Field Book. Tie a thread to each one.

- Khoekhoe people's claims to land rested on their use of springs and rivers. [IC]
- Georg Schmidt settled at Baviaanskloof on 23 April 1738. [IC] The mission there was renamed Genadendal in 1806. [IC]
- In 1854 plots in Greyton were sold with a right to use water from the leiwater, the furrows that carry water from the Gobos River. [IC] The furrows have carried water since 1854. [MS] Read more: [Greyton's Leiwater](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/history/leiwater).
- In 1858 the mission land at Genadendal was granted to the Moravian Church in trust for the people who lived there. [MS]
- In 1899 the Moravian Church bought a mountain farm called Langgezocht, about 1,881 hectares, to protect Genadendal's water sources. [MS]

**Words to meet:** steward (someone who looks after something that belongs to others as well), water right (the legal permission to use a share of water), furrow (a small channel that carries water from a river to land), in trust (held by one group for the benefit of another).

## Take care

Greyton and Genadendal have different histories with land and water. Tell each side only through what its own records and people say. Do not take sides. Do not write what you think either community wants.

If you find anything about who owns the land, or who has the right to it, do not say it aloud in class yet. Tell Teacher Cheryl first. The Transformasie Komitee has to agree with it before anyone uses it.

## Think of Europe

Towns often grew where two rivers met, or where a pass crossed a mountain. Where did people settle in our valley, and why there? Which kloofs were doorways through the mountains? Bring your answer from main lesson.

## Week 1

You have about two hours on your own.

**Do**

1. **Ask someone older (30 minutes).** Ask a parent, grandparent or neighbour: "Where did the valley's water come from when you were my age? Who decided how much you could use?" Write down what they say in their own words. Do not ask who owns land.
2. **Tie a string timeline (25 minutes).** Take a string about three metres long. Mark one end "before 1652" and the other end "today". Tie knots for 1738, 1854, 1858 and 1899. Hang or draw one small thing beside each knot that stands for it.

**Tell**

3. **Tell their story (25 minutes).** Stand up and tell your older person's story aloud as if you were them. Do this twice. Notice what you remembered and what you had to look up.

**Think**

4. **Your Field Book page (20 minutes).** Draw a scene from the story you were told. Write the speaker's own words beside it.
5. **Three facts (10 minutes).** Choose three facts from the list and tie a thread to each: blue, yellow or red. Their own words go with a yellow thread.
6. **Think of Europe (10 minutes).** Write two sentences in answer to the question above.
