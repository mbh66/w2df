## Your question

Who has held the valley's water over the centuries, and how did each one decide?

You are the class expert on the valley's history of water. "Holding" water means looking after it, using it or having the right to say who gets it.

## What you need to know

People have lived beside our rivers and springs for a very long time. Each group that came held the water in its own way. In class you learned the four forms of the TIME model: Tribes (T), Institutions (I), Markets (M) and Emergent (E). Each holder below fits one form best, and sometimes more than one. Your job is to decide which, and to give a reason.

### Four holders

1. **The Khoekhoe.** Khoekhoe people's claims to land rested on their use of springs and rivers. You will stamp this **Checked twice**.
2. **Greyton's water furrows.** In 1854 plots in Greyton were sold with a right to use water from the leiwater. The leiwater is a system of furrows that carry water from the Gobos River. You will stamp the sale and the water right **Checked twice**. You will stamp the claim that the furrows have carried water since 1854 **Their own word**.
3. **The Moravian Church and Farm 39.** The Moravian Church holds land that was granted to it in trust in 1858. You will stamp this **Their own word**. Genadendal, Bereaville, Voorstekraal and Boschmanskloof stand on this land.
4. **Langgezocht.** In 1899 the Moravian Church bought a mountain farm called Langgezocht, about 1,881 hectares, to protect Genadendal's water sources. You will stamp this **Their own word**.

### Take care

Greyton and Genadendal have different histories with land and water. Tell each side only through what its own records and people say. Do not take sides. Do not write what you think either community wants.

If you find anything about who owns the land, or who has the right to it, do not say it aloud in class yet. Tell Michael first. The Transformasie Komitee has to see it before the children use it.

### Words to know

- **Holder:** a person or group that looks after or controls something.
- **Water right:** the legal permission to use a share of water.
- **Furrow:** a small channel that carries water from a river to land.
- **In trust:** held by one group for the benefit of another.

## The three stamps

- **Checked twice:** two separate sources agree, or an official record says so.
- **Their own word:** it comes from the person or group the fact is about.
- **Not yet checked:** it seems likely, but nobody has confirmed it.
