## Your question

What does the land do with rain, and how could it hold more?

You are the class expert on soil, trees and streams. Your hands-first job is to find out how soil holds water.

## Facts to meet

Choose three of these for your Field Book. Tie a thread to each one.

- Healthy soil soaks up rain like a sponge and lets it out slowly into springs and streams. Damaged soil sheds the rain in a few hours. [MS] ([Water Retention Landscapes](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/water-retention-landscapes))
- Pines and wattles came from other countries. They drink more water than the native plants they push out. [MS]
- On one Cape mountain, streams ran 55% lower 23 years after pines moved in. [MS]
- Clearing invasive trees puts water back into streams. [IC]
- Nobody has measured how much extra water clearing would add in our valley. [TBV]

**Words to meet:** catchment (land where rain drains into one river), invasive (a plant from somewhere else that spreads and crowds out the plants that belong), native (a plant that belongs where it grows), runoff (rain that flows over the ground and does not soak in).

## Think of Europe

A mountain is a wall for travellers and a sponge for water. Where do Europe's great rivers begin? What do the mountains do with rain and snow before they send it down? Bring your answer from main lesson.

## Week 1

You have about two hours on your own.

**Do**

1. **The pour test (30 minutes).** Ask a grown-up to help you.
   - Find two spots outside. One has hard bare soil. The other has grass or fallen leaves.
   - Pour one cup of water slowly onto each spot from the same height.
   - Watch. Does the water sink in or run away? How long until it is gone?
   - Do the test twice. Write down the times.
2. **Paint the sponge (40 minutes).** Wet your paper first. Paint a mountain with rain falling on it. Let the blue run down and show where the rain sinks in and where it runs off. Use coloured pencils if you have no paints.

**Tell**

3. **Tell it back (15 minutes).** Tell a grown-up what you saw, in your own words, before you look at any facts. Ask them one question about it.

**Think**

4. **Your Field Book page (25 minutes).** Stick in your best drawing or painting. Write your pour-test times beside it. Choose three facts from the list and tie a thread to each: blue, yellow or red.
5. **Think of Europe (10 minutes).** Write two sentences in answer to the question above.

## Bring to Week 2
