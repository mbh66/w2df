title: "Water for the Valley: A Guide for Parents"
---


For parents and guardians of the Silverwood Primary learners taking part. You do not need to do anything. This page explains what your child is working on, so you know what to expect.

## What the project is

For six weeks, your child and seven classmates will study a real question: how could our valley be paid for the water its land holds?

A large bond (a kind of loan that investors buy) called the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond pays for clearing invasive trees from the mountains around Cape Town. The trees drink a great deal of water, and clearing them puts water back into the streams. Nobody yet knows whether work in our valley could be paid for in the same way. The children's task is to look for a route and to say honestly what would have to happen first.

The project builds on what they have learned over the past twelve weeks: the TIME model (four ways people organise themselves: Tribes, Institutions, Markets and Emergent) and Commitment Pooling (people and organisations making promises to each other, and holding those promises together).

## What your child will do

- **Choose a theme** from six: how land holds water, who decides where the water goes, how the bond works, who has looked after the valley's water over time, who does the restoration work, and how many parties can promise each other what none could do alone.
- **Map the people and organisations involved** on the TIME model, and say why each one sits where it does.
- **Work out what each party could offer and what it would need** before a plan could go ahead.
- **Choose one organisation** from the Ward 2 Development Forum's public Register of valley initiatives as the best partner, and explain the choice.
- **Tell a story together** to a panel the children choose and invite. Every child takes part.

## How the time is spent

Four hours a week for six weeks: two hours on their own and two with the guide, Michael Haupt. The sessions use story, movement, drawing and making as well as reading and discussion. The children build a wall chart of the TIME map and a Pool Board that shows every promise and every need.

## Who the children will meet

The children may write to, interview or visit local organisations from the Register, such as conservation groups, a water users' association and the Transformasie Komitee. They may also invite adults to sit on the panel.

**Safeguards.**
- The children write the letters. An adult sends every letter and reads every reply first.
- An adult is present at every interview and visit.
- No child's phone number or home address goes into any letter.
- Each organisation sees what the children write about it before the presentation and can ask for corrections.
- Silverwood's own child-protection rules apply wherever they say more.

## The panel

The panel is a practice run. Adults will play the parts of people connected to the bond, such as an issuer, an investor, a checker and a representative of the valley. They are not speaking for the real organisations, and the children will say so at the start. The children decide who to ask and write the invitations themselves.

## How the children learn to be careful with facts

Every fact the children use carries one of three stamps: checked twice, the organisation's own word, or not yet checked. The final story ends by saying what nobody knows yet. The children will probably come home with questions about how we know what we know.

## A request

The idea the children are studying is still being worked out. The Ward 2 Development Forum has not yet discussed it and some of the organisations involved have not yet been asked. Please treat what your child tells you as a school exercise and keep it within the family until the children have presented.

## What you can do

Nothing is required. If you would like to help:

- Ask your child to explain their theme and their favourite fact, and what stamp it has.
- Ask what the class still does not know.
- If you work in a field the children are studying (water, farming, banking, conservation, the law, teaching) and would be willing to be interviewed or to sit on the panel, tell Michael or your child's teacher.
- Come to the presentation, if the school invites families.
