For parents and guardians of the Silverwood Primary School students doing the Class 6, Term 4 project. You don't need to do anything. This page explains what your child is working on, so you know what to expect.

## What the project is

For six weeks, from 12 October to 20 November 2026, your child and their classmates will study a real question: how could our valley be paid for the water its mountains collect before it flows into the Riviersonderend River? This is a real-world use of what they learned in Term 3's Emergent Economics lessons.

A large bond (a kind of loan that investors buy) called the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond pays for clearing invasive trees from the mountains around Cape Town's dams. The trees drink a great deal of water. Clearing them puts water back into the streams. Our valley sits below the Theewaterskloof Dam, so nobody yet knows whether work done here could count toward that bond. The children's task is to look for their own route in and to say honestly what stands in the way.

The project builds on what they already know:
- [The TIME model](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/frameworks/time-framework) (four ways people organise themselves: Tribes, Institutions, Markets and Emergent), and
- [Commitment Pooling](https://wiki.bioconomy.earth/concepts/commitment-pooling) (people and organisations making promises to each other and holding those promises together).

## How the children work: Do, Tell, Think

We heard from parents that the first plan asked for too much reading and too many abstract ideas. The project now follows the order we use in main lesson.

1. **Do.** Every week begins with something the children make or try with their hands. They walk to the river, pour water on soil, build a valley in sand, measure a stream, weed a garden bed, and play games with wool and dried beans.
2. **Tell.** Michael tells a story. Each child then tells what they did back to a grown-up at home, in their own words, and tells it again after a night's sleep.
3. **Think.** Only after that do the children sort what they found, decide how sure they are of it, and make up their minds.

Each child keeps a **Field Book**, in the style of their main lesson books. Every week they add one large drawing or painting and three facts, each tied with a coloured thread: blue (checked twice), yellow (the organisation's own word) or red (not yet checked).

## Kinship Fridays

The guided session with Josh takes place on Fridays, when the children are outdoors for the whole of Kinship. The river, the field and the trees are part of the classroom. Most of the games are played outside: a floor map laid out on the grass, a web of wool passed between children under a tree, and a bean game beside a stream. Please send your child dressed for the weather, as they would for any Kinship day.

## Rivers and mountains

In their Europe lessons the class is meeting rivers as routes for trade and mountains as barriers. We weave both into the project. Each child has one question about Europe on their page. Rivers carry goods, news and money. Mountains hold travellers back, and they also catch rain and send it down. A kloof, a gap in the mountains, is a doorway. Genadendal began in one.

## What your child will do

Each child has chosen one theme from six:
- **The Sponge** (Mikiya): how land holds water.
- **The Dam and the Law** (Annabella): who decides where the water goes.
- **The Bond and the Money** (Evan follows the money, Ayden follows the checking): how the bond works and how anyone proves the water was delivered.
- **Water Stewards Through History** (Zara): who looked after the valley's water over the centuries.
- **Hands for the Work** (Gracey): who would do the restoration work.
- **The Promise Pool** (Sam): how many parties can promise each other what none could do alone. Sam is the Weaver who helps the whole class build the Pool Board.

Together the class will make:
- **A TIME map on the floor**, with organisations carried to the four corners marked T, I, M and E.
- **A Pool Board woven in wool**, showing what each organisation can promise and what it needs before a plan can go ahead.
- **A partner**, chosen from the Ward 2 Development Forum's public [[register|Register]] of valley initiatives, with reasons.
- **A story** told, acted, sung and shown to a panel the children choose and invite. Every child takes part.

## Who the children will meet

The children may write to, interview or visit local organisations from the Register, such as conservation groups, a water users' association and the Transformasie Komitee. They also invite adults to sit on the panel which will hear their final presentation.

**Safeguards.**
- The children write the letters, but an adult sends every letter and reads every reply first.
- An adult is present at every interview and visit.
- No child's phone number or home address goes into any letter.
- Each organisation sees what the children write about it before the presentation and can ask for corrections.
- Silverwood's own child-protection and outdoor rules apply wherever they say more.

## The panel

The panel is a practice run. Adults play the parts of people connected to the bond, such as an issuer, an investor, a checker and a representative of the valley. They don't speak for the real organisations, and the children say so at the start. The children decide who to ask and write the invitations themselves. If everyone is comfortable at the end, the class could ask its chosen partner organisation whether it wishes to take the idea further.

## How the children learn to be careful with facts

Every fact the children use carries a thread:
- blue: checked twice [IC],
- yellow: the organisation's own word [MS], or
- red: not yet checked [TBV].

The story ends by naming what the children still don't know. They will probably come home with questions about how we know what we know.

## A request

The idea the children are studying is still being worked out in real life. The Ward 2 Development Forum has prepared a Concept Note but has not yet discussed it, and some of the organisations involved have not yet been asked or briefed. Please treat what your child tells you as a school exercise and keep it within the family until the children have presented their findings.

This is a chance for your child to play a real role in the future of our valley. We ask for a little caution because of the tensions and politics in the valley.

## What you can do

Nothing is required. If you would like to help:

- Ask your child to show you their Field Book, to explain their favourite fact, and to say which thread it carries.
- Ask what the class still needs to find out.
- Listen when your child tells you their week's task back to you. The telling is part of the learning.
- If you work in a field the children are studying (water, farming, banking, conservation, the law or teaching) and would be willing to be interviewed or to sit on the panel, tell Michael or Cheryl.
- If you're comfortable using AI, point your favourite agent to a machine-readable version of the project: https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/cultural/education/project/llms.txt. The children may also use AI to check a fact or explain a hard word, and they have been shown how. The painting, building and telling are theirs. You can also use the search bar in the top left-hand corner of every page.
- Come to the presentation once the school confirms the date.
