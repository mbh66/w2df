---
title: "Water for the Valley: Your Term 4 Project"
---


Your project for the next six weeks.

## Your question

How could our valley be paid for the water its land holds? And what must each person or group promise before a plan can go ahead?

A big bond called the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond pays for clearing invasive trees so that more water reaches Cape Town. Nobody knows yet whether the valley's work could count. Your job is to find a way in and to say honestly what stands in the way.

## What you will make

Together you will make four things:

1. **A TIME map.** Every group and organisation in your story gets a place on it: T, I, M or E. You must say why in one sentence.
2. **A Pool Board.** For every party you write what it can promise and what it needs before a plan can go ahead. You will use what you learned about Commitment Pooling.
3. **A partner.** You choose one organisation from the Ward 2 Development Forum's Register to work with, and you explain your choice.
4. **A story.** All of you tell it to a panel. You choose who sits on the panel and you write their invitations.

## Your theme

Each of you picks one theme. All six must be taken before any is taken twice.

1. **The Sponge.** What does the land do with rain, and how could it hold more?
2. **The Dam and the Law.** Who decides where the valley's water goes?
3. **The Bond and the Money.** How does a bond turn clean water into something people will pay for?
4. **Who Has Held the Water.** Who looked after the valley's water over the centuries, and how did they decide?
5. **Hands for the Work.** Who would do the clearing and the restoring, and what would they need to learn?
6. **The Promise Pool.** How could many parties promise each other what none could do alone?

Whatever your theme, you also place your parties on the TIME map, fill in their part of the Pool Board, and think about which organisation would make the best partner.

## Your six weeks

Each week has two hours with Michael and two hours on your own.

| Week | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | Walk to the water. Learn the story. Choose your theme. Write ten fact cards and five questions. |
| 2 | Find your parties. Put them on the TIME map. Read their Register pages. Decide who to write to. |
| 3 | Send your letters and panel invitations. Interview people. Fix the Pool Board with what they tell you. |
| 4 | Finish the Pool Board. Match every need with a promise. Choose three possible partners and score them. |
| 5 | Decide on your partner together. Send each organisation what you wrote about it. Build the story. |
| 6 | Rehearse. Present to the panel. Look back on what you found. |

## Four rules

**1. Every fact gets a stamp.** Before a fact goes into your story, give it one of these:

- **Checked twice.** Two separate sources agree, or an official record says so. [IC]
- **Their own word.** It comes from the person or organisation the fact is about. [MS]
- **Not yet checked.** It seems likely, and nobody has confirmed it. [TBV]

Ask yourself: what would move this fact up one level?

**2. You can only promise what you control.** Nobody promises for someone else. A need is a request. It turns into a promise when somebody says yes.

**3. Map what groups do.** Put organisations on the TIME map and write how each one gets its say. Leave people and whole communities off it.

**4. An adult is always there.** You write every letter. An adult sends it and reads the replies first. An adult comes with you to every interview or visit. Never put your phone number or home address in a letter.

## Before you write about an organisation

Everything you write about an organisation goes back to it before you present. Ask: is this right? What would you change?

## The panel

Real adults will play people connected to the bond. It is a practice run. Say so at the start of your presentation. Choose the roles you want in the room first, then choose who could play each one. Send your invitations by the end of Week 3.

## When your project is finished

- Every party on the TIME map has a reason for its place.
- The Pool Board shows a promise and a need for every party, each with a stamp.
- The needs nobody has met are listed. These are what must happen before a plan can go ahead.
- You have chosen one partner and can say why.
- Every organisation has seen what you wrote about it.
- Every one of you speaks, sings, acts or shows your work at some point in the story.
- The story ends by naming what nobody knows yet.
