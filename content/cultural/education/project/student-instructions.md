title: "Water for the Valley: Your Term 4 Project"
---


Your Term 4 project for the next six weeks.

## The question you'll be answering

How could our valley be paid for the water its land holds? And what must each person or group promise before a plan can go ahead?

A big bond called the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond pays for clearing invasive trees so that more water reaches Cape Town from Theewaterskloof Dam. We sit below the Dam, which means none of the water in our river ever reaches Cape Town. Nobody knows yet whether work we do in our valley could count towards that water bond. 

The project is to figure out a way to tap into that bond, and if it cannot be done, to  say so honestly and explain what the blockages are.

## What you will make

Together you will make four things:

1. **A TIME map.** Every group and organisation in your project gets place on that map: T, I, M or E. You must say why in one sentence for each group.
2. **A Pool Board.** For every group, write what it can *promise* and what it *needs* before your project can go ahead. You will use what you learned in term 3 about Commitment Pooling.
3. **A Partner.** You choose one organisation from the Ward 2 Development Forum's Register to work with, and you explain your choice.
4. **A Story.** All of you tell your story, together, to a panel. You will choose who sits on the panel and you will write invitations to them.
5. All of the answers you need can be found at https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/. Use the search bar in the top left hand corner.

## Your theme

Each of you picks one theme. All six themes must be taken before any is taken twice.

1. **The Sponge.** What does the land do with rain, and how could it hold more?
2. **The Dam and the Law.** Who decides where the valley's water goes? Who decides how much water can be taken from a river?
3. **The Bond and the Money.** How does a bond turn clean water into something people will pay for?
4. **Who Has Stewarded the Water.** Who looked after the valley's water over the centuries, and how did they decide how much could be used?
5. **Hands for the Work.** Who would do the clearing and the retaining, and what would they need to learn?
6. **The Promise Pool.** How could many parties promise each other what none could do alone?

Whatever your theme, you all place your parties on the TIME map (you build it together), fill in their part of the Pool Board, and think about which organisation would make the best partner.

## Your six weeks

Each week has two hours with Michael and two hours on your own (or in groups).

| Week | What happens                                                                                                  |

| 1    | Go to the rivers during Kinship. Learn the story. Choose your theme. Write ten fact cards and five questions. |

## Four rules

**1. Every fact gets a stamp.** Before a fact goes into your story, give it one of these:

- **Checked twice.** Two separate sources agree, or an official record says so. [IC] - Independently Corroborated, which is the most trusted information
- **Their own word.** It comes from the person or organisation the fact is about. [MS] - Mission Sourced, which should ideally be corroborated
- **Not yet checked.** It seems likely, but nobody has confirmed it. [TBV] - To Be Verified, which can be included and then asked during interviews.

Ask yourself: what would move each fact up one level?

**2. You can only promise what you control.** Nobody promises for someone else. A need is a request. It turns into a promise when somebody says yes, they can deliver on it.

**3. Map what groups in the valley do.** Put organisations on the TIME map and write how each one gets its say. Leave individual people and whole communities/towns off this map - only organisations.

**4. A teacher or parent is always available.** You will write every letter yourself, and a teacher will send it and read the replies first. A teacher or parent will come with you to every interview or visit. Never put your phone number or home address in a letter, but you can use the school's address.

## Before you present about an organisation

Everything you write about an organisation gets checked by them before you present anything. Ask: is this right? What would you change?

## The panel

Real adults from people from groups doing work in the valley will play the role of people connected to the bond. It's a practice run. Say so at the start of your presentation. Choose the roles you want in the panel first, then choose who could play each one. Send your invitations by the end of Week 3.

## When your project is finished

- Every party on the TIME map has a reason for its place.
- The Pool Board shows a promise and a need for every party, each with a stamp.
- The needs nobody has met are listed. These are what must happen before a plan can go ahead.
- You have chosen one partner organisation to work with and can say why.
- Every organisation has seen what you wrote about it.
- Every one of you speaks, sings, acts or shows your work at some point in the story.
- The story ends by naming what still needs to be answered or looked into.
