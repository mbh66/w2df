This is your own page for the Water for the Valley project. New homework is added below the old homework each week. Right now it is Week 1.

## Your question

What does the land do with rain, and how could it hold more?

You are the class expert on soil, trees and streams.

## What you need to know

Rain falls on the mountains above our valley. What happens next depends on the land.

Healthy land works like a sponge. Rain soaks into the soil and the soil holds it. The water then leaks out slowly into springs and streams. A stream can keep running for weeks after the rain has stopped.

Damaged land works like a hard kitchen counter. Rain runs off in a few hours and rushes down to the river. The rain is the same. The land is different.

Helping land hold more water is called making a Water Retention Landscape. The BioConomy wiki explains the idea.

### The thirsty trees

Some trees came from other countries and spread over our mountains. Pines and wattles are two of them. They are called invasive trees. They drink more water than the native plants that grew there before.

One study on the Cape mountains found that streams ran 55% lower, 23 years after pines moved in. You will stamp this fact **Their own word**, because you only know it from the BioConomy wiki's account of the study.

When people clear invasive trees, water comes back into the streams. You will stamp this fact **Checked twice**.

### What nobody knows yet

Nobody has measured how much extra water clearing would give our valley. You will stamp this **Not yet checked**. It is the biggest puzzle in your theme. How could anyone prove that more water is flowing?

### Words to know

- **Catchment:** the land where rain drains into one river.
- **Invasive:** a plant from somewhere else that spreads and crowds out the plants that belong.
- **Native:** a plant that belongs in the place where it grows.
- **Runoff:** rain that flows over the ground instead of soaking in.

## The three stamps

- **Checked twice:** two separate sources agree, or an official record says so.
- **Their own word:** it comes from the person or group the fact is about.
- **Not yet checked:** it seems likely, but nobody has confirmed it.

## Week 1 homework

This should take about two hours.

**1. Read this page twice (15 minutes).** Underline every word you do not know. Bring the list to Week 2.

**2. Do the pour test (35 minutes).** Ask a grown-up to help you.

- Find two spots outside. One has hard bare soil. The other has grass or fallen leaves.
- Pour one cup of water slowly onto each spot. Use the same cup and pour from the same height.
- Watch. Does the water sink in or run away? How long until it is gone?
- Do the test twice. Draw what you see and write down the times.

In Week 2 you will make a bigger version with trays.

**3. Write ten fact cards (40 minutes).** Use a card like this for each one:

- Write at least five facts from this page.
- Write at least two facts from your pour test. Stamp them **Not yet checked** until a second person has watched the test and agrees with you.
- Write at least two facts from the Register pages for [Wild Restoration](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration) and [Zonderend Water Users Association](https://df.valleyofgrace.co.za/economic/farmers-land-water/zonderend-water-users-association). Those pages are the groups' own words.

**4. Write five questions you want answered (15 minutes).** Good questions start with How, Why or Who.

**5. Tidy up (15 minutes).** Put your cards, questions and pour-test drawing in one folder.
