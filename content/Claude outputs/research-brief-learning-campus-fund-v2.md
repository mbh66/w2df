**Is the valley eligible for water-bond money?**
- The Western Cape Water Supply System's largest dam is Theewaterskloof, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp [IC].\[58\]\[59\] The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans join the Riviersonderend [IC].\[58\] *Inference:* its streams do not feed the supply system's dams.
- The Upper Riviersonderend, above the dam, is the second of the Water Fund's seven priority sub-catchments [MS].\[51\] That is upstream of the valley.
- The Boland strategic water source area covers about 608,000 to 619,000 hectares, and the Riviersonderend is one of the main rivers that flow from it [IC].\[55\]\[56\] Whether the mountains above Greyton and Genadendal fall inside its boundary was not confirmed [TBV]. The national boundary data would settle it.
- *Inference.* The first bond pays for clearing above the dam, so the valley cannot draw on it. A later bond in the series could reach the valley only if it pays for a strategic water source area that includes the valley's mountains, or for a measure other than supply to Cape Town, such as dry-season flow in the Breede. The Boland–Groot Winterhoek SWSA Collective, which names Theewaterskloof Municipality among the first it would approach, is the forum where that would be discussed [MS].\[57\]

## C. The three architectures compared

| Feature | (a) One pooled fund with windows | (b) Separate vehicles under one governing trust | (c) Blended: investor-financed boarding buildings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal vehicles | One trust or non-profit company, PBO with Section 18A; three internal windows | Ward governing trust (land, no debt); capital campaign NPC (PBO, 18A); endowment trust (PBO, 18A); landscape fund NPC (PBO; 18A for qualifying conservation work); building company NPC | As (b) plus an investor-facing vehicle that holds debt |
| Who holds the land | The pooled fund or the ward trust | Ward trust, or the receiving body for Farm 39 once the Minister decides | Ward trust; buildings sit with a vehicle that can carry debt |
| How the boarding school stays a tenant | Lease from the fund | Lease from the building company, which leases from the ward trust; at most one seat on the ward trust board [MS]\[110\] | As (b); rent services investor debt |
| How a funder sees what its money did | Window-level reports | Each vehicle reports on its own audited accounts | Investors see debt service; donors see the rest |
| Fit with Bevryde Grond Trust deed | Breaks if the pooled fund borrows against trust land | Fits: the land vehicle carries no debt | Fits only if no security over trust land (see below) |
| Main risk | Funders cannot ring-fence; a dispute in one window freezes all | More administration | Fee and occupancy risk; deed constraint |

**Reporting through the R100 Resource Journey.** Ranyaka's model measures "how much of each R100 committed reaches local actors, stays as local value, and makes the next R100 easier to use" [MS].\[107\] Ridge to River to Table proposes a yearly report on this site: of every R100 committed, how much reached the valley, how much reached local people and businesses, and what remains [MS].\[108\] Each architecture would report this way:

- **(a)** One report, split by window. A funder to one window sees the whole fund's figures.
- **(b)** One report per vehicle, rolled up by the ward trust. The capital campaign reports buildings and local contracts. The endowment reports bursaries paid and wages. The landscape fund reports restoration wages and hectares verified.
- **(c)** As (b), with a separate line for interest and capital repaid to investors, which leaves the valley.

### C1. The Bevryde Grond Trust no-encumbrance test for architecture (c)

The wording below comes from the signed deed: DEED OF TRUST in respect of DIE BEVRYDE GROND TRUST, formerly the Leerkrans Trust, IT No. 3509/95, prepared by Cullinan & Associates Inc. (copy held by the Forum) [MS].\[1\] The copy bears the stamp of the Master of the Western Cape High Court, dated 15 November 2012 [MS].\[112\]

- **Clause 17.1:** "the trustees shall not have the power to transfer ownership of any land owned by the Trust or to encumber, mortgage, pledge, grant any option, lien, restriction, right to acquire, right of pre-emption, third party right or interest or security interest of any kind, over such land." [MS]\[1\]
- **Clause 17.2:** transfer is allowed only "if the trustees are unanimous that there are adequate safeguards in place to ensure that the land will continue to be protected, conserved and used in manner that is consistent with the vision and objects of the Trust." [MS]\[1\]
- **Clause 12.3.7(ii):** borrowing is "limited to an amount sufficient to cover the Trust's administrative costs." [MS]\[1\]
- **Annex B, B1.9 and B1.10:** borrowing "without security or upon such security over movable property"; pledging is limited to "movable or incorporeal property". [MS]\[1\]
- **Annex B, B1.6:** leasing is "subject to clause 17". [MS]\[1\]
- **Clause 23.1:** clauses 5 (vision), 6 (objects) and 17 "shall not be capable of amendment". [MS]\[1\]

| Option | What the deed says | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Trust borrows, secured on lease income | 12.3.7(ii) caps borrowing at administrative costs; B1.9 allows security only over movables | Not available to the trust |
| 2. Separate building company borrows; trust grants it a short, unregistered lease; lender takes a cession of the boarding operator's rent | B1.6 allows leasing only "subject to clause 17"; 17.1 bars any "third party right or interest ... of any kind" over land | Uncertain. A personal lease right may or may not fall within 17.1. Needs a legal opinion |
| 3. Long notarial lease (registered) or surface rights, buildings in a separate vehicle, lender takes a bond over the lease | A registered real right and a bond over it are third-party rights and security interests over land | Appears barred |
| 4. Boarding buildings stand on land the Bevryde Grond Trust does not hold (a ward trust, the Farm 39 receiving body, or a municipal lease on Erf 595) | No conflict | Compatible |
| 5. Investors provide unsecured, fee-linked finance to the operator | No security over land | Compatible; dearer capital |

After that date the Forum will present it to international partners, starting with the Moravian bodies that founded the mission in 1738." This holds only once the feasibility study exists and the Moravian partners have seen it.

- **Deadline.** The South African window opens when the 2027 feasibility study is published. It closes 18 months later, on a fixed date.
- **Evidence that this framing moves SA donors.** None found [TBV]. Wupperthal shows a Rupert foundation acting quickly on Moravian heritage [IC].\[2\] It says nothing about how SA donors respond to international pressure.
- **Backfire risk (Inference).** Donors may read the deadline as a threat. Present it as a timetable.
- **What international money would change.** Control, through board seats; reporting standards; and possibly the name.
- **What the ward trust must protect.**
  - majority control by valley residents;
  - the Afrikaans name;
  - the boarding school's limit of one seat;
  - the rule against encumbering land.

### The five direct answers

**1. TRANCRAA and Farm 39.**
- **What kind of law it is.** TRANCRAA is a land reform law. In 2014 the Land Claims Court held that it does not deal with restitution [IC].\[17\]
- **Where the process stands.**
  - The transitional period for Farm 39 began on 8 July 2009 and ended in January 2011 without a transfer, so the land stayed with the Minister [IC].\[17\]
  - A Komitee office-bearer told GroundUp in 2024 that the community had set up a CPA and was waiting for the Department to finalise it [MS].\[105\] No record of a CPA registration was found [TBV].
  - The Act allows the Remainder to go to the municipality, a CPA, or another body the Minister approves [IC].\[17\]
- **What the fund can do.**
  - pay for surveys, legal work, registering the receiving body, and the community process;
  - hold pledges in escrow, released when transfer is registered;
  - take long leases from the receiving body once it exists.
- **What it cannot do.** Choose the receiving body. The Komitee decides first on anything on Farm 39 land, and the national Minister decides the transfer.
- **The Bevryde Grond Trust idea.** It is one option for the Komitee to consider, accept or decline.
- **Other TRANCRAA areas.** Haarlem, Mamre and most of Ebenhaeser were transferred in 2020/21; Enon/Bersheba went to the Witrivier CPA; title deeds were handed to Namakwa bodies in August 2025 [IC].\[105\] Every transfer found came more than twenty years after the Act [IC].\[105\] In October 2024 Parliament was told that 603,000 of about 1.4 million hectares had been transferred, and that "disputes within communities about the land to be transferred" were among the main obstacles [IC].\[47\]\[105\] How these areas have fared since transfer was not found [TBV].

**2. Erf 595.**
- **What the Council would need to decide.**
  - who holds title to Erf 595 and its subdivisions, Erf 1723 and Erf 1724 (open question 1);
  - a lease or disposal of municipal land under the MFMA;
  - rezoning;
  - how the campus relates to the 6- to 7-hectare housing site.
- **What the campus could give in return.**
  - rates and service income;
  - a trained workforce for the housing project;
  - restoration of the remaining land.
- **Scale.** Erf 595 measures 1,404.89 hectares [IC].\[105\] A campus needs about 9 to 11 hectares plus reserve [MS].\[111\]
- **The nature reserve.** The municipality's SDF proposes studying an extension of the north-south environmental corridors between Farm 39 and Greyton "for conservation purposes" [IC].\[48\] The Greyton Conservation Society says CapeNature's stewardship panel has approved a proposed extension of the reserve [MS].\[123\] Inference: a campus focused on restoration could manage part of an extended reserve.
- **Gap.** No statement by the Greyton Ratepayers and Residents Organisation on Erf 595 or the reserve was found, so its view is not recorded here.

**3. Municipal finance.**
- **What the FRP says.** "All revenue, expenditure and budget decisions must be taken within the framework of, and subject to the limitations of, the recovery plan" [IC]. The plan names weak credit control in Eskom-supplied areas and non-revenue water of 43% [IC].\[45\]
- **What the SDF says.** The municipality may not invest in infrastructure on land it does not own [IC].\[48\]
- **Effect of the campus.**
  - A campus on Farm 39 adds service demand the municipality cannot fund until the land question is settled.
  - A campus that pays for its own bulk connections and pays rates adds revenue.
  - It does not change the recovery plan itself.
