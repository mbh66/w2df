Wild Restoration is a non-profit based in Greyton, registered as Wild Restoration NPC [MS]. Michelle de Bruyn and Rupert Barnard co-founded it in 2022 [MS]. It clears invasive alien plants, restores and monitors biodiversity, and helps groups across the Cape Floristic Region work together [MS]. Its clearing work is in the Overberg [MS]. It is a registered Public Benefit Organisation (PBO 930075885), so donations from South African taxpayers are tax deductible [MS]. The United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification lists it as an accredited civil society organisation [IC].

Its work in and around the valley [MS]

- **The Riviersonderend mountains.** The range covers more than 28,000 hectares, including the Greyton and Riviersonderend nature reserves. It is an important water catchment and a habitat for leopard. Pines have invaded the higher slopes. The current focus is the high-altitude wetlands of the Greyton Nature Reserve and nearby [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]] land. Crews reach these areas on foot, by 4x4, and by helicopter.
- **The Greyton Sandpit.** A 230-hectare site near Greyton that links the mountains to the Riviersonderend River. Local people use it for grazing, woodcutting, and harvesting, and for cycling, running, and swimming. Wild Restoration has been clearing it since 2023 with an experienced local team, working with the [[economic/farmers-land-water/greyton-conservation-society|Greyton Conservation Society]], and clears along the river with the [[economic/farmers-land-water/zonderend-water-users-association|Zonderend Water Users Association]].
- **Private land.** Helping landowners between the Riviersonderend mountains and the Kleinriviersberg to plan and oversee clearing, firebreaks, and fire management, and to assess and protect biodiversity on their land.

**Helihacks.** Wild Restoration runs clearing trips it calls helihacks, where a helicopter carries crews and volunteers to the most inaccessible parts of the mountains [MS]. More than 40 people took part in the December 2023 helihack [MS].

**The EOCA project.** In late 2023 the [[organisations/european-outdoor-conservation-association|European Outdoor Conservation Association]] chose Wild Restoration's project "Be wild and rewild the mountains in the Cape Floral Kingdom" in a public vote, and gave it a grant [IC]. The Hermanus Times reported that the grant runs for two years [MS]. The amount was not published [IC]. The project aims to clear 280 hectares of invasive pines in the Greyton Nature Reserve [MS]. It also pays for training, and for about four community members to join each hack as part-time workers [MS]. In October 2025, as part of this project, 19 people spent three days on the upper slopes of the Uitkyk and felled more than 10,000 pines across 21 hectares [MS]. Wild Restoration says that this clearing will save more than nine million litres of water a year [MS].

**Partners.** Wild Restoration names these partners on its website, each with the work they do together [MS]:

- The [[organisations/south-african-national-biodiversity-institute|South African National Biodiversity Institute]]: aligning clearing with biodiversity priorities, and, through its CREW programme, identifying rare and endangered plants in clearing areas.
- The [[organisations/european-outdoor-conservation-association|European Outdoor Conservation Association]]: the "Be wild and rewild" project.
- The [[organisations/overberg-renosterveld-conservation-trust|Overberg Renosterveld Conservation Trust]]: clearing and protecting biodiversity in renosterveld.
- [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]]: clearing in the Riviersonderend mountains.
- [[organisations/natural-state|Natural State]]: trials of sound monitoring.
- The [[organisations/table-mountain-fund|Table Mountain Fund]]: developing the Invasive Clearing Action Network.
- The [[economic/farmers-land-water/greyton-conservation-society|Greyton Conservation Society]]: clearing and restoration around Greyton.
- [[cultural/education/greyton-house-school|Greyton House School]]: environmental education and time in nature for children.
- The [[economic/farmers-land-water/zonderend-water-users-association|Zonderend Water Users Association]]: clearing along the river in the Greyton Sandpit.

## Needs
- Volunteers to clear invasive plants, from pulling out seedlings for half an hour to cutting trees on the mountain peaks [MS]
- Volunteers with filming, photography, social media, event management, or marketing skills [MS]
- Donations to pay the local clearing team, and ongoing funders or sponsors [MS]

## Offers
- Work for a clearing team drawn from the local community [MS]
- Regular volunteer clearing days, run with the Greyton Conservation Society. More than 150 volunteers have joined [MS].
- Support for private landowners with clearing, GPS-tracked progress maps, and fire management plans [MS]
- The Invasive Clearing Action Network, open to local and volunteer clearing groups across the Cape Floristic Region, for sharing methods, tools, and support. More than 100 groups take part [MS].
- Biodiversity monitoring with trail cameras, sound recordings, and iNaturalist observations, which have recorded leopard, genet, bushpig, and klipspringer at its clearing sites [MS]
