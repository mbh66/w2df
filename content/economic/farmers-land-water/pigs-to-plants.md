Pigs to Plants is a programme of [[greyton-farm-animal-sanctuary|Greyton Farm Animal Sanctuary]] [MS]. It offers people who keep pigs in their backyards the chance to change to growing vegetables. A farmer either hands their pigs over to the sanctuary or closes their pig business slowly. In return, the programme gives money, materials, and staff to turn the space into a market garden [MS].

The sanctuary says backyard piggeries often keep animals in poor conditions, pollute water and soil, and cause conflict with neighbours [MS]. The programme aims to replace them with gardens that grow fresh fruit and vegetables where people live [MS].

A team of gardeners recruited locally works on five gardens in the Greyton and Genadendal valley, with more planned in the valley and in other municipalities [MS]. Produce from the gardens is sold under the brand The Graceful Garden, as vegetable boxes, salad packs, preserves, spices, and sauces, to earn a better price [MS]. The programme aims to pay for itself within five years [MS].

The Olsen Animal Trust and Humane Society International fund the programme. The University of the Western Cape's Department of Sociology will help measure its results [MS].

## Needs
- Backyard pig farmers in the valley who want to change to growing vegetables [MS]
- Buyers for The Graceful Garden's produce [MS]

## Offers
- Money, materials, and staff to turn a piggery into a market garden [MS]
- Jobs for local gardeners [MS]
- Fresh vegetables grown in the community [MS]
- Lessons in schools on why growing vegetables is kinder to health, animals, and the environment [MS]
