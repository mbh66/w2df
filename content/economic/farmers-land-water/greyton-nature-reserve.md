The Greyton Nature Reserve is a municipal nature reserve on the steep southern slopes of the Riviersonderend mountains, directly above [[villages/greyton|Greyton]] [IC]. It was proclaimed on 13 May 1977 on what Greyton's histories call the village's "mountain commonage" [IC], and was established by Professor [[history/people/brian-rycroft|Brian Rycroft]], then Director of Kirstenbosch [IC]. It covers about 2,200 hectares, up to the peaks of Uitkyk, Wa en Osse and Perdekop [MS]. The [[economic/farmers-land-water/greyton-conservation-society|Greyton Conservation Society]] supports Theewaterskloof Municipality in managing it [MS].

## The land and who looks after it

- **A local authority reserve.** A Western Cape Government list of conservation areas in the Overberg, published in 2005, gives the Greyton Nature Reserve as a local authority nature reserve of 2,069 hectares [IC]. The Greyton Conservation Society gives about 2,200 hectares [MS]. The difference has not been explained [TBV].
- **The law behind it.** Which law the reserve was proclaimed under in 1977 is not yet known [TBV].
- **The commonage.** Greyton's histories say [[history/people/herbert-vigne|Herbert Vigne]] left the land around the village to its plot owners as a commonage [TBV]. Which properties the reserve stands on is still open. See [[villages/erf-595-greyton|Erf 595, Greyton]] and [[villages/farm-38-boschmans-kloof|Farm 38, Boschmans Kloof]].
- **Day-to-day care.** The Greyton Conservation Society runs the Greyton Nature Programme in the reserve, with a conservation manager and a team of rangers [MS]. The rangers maintain the trails and signs, greet visitors at the gate, and work to help people and baboons live side by side [MS]. The [[organisations/sophia-foundation|Sophia Foundation]] matches community donations to the programme Rand for Rand, up to R500,000 a year [MS].
- **The mountains beyond.** Above and east of the reserve lies [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]]'s Riviersonderend Complex, about 28,580 hectares of protected areas managed from Vrolijkheid Nature Reserve near Robertson [IC].

## Plants, animals and water

- **Fynbos.** The reserve is mountain fynbos, with proteas, including pincushions, and aloes [MS]. One outdoor guide says it holds more than 300 plant species [TBV]. The flowers are at their best in spring [TBV].
- **Animals.** Baboons live in the reserve [IC]. Klipspringer, dassies and mongooses are also reported [TBV]. Birds include the Cape sugarbird and the malachite sunbird [MS]. A number of leopards have been recorded on a camera trap in the reserve [MS].
- **Water.** The Gobos River runs through the reserve, with waterfalls and pools along its course [IC]. The reserve has high-altitude wetlands on its upper slopes [MS]. The municipality's [[rules/theewaterskloof-spatial-development-framework|Spatial Development Framework]] calls for ecological corridors along the Gobos and Bosjemanskloof rivers to be protected [IC].

## Fire and invasive plants

- **Invasive pines.** Pines have invaded the higher slopes [MS]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]]'s project funded by the [[organisations/european-outdoor-conservation-association|European Outdoor Conservation Association]] aims to clear 280 hectares of invasive pines in the reserve [MS]. In October 2025, 19 people spent three days on the upper slopes of the Uitkyk and felled more than 10,000 pines across 21 hectares [MS].
- **The monthly hack.** Residents and visitors clear invasive plants for two hours on the first Sunday of each month [MS].
- **Fire.** In December 2019 about 5,950 hectares burned on the mountain slopes above Greyton [IC]. How much of the reserve burned is not recorded on this site [TBV]. The [[rules/national-veld-and-forest-fire-act|National Veld and Forest Fire Act]] sets the rules for fire on this land.

## The proposed extension

The Greyton Conservation Society says CapeNature's stewardship panel has approved a proposed extension of the reserve, and that further steps are needed before it is final [MS]. The society's map adds Knietjieshoogte, Zigzag and Gifkloof, the Sandpit, the Gobos River and Loerkop around the village [MS]. It says recreation, mountain biking, dog walking and low-intensity grazing would be allowed in the extension [MS]. The Spatial Development Framework says the reserve is to be expanded through the formal statutory process [IC].

## Offers
- **Walking and running trails.** Short and long trails start from the reserve gate in Greyton [MS]. Outdoor guides list the Platkloof trail (about 2.4 km, circular) and the Hardepeerkloof trail (about 5.6 km, circular), both with waterfalls and fynbos [TBV]. Short walks need no permit [IC].
- **The Boesmanskloof trail.** A trail of about 14 km over the mountains between Greyton and Die Galg, about 14 km south of McGregor [IC]. Many hikers walk it both ways over two days, about 28 km in all [IC]. It is the only trail in the area that needs a permit [MS]. CapeNature manages the trail, allows up to 50 hikers a day, and does not allow dogs, fires or camping [IC]. Oakes Falls, a series of waterfalls and pools, lies along the way [IC].
- **Bird and nature walks.** [[economic/tourism/adventure-hub-greyton|Adventure Hub Greyton]] offers walks in the reserve led by a local guide [MS].
- **A place to learn.** The Greyton Nature Programme includes environmental education and biodiversity monitoring [MS].

## How to get involved
Visitors enter on foot from the gate at the northern end of Park Street in Greyton [IC]. Dogs and mountain bikes are not allowed in the reserve [IC]. Trail maps are available from Adventure Hub Greyton and Pure Café, and permits for the Boesmanskloof trail from Greyton Tourism [MS]. Streams can rise quickly in winter, there is little water or shade on the higher slopes in summer, and baboons should be ignored and never fed [MS].

To join a hack or donate, see the [Greyton Conservation Society's website](https://greytonconservationsociety.com/nature-programme/). For anything else, contact the moderators via [[how-to-submit|How to Submit]].
