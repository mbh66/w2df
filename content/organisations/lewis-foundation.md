The Lewis Foundation is a South African trust that funds conservation and animal welfare [MS]. Tony Lewis and his wife Lisette set it up in 1994 [MS]. Today it works on three things: training people for jobs in conservation, nature-based solutions, and wildlife welfare [MS]. In April 2026 [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]] named it as one of five outcome funders of the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond [MS]. The bond pays for clearing invasive plants in the catchments that feed Cape Town's dams [IC]. The valley lies downstream of the largest of those dams, Theewaterskloof [IC].

## What it does

The foundation's founders died within nine months of each other in 1995 [MS]. Its trustees have run it since [MS].

- **1995 to 2007.** The foundation gave large grants to conservation organisations, among them [[organisations/wwf-south-africa|WWF South Africa]], the [[organisations/endangered-wildlife-trust|Endangered Wildlife Trust]], and BirdLife South Africa [MS].
- **Training people for conservation work.** After a review in 2008, the trustees chose to focus on building skills in the conservation sector [MS]. The foundation helped set up a national plan for this, the Biodiversity Human Capital Development Strategy, and programmes such as GreenMatter and Fundisa for Change [MS]. It leads GreenMatter together with the [[organisations/south-african-national-biodiversity-institute|South African National Biodiversity Institute]] [MS]. GreenMatter funds fellowships for postgraduate students [MS].
- **Nature-based solutions.** Since 2023 the foundation has grouped part of its work under this name [MS]. Its projects include work on catchment and landscape conservation, birds and renewable energy, and climate hubs with WWF and the National Business Initiative [MS]. The foundation says it is developing an interest in green investments [MS].
- **Wildlife welfare.** It funds the Endangered Wildlife Trust's Wildlife and the Law project, and paid for a review of wildlife welfare laws [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The bond.** [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued the R2.5 billion Cape Water Performance-Based Bond in April 2026, arranged by Rand Merchant Bank [IC]. Rand Merchant Bank names the Lewis Foundation among the outcome funders, the donors who pay the investors' bonus only when the clearing is checked and confirmed [MS]. The other outcome funders are the FirstRand Foundation, the Development Bank of Southern Africa, [[organisations/remgro|Remgro]], and the [[organisations/rupert-nature-foundation|Rupert Nature Foundation]] [MS]. The FirstRand Foundation and the Development Bank of Southern Africa have each committed R50 million [IC]. The Lewis Foundation's amount has not been made public. [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy South Africa]] does the clearing through the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]], and [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results [MS].

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]] [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known.
