Conservation Alpha is a specialist firm that works on how conservation is valued, managed, and financed [MS]. In the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, listed in April 2026, it is the independent design and technical agent and oversees the checking of results [MS]. Part of what the bond's investors earn depends on those checked results [IC]. The clearing the bond pays for is in the catchments of the Western Cape Water Supply System, and the valley lies downstream of that system's largest dam [IC].

## What it does

Conservation Alpha says it combines business management practice with conservation science [MS]. It supports its clients in four areas: strategic frameworks, impact measurement and verification, programme and governance design, and large-scale conservation financing [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The bond.** [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued the R2.5 billion bond, and it was listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on 17 April 2026 [IC]. Part of investors' returns is linked to independently verified restoration [IC]. Conservation Alpha provides that verification oversight, so that the environmental results are measured [MS]. Its Director of Science has said that because returns depend on performance, independent systems are needed to verify the results [MS].

**The catchments.** [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy South Africa]] does the clearing through the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]], in remote priority catchments of the Western Cape Water Supply System [MS]. The public releases about the bond do not name those catchments.

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]] [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known. Whether any clearing in those mountains is part of what Conservation Alpha checks is also not known.
