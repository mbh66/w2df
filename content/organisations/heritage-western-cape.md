Heritage Western Cape is the provincial heritage resources authority, and administers most of the [[rules/national-heritage-resources-act|National Heritage Resources Act]] in the Western Cape [IC]. Altering or demolishing any part of a structure older than 60 years needs its permit before work starts, and larger developments must notify it first [IC].

## What it does

Heritage Western Cape issues permits under sections 34 to 36 of the Act and receives notices of larger developments under section 38 [IC]. It publishes its application forms and guidelines on its website [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

- **Old buildings.** Much of Genadendal and old Greyton is older than 60 years, so work on those buildings involves Heritage Western Cape. The [[rules/national-heritage-resources-act|National Heritage Resources Act]] page sets out what that means in each village.
- **The Genadendal heritage precinct.** The [[concepts/tourism-growth-fund-application|Valley of Grace Heritage Trail]] lists its approval for panels inside the precinct [MS], and [[concepts/overberg-leopard-corridor|The Overberg Leopard Corridor]] names it for any work there.
- **Greyton.** The Stanford Conservation Trust says Heritage Western Cape regards Stanford as the third best preserved village in the Western Cape, after Montagu and "certain areas of Greyton" [MS].
