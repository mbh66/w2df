Kwaxala describes itself as an Indigenous nature regeneration network [MS]. It was founded by the Kwiakah First Nation in British Columbia, Canada, where it turned a licence to log a forest into a right to regenerate it [MS]. In September 2026 Kwaxala's Director of Vision and Leadership told the Forum's convenors that Kwaxala wants to work with the valley [MS].

## What it does

Kwaxala's investors buy Living Forest Shares, which it describes as perpetual units held in exchange for a share of what the forest produces [MS]. Income comes from carbon credits and other services the forest provides [MS]. Kwaxala says it is majority owned by its Indigenous partners, and that more than 90% of the revenue from each forest stays in the local community [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

- **The offer.** The aim discussed would be to take existing water rights and turn them into rights to regenerate the river's flow, in the way Kwaxala turned logging rights into rights to regenerate forests [MS]. Nobody has yet worked out how its model would fit South African water law.
- **The BioHub.** The Valley of Grace BioHub already names Kwaxala as a model to learn from [MS].
