The International Finance Corporation (IFC) is the part of the World Bank Group that invests in private companies. It describes itself as owned by 186 member countries [MS]. IFC was the lead investor in the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, subscribing for about R1.6 billion of the R2.5 billion issue [IC]. It says it invested to anchor "the first outcome-based bond issued by a commercial bank globally" [MS].

## What it does

IFC says its mission is "promoting development by providing debt and equity to the private sector" in more than 100 countries [MS]. Its biodiversity work includes a Biodiversity Finance Reference Guide and Blue Finance Guidelines [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**What IFC put in.** IFC's own disclosure, project 52393, describes "an up to ZAR1.6 billion IFC anchor investment in a senior unsecured outcome-based bond", approved on 20 March 2026 and invested on 31 March [MS]. The joint release of the bond's investors says IFC "subscribed for approximately R1.6 billion" [IC]. That is about two thirds of the issue.

**Why IFC says it invested.** In the joint release, IFC's Financial Institutions Group Regional Manager for Southern Africa said IFC was "proud to be the lead investor" in a transaction "which leverages the capital markets to enable and crowd in private sector capital toward conservation activities" [MS]. She said the bond "links investor returns to measurable environmental outcomes in South Africa's strategic water catchment areas and sets a replicable blueprint for nature finance across Africa and globally" [MS]. She also said the structure "would help address water security in South Africa", which she called "a key development challenge in the country", "and create jobs, including for women and youth" [MS]. IFC's disclosure adds that its participation should draw in other investors, and that its investment was needed to reach the target size [MS].

**What FirstRand says about IFC.** FirstRand's Group Treasurer said: "The IFC's investment was essential to achieve the target bond size, enabling the overall transaction" [MS].

**Where the money goes.** IFC's disclosure says the bond's proceeds are lent on to small agricultural businesses, with returns linked to the clearing project [MS]. The Nature Conservancy says the clearing itself is paid for with R150 million in donations and related funding over five years [MS].

**How the bond works.** [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued one R2.5 billion senior unsecured note, arranged by [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]], and listed it on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on 17 April 2026 [IC]. Noteholders earn a base return plus a success premium [MS]. The premium is paid by outcome funders, led by the FirstRand Foundation, only when independently checked clearing of invasive plants is achieved [MS]. [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results, and [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy South Africa]] does the clearing through the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] [MS]. The JSE, FSD Africa, and IFC all describe the co-anchors as buyers of the notes, separate from the outcome funders [IC].

**Other World Bank nature bonds.** The World Bank's lending arm, a separate part of the group, issued its own outcome bonds for rhino conservation in 2022 and Amazon reforestation in 2024, and priced a Spekboom Restoration Outcome Bond in South Africa on 23 April 2026 [MS]. In those, the World Bank is the issuer. In the Cape Water bond, IFC is an investor. IFC does not link the Cape Water bond to them.

## What is still open

- The bond's coupon, the size of the success premium, the clearing targets, and the maturity date, which are in the listing documents and not yet read for this page.
- What happens to noteholders if the clearing targets are missed. FSD Africa describes a base return plus a premium; Business Day and Aluwani describe investors carrying some of the risk.
- How the on-lending to small agricultural businesses that IFC describes relates to the clearing work.
