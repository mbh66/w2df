Aluwani Capital Partners is a South African asset manager that describes itself as black-owned and controlled, with R169.3 billion under management at 30 June 2026 [MS]. It invested R350 million in the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond [IC]. The JSE names it as one of three co-anchor investors, with the [[organisations/international-finance-corporation|International Finance Corporation]] and [[organisations/fsd-africa-investments|FSD Africa Investments]] [MS]. FSD Africa describes it as having "led local institutional participation" [MS].

## What it does

Aluwani was formed in November 2015 from part of Momentum Asset Management's institutional business, with R62 billion in assets [MS]. In 2022 its management agreed to buy out Momentum's remaining stake and its empowerment partners [MS]. It is an authorised financial services provider and manages money for institutional and individual clients [MS]. Its ESG policy endorses the United Nations Principles for Responsible Investment and the Code for Responsible Investing in South Africa [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**What Aluwani put in.** Aluwani invested R350 million [IC], the largest disclosed South African stake in the bond. Its own 2026 Stewardship Report confirms the investment and does not give the amount [MS].

**Why Aluwani says it invested.** Its Co-Head of Credit said: "Water is an increasingly scarce and mispriced resource. Our investment in the Cape water performance-based bond reflects a deliberate commitment to investing in water" [MS]. She said this means investing "not only in infrastructure such as pipes and dams, but in the ecosystems that sustain supply" [MS]. "By unlocking additional water yield at a fraction of the cost of traditional infrastructure, we are delivering measurable environmental and social impact, in line with our responsibility as stewards of capital" [MS].

Aluwani's 2026 Stewardship Report calls the investment "a deliberate effort to allocate capital to this underpenetrated segment", and says water is "relatively underrepresented in capital markets" [MS]. It says "the structure directly links financial returns to verified ecological outcomes", and that "Investors provide upfront funding and assume execution risk" [MS].

**What FirstRand says about Aluwani.** FirstRand's Group Treasurer credited the "strong support and early commitment" of Aluwani with helping complete the sale of the bond [MS].

**How the bond works.** [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued one R2.5 billion senior unsecured note, arranged by [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]], and listed it on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on 17 April 2026 [IC]. Noteholders earn a base return plus a success premium [MS]. The premium is paid by outcome funders, led by the FirstRand Foundation, only when independently checked clearing of invasive plants is achieved [MS]. [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results, and [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy South Africa]] does the clearing through the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] [MS]. The JSE, FSD Africa, and IFC all describe the co-anchors as buyers of the notes, separate from the outcome funders [IC].
