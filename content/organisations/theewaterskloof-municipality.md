Theewaterskloof Municipality is the local municipality for Ward 2 and thirteen other wards, with its seat in Caledon [IC]. Its history, from the boards that ran Genadendal to the merger of December 2000, is on [[history/institutions/municipality|The Municipality]]. This page describes the municipality today. Since November 2024 it has been under an intervention by the Western Cape Government, and since October 2025 it has worked under a binding [[rules/theewaterskloof-financial-recovery-plan|Financial Recovery Plan]] [IC]. In July 2026 National Treasury withheld part of its share of national revenue for failing to deal with irregular and wasteful spending [IC]. Its Economic Development office co-convened the first meeting of the [[civic/governance/ward-2-development-forum|Ward 2 Development Forum]] on 16 September 2026 [MS].

## Its finances

**The intervention.** The Western Cape Government announced the intervention on 11 November 2024 [IC]. It cited "prolonged financial instability, including mounting debt, severe cash flow issues, and challenges in revenue collection" [IC]. Revenue collection had fallen from over 95% in 2022/23 to 64% in August 2024 [IC]. The intervention was mandatory, because the municipality's overdue bills had passed the legal threshold [IC]. The plan that followed, what it requires, and what it means for Greyton and Genadendal are set out on [[rules/theewaterskloof-financial-recovery-plan|Theewaterskloof Financial Recovery Plan]].

**Progress.** The province's first progress report, adopted in May 2026, found the municipality still in Phase 1 [IC]. It signed a new repayment agreement with Eskom in March 2026 [IC]. The municipality's report for the quarter ending 31 March 2026 is reported to give a collection rate of 83%, below the plan's target of 85% for the year [TBV]. The DA has said the municipality's cash rose from R10.33 million to R34.33 million in one quarter [MS]. The ANC has said total municipal debt passed R400 million and that Eskom arrears grew to over R110 million [MS]. Neither figure matches the official reports.

**The equitable share withheld, July 2026.** Every municipality receives a share of national revenue, called the equitable share, in instalments through the year. On 7 July 2026 National Treasury withheld the July instalment from 69 municipalities, under section 216(2) of the [[rules/constitution|Constitution]] and section 38 of the Municipal Finance Management Act [IC]. Theewaterskloof was one of three in the Western Cape, with Laingsburg and Beaufort West [IC]. Treasury's reasons for the stoppages included unauthorised, irregular, fruitless and wasteful spending that had not been dealt with by the councils' public accounts committees, and a lack of disciplinary and recovery action [IC].

- The amount withheld from Theewaterskloof was reported as R65.8 million, the largest in the province [TBV].
- Treasury reportedly found "no evidence of adequate consequence management" at Theewaterskloof, with R397 million in such spending outstanding [TBV].
- Treasury reportedly set reductions in that backlog as the condition for releasing the money [TBV]. Later reports say the Minister of Finance released the July transfers before the end of the month, and warned that the December 2026 and March 2027 instalments could also be withheld [TBV].

## Its audits

| Year | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| 2019/20 to 2021/22 | Unqualified, with no findings [IC] |
| 2022/23 | Unqualified, with findings [IC] |
| 2023/24 | Qualified, with doubt about whether the municipality could keep operating [IC] |
| 2024/25 | Qualified [IC] |

The 2023/24 qualification arose because the municipality could not support how it had spent R41.3 million from the Municipal Disaster Response Grant, given after the floods of September 2023 [IC]. Irregular spending that year stood at R353.2 million in total [IC]. The Municipal Manager later estimated irregular spending at about R350 million for 2024/25 [TBV].

**What residents' groups have done.** In September 2024 four ratepayers' associations, including the Greyton Union of Taxpayers, went to the Caledon Magistrates' Court together to stop council meetings while a High Court review of the dismissal of councillors was pending [TBV]. They obtained an interdict, and the council met anyway [TBV]. The Caledon association's chair called it the first joint legal action by the municipality's ratepayer bodies [TBV]. The [[civic/governance/ward-2-forum-2016-2019|Ward 2 Forum]] of 2016 to 2019 published a detailed study of the Farm 39 transfer in 2017 [MS]. No published findings of fraud by the [[civic/residents-association/greyton-ratepayers-and-residents-organisation|Greyton Ratepayers & Residents Organisation]], the Greyton Union of Taxpayers, or the earlier Ward 2 Forum have been found. ## Its leadership

The municipality's political and administrative leadership has changed often since 2021 [IC]. As of October 2026:

- **Executive Mayor.** Lincoln de Bruyn (DA) [MS]. The council removed him on 1 October 2026 [IC], and is reported to have reversed the removal about two weeks later, after a legal demand from the DA [TBV].
- **Deputy Mayor.** Cynthia Clayton (GOOD), who also chairs the Economic Development portfolio [MS].
- **Speaker.** Welldon Wells (GOOD) [MS].
- **Ward 2 councillor.** Chris Cloete (DA, based in Greyton), who also chairs Financial Services [MS].
- **Municipal Manager.** Walter Hendricks was appointed on a fixed-term contract from 1 July 2025 [IC]. Through 2026 he was reported to be suspended, and an acting Municipal Manager was named [TBV]. Who holds the office today has not been confirmed from an official source.
- **Council.** 27 seats. The governing coalition of the DA, GOOD, and SRWP is reported to hold 14 [TBV].

Local government elections are due on 4 November 2026 [TBV]. The new council will elect its office-bearers, set up new ward committees, and adopt the next budget and [[rules/theewaterskloof-integrated-development-plan|Integrated Development Plan]] inside the limits of the recovery plan.

## How its work reaches the valley

- **Services.** Water, sanitation, refuse, and roads in the ward [TBV]. It supplies electricity in Greyton, while Genadendal is supplied directly by Eskom [IC].
- **Farm 39.** The municipality was given the first duty to propose how Farm 39 would be transferred under [[rules/trancraa|TRANCRAA]], and it does not provide full basic services at [[villages/madiba-park|Madiba Park]] [IC]. See [[civic/residents-association/trancraa-process-farm-39|TRANCRAA and Farm 39]] and [[civic/residents-association/madiba-park-land-and-services|Madiba Park: Land Status and Basic Services]].
- **Genadendal accounts.** The recovery plan puts Genadendal at about a fifth of the debt owed to the municipality [IC]. In 2019 the High Court allowed two Genadendal residents to defend the municipality's claims for debt on TRANCRAA grounds [IC].
- **Provincial support.** The province funded R1.65 million to upgrade the raw water storage at Voorstekraal in 2025/26, and in August 2026 the MEC for Local Government announced further support to continue after the formal intervention [IC]. See [[organisations/western-cape-government|Western Cape Government]].
