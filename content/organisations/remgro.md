Remgro Limited is an investment holding company based in Stellenbosch and listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange [MS]. The Rupert family holds the shares that carry about 43% of its votes [MS]. In April 2026 Remgro was named as one of five outcome funders of the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, which pays for clearing invasive plants in the catchments that feed Cape Town's dams [MS]. It has sat on the steering committee of the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] since the fund began in 2018 [MS]. The valley lies downstream of the largest of those dams, Theewaterskloof [IC].

## What it does

Remgro owns large shares in other companies and helps run them [MS]. Anton Rupert started the business in 1948 as a tobacco company, and its predecessor, Rembrandt, listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange in 1956 [MS]. Remgro took its present form in 2000 [MS]. Johann Rupert is its chairman, and Jannie Durand has been chief executive since 2012 [MS].

- **What it owns.** Its holdings include Mediclinic Southern Africa (fully owned since 1 July 2026), OUTsurance Group, RCL Foods and Rainbow, Heineken Beverages, Air Products, TotalEnergies South Africa, and the fibre business that runs Vumatel [MS].
- **Who controls it.** Rupert Beleggings, the Rupert family's holding company, owns all of Remgro's unlisted B shares [MS]. They are about 7% of the company's shares and carry about 43% of the votes [MS].
- **Water.** Remgro's 2025 sustainability report says "water is a strategic risk to South African businesses" [MS]. It asks the companies it owns to measure their water use and set targets to reduce it [MS]. The report describes one of those companies working with partners to clear water-hungry alien plants in important catchments, without naming where [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The bond.** [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued the R2.5 billion Cape Water Performance-Based Bond in April 2026, arranged by [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]] [IC]. Rand Merchant Bank names Remgro among the outcome funders, the donors who pay the investors' bonus only when the clearing is checked and confirmed [MS]. The other outcome funders are the FirstRand Foundation, the Development Bank of Southern Africa, the [[organisations/rupert-nature-foundation|Rupert Nature Foundation]], and the [[organisations/lewis-foundation|Lewis Foundation]] [MS]. The FirstRand Foundation and the Development Bank of Southern Africa have each committed R50 million [IC]. Remgro's amount has not been made public. Remgro did not lend money to the bond, and it is not on any list of the bond's investors [MS].

**The water fund.** Remgro was one of the companies, government bodies, and conservation groups that formed the Greater Cape Town Water Fund's steering committee in 2018 [MS]. It was still on the committee in September 2020 [MS]. In March 2021 Remgro was one of seven members of the fund's working group on long-term funding [MS]. That group looked at paying for results as one way to fund the fund's 30 years of work [MS].

**FirstRand shares.** Remgro was once a large shareholder in the group that owns FirstRand Bank. It gave those shares to its own shareholders in 2020, and sold the last of its FirstRand shares in early 2026 [IC]. No public source links those shares to the bond.

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]] [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known.
