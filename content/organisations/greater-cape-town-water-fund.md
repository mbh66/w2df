The Greater Cape Town Water Fund clears invasive plants from the mountain catchments that feed the dams supplying Cape Town [MS]. [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy]] and the [[organisations/city-of-cape-town|City of Cape Town]] set it up in 2018, after the drought, as a partnership between public bodies and private companies [MS]. The Nature Conservancy leads it [MS]. Since April 2026 the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond has paid for its next five years of work [MS]. The valley lies downstream of Theewaterskloof Dam, the largest dam in the system the fund works for [IC].

## What it does

A water fund pools money from the people and businesses that use water, and spends it on the catchments the water comes from [MS]. This fund was Africa's second, after the Upper Tana-Nairobi Water Fund in Kenya [MS].

- **The work.** Clearing pines, Australian acacias, and other invasive plants from seven priority mountain sub-catchments, then following up so that they do not grow back [MS]. It also restores wetlands upstream of dams and natural vegetation on the Atlantis Aquifer [MS].
- **The targets.** 54,300 hectares restored, of which about 41,000 had been cleared by April 2026 [MS]. Over 30 years the fund aims to return 100 billion litres of water a year, which it says is a third of Cape Town's annual supply [MS].
- **The steering committee** in 2020: Coca-Cola Peninsula Beverages, Remgro, Nedbank, PepsiCo, the national departments of Environmental Affairs and of Water and Sanitation, the [[organisations/south-african-national-biodiversity-institute|South African National Biodiversity Institute]], the Western Cape Government, CapeNature, the City of Cape Town, and [[organisations/wwf-south-africa|WWF South Africa]] [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The bond.** [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued the R2.5 billion Cape Water Performance-Based Bond in April 2026, arranged by [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]] [IC]. The Nature Conservancy receives R150 million over five years from it for clearing through the fund [MS]. [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results, and part of what investors earn depends on them [MS].

**CapeNature.** CapeNature sits on the fund's steering committee [MS]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]] says its current focus is the high-altitude wetlands of the Greyton Nature Reserve and nearby CapeNature land [MS]. Wild Restoration lists CapeNature as a partner [MS].

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with CapeNature [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known.
