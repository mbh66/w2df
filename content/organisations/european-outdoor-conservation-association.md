The European Outdoor Conservation Association (EOCA) is a charity that funds conservation projects around the world with money from companies in the outdoor industry [MS]. The European Outdoor Group set it up in 2006, and it is registered as a not-for-profit association in the canton of Zug, Switzerland [MS]. In late 2023 it gave [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]] a grant to clear invasive pines in the Greyton Nature Reserve [IC].

## What it does

- **Who pays.** Nearly 140 member companies from the outdoor sector, including makers, suppliers, service providers, and shops [MS].
- **What it funds.** Conservation, protection, and regeneration projects run by non-profit organisations [MS]. It says it has funded 214 projects in 65 countries, with nearly €6 million [MS].
- **How projects are chosen.** It takes applications each March. Shortlisted projects go to a public online vote, and member companies then vote to fund more [MS]. In the autumn 2023 round, each project could ask for up to €60,000, and four projects shared €180,000 [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

Wild Restoration's project "Be wild and rewild the mountains in the Cape Floral Kingdom" was chosen in the public vote in the autumn 2023 round [IC]. It was entered in the Wild Places category [MS]. The Hermanus Times reported in December 2023 that the grant runs for two years [MS]. EOCA says the project will clear 280 hectares of invasive pines from the mountains of the Greyton Nature Reserve, and will include education, training, and awareness work [MS]. In October 2025, as part of this project, a team of 19 felled more than 10,000 pines across 21 hectares on the upper slopes of the Uitkyk [MS].
