The Minister of Land Reform and Rural Development is the member of the national Cabinet responsible for land reform [IC]. Under the [[rules/trancraa|Transformation of Certain Rural Areas Act]] (TRANCRAA), the Minister holds the Remainder of [[villages/farm-39|Farm 39]] in trust for its residents and makes the final decision on which body will receive it [IC]. This is a national office. It is separate from the [[organisations/western-cape-department-of-agriculture|Western Cape Department of Agriculture]], which is a provincial department, and from the national Minister of Agriculture [IC]. Since July 2024 the Minister has been Mzwanele Nyhontso [IC]. The Minister works through the [[organisations/department-of-land-reform-and-rural-development|Department of Land Reform and Rural Development]] [IC].

## Why this Minister, and not the Department of Agriculture

TRANCRAA was passed in 1998. It gives its powers to "the Minister for Agriculture and Land Affairs", the name of the portfolio at the time [IC]. Since then the portfolio has been renamed several times, and the powers have stayed with the Minister responsible for land reform [IC].

| Years | Name of the portfolio |
| --- | --- |
| 1996 to 2009 | Agriculture and Land Affairs [IC] |
| 2009 to 2019 | Rural Development and Land Reform [IC] |
| 2019 to 2024 | Agriculture, Land Reform and Rural Development [IC] |
| 2024 to now | Land Reform and Rural Development [IC] |

In mid-2024 land reform and agriculture were given to two separate Ministers [IC]. The department behind them was split in two, and the split was finalised on 1 April 2025 [IC]. TRANCRAA went with land reform: in the 2026/27 budget vote, Minister Nyhontso reported on land transferred from the state to communities under the Act [IC].

The [[organisations/western-cape-department-of-agriculture|Western Cape Department of Agriculture]] is a provincial department. TRANCRAA does not name it [IC]. The Act does let the Minister delegate powers in writing to the Premier of a province, such as the [[organisations/premier-of-the-western-cape|Premier of the Western Cape]], who may pass them on to a provincial member of the Executive Council or official [IC]. Whether this has been done for Farm 39 is not known [TBV].

## What the office does on Farm 39

- **Holds the land.** Trust land that has not been transferred when the transitional period ends "vests in the Minister", who may keep holding it in trust and transfer it later under the Act's principles [IC]. The transitional period for Farm 39 ended in January 2011 without a transfer, so the land stayed with the Minister [IC].
- **Decides who receives it.** The Remainder may go to the municipality, a [[rules/communal-property-associations-act|Communal Property Association]], or another body the Minister approves [IC]. The Minister may transfer it only once satisfied that the receiving body's rules balance the rights of residents, members, and users [IC].
- **Recognises the residents' committee.** The Act defines an elected committee as one "which has satisfied the Minister that it represents the majority" of residents [IC].
- **In court.** In 2008 the Genadendal Transformation Committee, the Minister of the day, and the municipality signed an Agreement of Settlement that the High Court made an order [IC]. In 2014 the Committee asked the Land Claims Court to find that the municipality and two Ministers had breached that order [IC].
- **Letters from the municipality.** In September 2016 the Municipal Manager wrote to the department asking the Minister to decide on a receiving body [IC]. In September 2022 the DA's Western Cape spokesperson on human settlements said the municipality had written twice to the then Minister, Thoko Didiza, about interim basic services and land use at [[villages/madiba-park|Madiba Park]], and had received no reply [MS].
