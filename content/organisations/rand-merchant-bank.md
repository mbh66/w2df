Rand Merchant Bank (RMB) is the corporate and investment banking division of [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] [IC]. It arranged, structured, and distributed the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, which it launched on 1 April 2026 [MS]. The bond pays for clearing invasive plants in catchments that feed Cape Town's dams [IC]. The valley lies downstream of the largest of those dams, Theewaterskloof [IC].

## What it does

RMB is the corporate and investment banking arm of FirstRand Bank [IC]. RMB's own website describes it as a division of FirstRand Bank Limited [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The bond.** RMB announced the bond on 1 April 2026 as Africa's first nature-linked outcomes-based bond [MS]. FirstRand Bank issued it, and it was listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on 17 April 2026 [IC]. Part of what investors earn is linked to independently verified restoration [IC]. An RMB spokesperson called it "a R2.5 billion market signal that natural capital has entered mainstream finance" [MS].

**A series.** RMB describes the bond as the first in a series designed for South Africa's 22 strategic water source areas [MS]. These areas cover about a tenth of South Africa's land and supply about 60 percent of its water [MS].

**Where the money goes.** [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy South Africa]] clears invasive plants through the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]], in remote priority catchments of the Western Cape Water Supply System [MS]. [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results [MS].

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]] [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known.
