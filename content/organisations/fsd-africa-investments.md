FSD Africa Investments (FSDAi) is the investment arm of FSD Africa, a development agency set up in 2012 by the UK government and based in Nairobi [MS]. FSDAi committed R234 million to the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond and, with the [[organisations/international-finance-corporation|International Finance Corporation]], anchored it [IC]. The JSE says this was FSDAi's first investment in South African listed debt [MS].

## What it does

FSD Africa calls itself "a specialist development agency working to strengthen African financial markets" [MS]. FSDAi was set up in 2017 as its investment arm, and the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office has committed £309 million in capital to it [MS]. FSDAi says: "We invest at the frontier of finance where commercial capital is hesitant to go" [MS]. FSD Africa says it has supported 23 sustainable bonds in Africa, mostly with technical help to issuers [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**What FSDAi put in.** FSDAi committed R234 million, about a tenth of the issue [IC]. The JSE says it was FSDAi's "first South African listed debt capital markets investment" [MS]. FSD Africa calls the investment "catalytic", and says FSDAi's part helped "validate the structure and attract other institutional investors" [MS].

**Why FSDAi says it invested.** FSD Africa gives three reasons for anchoring the bond: to show the structure works, to draw South African asset managers and pension funds in behind it, and to create a template for future nature-linked bonds [MS]. FSDAi's Chief Investment Officer said: "Nature has long been treated as a cost; this bond demonstrates it can be structured as an asset" [MS]. She said FSDAi stands with partners "united by the conviction that Africa's markets are ready to price nature differently", and that the bond "opens the door to an entirely new asset class in Africa's capital markets" [MS].

**The UK government's view.** The British High Commissioner to South Africa, speaking for FSDAi's funder, said: "Nature is critical infrastructure, and linking investment returns to verified environmental improvements shows how finance can drive real resilience" [MS].

**How the bond works.** [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued one R2.5 billion senior unsecured note, arranged by [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]], and listed it on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on 17 April 2026 [IC]. Noteholders earn a base return plus a success premium [MS]. The premium is paid by outcome funders, led by the FirstRand Foundation, only when independently checked clearing of invasive plants is achieved [MS]. [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results, and [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy South Africa]] does the clearing through the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] [MS]. The JSE, FSD Africa, and IFC all describe the co-anchors as buyers of the notes, separate from the outcome funders [IC].

**Earlier work.** Before this bond, FSDAi's work in bond markets went through funds and facilities, such as £10 million to a Nigerian facility that shares the risk of local infrastructure bonds [MS]. FSD Africa also runs a pilot of nature-related financial reporting that includes FirstRand [MS]. FSD Africa does not say whether that pilot led to the investment.

## What is still open

- The bond's coupon, the size of the success premium, the clearing targets, and the maturity date, which are in the listing documents and not yet read for this page.
- What happens to noteholders if the clearing targets are missed. FSD Africa describes a base return plus a premium; Business Day and Aluwani describe investors carrying some of the risk.
