The Table Mountain Fund is a trust fund that pays for conservation across the Cape Floristic Region [MS]. It was registered in 1998 and is an associated trust of [[organisations/wwf-south-africa|WWF South Africa]] [MS]. It is based at the Centre for Biodiversity Conservation at Kirstenbosch, in Cape Town [MS]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]] lists it as a partner in developing the Invasive Clearing Action Network [MS].

## What it does

Sir Edmund Hillary launched the Fund [MS]. WWF South Africa raised its starting capital, and the World Bank later invested in it [MS]. It says it has put R116 million into more than 380 projects [MS].

Its grants fall under four programmes [MS]:

- **Fynbos Forever.** Healthy ecosystems, through new protected areas, land securement, stewardship, restoration, and research.
- **Pride.** Building people's appreciation of fynbos.
- **Prosperity.** Livelihoods that depend on fynbos.
- **Care.** Conservation that includes more people, with small and micro grants. A partner-run Small Grants Facility gives grants of up to R36,250.

Donations to the Fund may qualify for a Section 18A tax certificate [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

The valley lies inside the Cape Floristic Region, the area the Fund covers [IC]. Wild Restoration names the Fund among its partners, for "developing the invasive clearing action network" [MS]. That network is open to local and volunteer clearing groups across the Cape Floristic Region, and Wild Restoration says more than 100 groups take part [MS].
