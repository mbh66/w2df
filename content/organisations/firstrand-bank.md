FirstRand Bank Limited is the main South African bank of the FirstRand group, which is based in Johannesburg [IC]. Its divisions include First National Bank (FNB), [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]] (RMB), and WesBank [IC]. In April 2026 it issued the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, which pays for clearing invasive plants in catchments that feed Cape Town's dams [IC]. The valley lies downstream of the largest of those dams, Theewaterskloof [IC].

## What it does

FirstRand Limited is the holding company of FirstRand Bank, and is listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange and the Namibian Stock Exchange [IC]. FirstRand Bank works through separately branded divisions: FNB for retail and commercial banking, RMB for corporate and investment banking, and WesBank for vehicle and instalment finance [IC].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The bond.** FirstRand Bank issued the R2.5 billion Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, listed on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange on 17 April 2026 under the code FR31PB [IC]. Part of what investors earn is linked to independently verified restoration [IC]. FirstRand Bank describes its roles as issuer, project agent, and conditional funding donor [MS]. RMB arranged the bond [MS]. FirstRand's Group Treasurer said one of the key aims was to "make nature an investable asset class" [MS].

**The FirstRand Foundation.** The FirstRand Foundation is the bond's anchor outcomes-based funder, and coordinates its philanthropic partners [IC]. RMB's launch announcement gives the Foundation's anchor commitment as R50 million [MS].

**Where the money goes.** [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy South Africa]] receives R150 million over five years to clear invasive plants through the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]], in remote priority catchments of the Western Cape Water Supply System [MS]. [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results [MS].

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]] [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known.
