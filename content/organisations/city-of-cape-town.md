The City of Cape Town is the metropolitan municipality for Cape Town [IC]. It is a separate municipality from Theewaterskloof, of which Ward 2 is a part [IC]. The City draws much of its water from the Western Cape Water Supply System, whose largest dam, Theewaterskloof, lies upstream of the valley on the Riviersonderend [IC]. In 2018 the City and [[organisations/the-nature-conservancy-south-africa|The Nature Conservancy]] set up the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]] to clear invasive plants from the catchments that feed its dams [MS].

## What it does

The City provides water and other municipal services to Cape Town [IC]. Its dam-level reports list six major dams in the Western Cape Water Supply System, which hold 898,221 megalitres when full [IC]. Theewaterskloof holds 480,188 megalitres of that, a little over half [IC]. The dam is owned by the national [[organisations/department-of-water-and-sanitation|Department of Water and Sanitation]], and its water reaches Cape Town through a tunnel to the Berg River side of the mountains [IC].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The water fund.** The City and The Nature Conservancy set up the Greater Cape Town Water Fund in 2018 as a partnership between public bodies and private companies [MS]. The City sits on its steering committee [MS]. In 2021 the City committed R62 million over three years for clearing in the upper catchments of the Wemmershoek, Steenbras, and Berg River dams, matched rand for rand by The Nature Conservancy [MS]. None of these is the Theewaterskloof catchment.

**The bond.** The releases about the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond do not name the City as a party [MS]. The bond pays for clearing through the fund the City helped set up [MS].

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with [[organisations/capenature|CapeNature]] [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known.
