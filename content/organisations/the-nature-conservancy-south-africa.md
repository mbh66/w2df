The Nature Conservancy (TNC) is a global conservation non-profit that works in 86 countries and territories [MS]. Its South African programme is led by a country director based in Cape Town [MS]. After the drought of 2018, TNC helped set up the [[organisations/greater-cape-town-water-fund|Greater Cape Town Water Fund]], and it has led the fund since [MS]. It is the implementer of the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, clearing invasive plants in catchments that feed Cape Town's dams [MS]. The valley lies downstream of the largest of those dams, Theewaterskloof [IC].

## What it does

TNC says it works in 86 countries and territories, in 41 of them directly and in 45 through partners [MS]. In South Africa its best-known work is the Greater Cape Town Water Fund, which clears pines, Australian acacias, and other invasive plants from the mountains whose streams fill the region's dams [MS].

## How its work reaches the valley

**The water fund.** TNC says that clearing invasive plants in the catchments gives water at about one tenth of the cost of desalination or new groundwater [MS]. The fund aims to restore 54,300 hectares, and had cleared about 41,000 by April 2026 [MS]. In 2021 TNC matched the City of Cape Town's R62 million for clearing, rand for rand [MS].

**The bond.** Under the Cape Water Performance-Based Bond, TNC South Africa receives R150 million over five years, as donations and related funding, for clearing and catchment restoration through the fund [MS]. [[organisations/rand-merchant-bank|Rand Merchant Bank]] and TNC developed the bond together [MS]. [[organisations/firstrand-bank|FirstRand Bank]] issued it, and [[organisations/conservation-alpha|Conservation Alpha]] checks the results [MS].

**The valley.** Theewaterskloof Dam, on the Riviersonderend near Villiersdorp, is the largest dam in the Western Cape Water Supply System [IC]. The valley lies downstream of it, where the Gobos and Baviaans rivers join the Riviersonderend [IC]. [[economic/farmers-land-water/wild-restoration|Wild Restoration]], which clears invasive plants in the Riviersonderend mountains above Greyton, has told the Forum that the Greater Cape Town Water Fund supports clearing in those mountains through a partnership with CapeNature [TBV]. Which slopes that covers, and whether they drain into the dam or into the valley's own rivers, is not yet known.
